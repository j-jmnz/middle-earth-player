# Middle Earth LCG — decisions and implementer brief

This is the source of truth for the app. It records what was decided and how to build it. Read the sections your task touches before writing code.

Authority, in order:

1. The MEC109 booklet, the quest cards and the campaign cards win on a rule; the core-set Learn to Play wins on the ordinary round and on campaign play; the Rules Reference wins on definitions. A card's text is what is printed on it: the ledger in `data/printed/`, read from a pinned scan; Hall of Beorn and DragnCards text is a candidate the build checks against it. Their text is in `data/rules/` and split into rule ids in `data/rules/entries.json` (`pnpm data:query "select id, title from rules where text like '%surge%'"`). Code and tests name the rule ids they implement. When a rule changes, change the code, the test, and this file in the same commit.
2. This file, for product shape and stack. Structure is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).
3. `data/` for cards, scenarios and rules text. Where it came from is in `data/sources.json`; where a source was wrong is in `data/overrides.json`, each with a reason.

Do not reopen the product shape unless a decision below is impossible.

Performance is a product requirement. A hitch under a finger is a failed step, the same as a wrong threat number. The section Performance and feel is binding on every screen.

The app applies the rules. Keywords, phases, effects, damage, resources, threat, and progress are engine results. The player chooses among options those rules currently allow, and cannot type the results.

## Product

An offline, single-device, fan-made app for The Lord of the Rings Living Card Game, with no connection to FFG. Official campaigns are the product. The first campaign is the 2022 **Fellowship of the Ring Saga Expansion** (FFG product MEC109). Every official campaign and scenario is added to the same app in turn, and then the fan-made ALeP (A Long-extended Party) ones (user decision, 2026-11-06). The README's Roadmap lists what comes next.

One human. No accounts, no rooms, no matchmaking, no server in the play loop. True solo is one local seat. Two-handed solo is two local seats on the same phone, because staging and engagement are defined per player. That is not multiplayer. The paper log has Player 3 and Player 4 columns. This app does not. `seatCount` is 1 or 2, chosen once at campaign start.

The app opens on the campaign list, not on a blank table. Deckbuilding is a separate root screen and is available before any scenario is won. The dock under the root screens is Campaigns, Scenarios, Decks, Cards and Rules (user requests, 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-28), and in an expanded window the dock is a rail down the left edge: the campaign list, standalone play, the deck list, the card pool, and the rules to look up between turns. Settings is a pushed screen (Screens).

The booklet allows playing a scenario as a standalone adventure (`mec109.campaign-mode`: "players can still play the scenarios as standalone adventures if they choose"). The app has it: a Scenarios tab beside the campaign list deals any playable scenario of the box on its own, with no campaign mechanics (Standalone play, below; user decision 2026-10-25). The Scenarios tab lists one section for each box the app plays: the Fellowship of the Ring, the Revised Core Set (Passage Through Mirkwood, Journey Along the Anduin, Escape from Dol Guldur) The Dark of Mirkwood (The Oath, The Caves of Nibin-Dûm) Dream-chaser (its nine scenarios) and Ered Mithrin (its nine scenarios), see Mirkwood Paths and The Dark of Mirkwood, Dream-chaser and Ered Mithrin, below. A box's scenario is dealable once `src/campaign/playable.ts` lists it.

The repository is open source: the code is under the MIT License, and game content keeps its owners' terms (`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). An app store listing is a different copyright situation and is not decided here. Do not start one.

## Non-goals

Do not do any of these.

- Do not port DragnLang, Phoenix channels, GenServers, or the generic pile engine. The DragnCards plugin's data (card database, scenario load lists, framework steps, automation snippets) is a source for `data/`, fetched by `pnpm data:sync`; the app never calls it at runtime. Where a DragnCards automation snippet seems to solve a rule, encode the booklet's rule and cite its rule id.
- Do not build a rules language for other card games.
- Do not add accounts, auth, Patreon, image hosting, or a backend.
- Do not restrict the deck builder to cards that have automated effects, or to cards printed in the Fellowship box.
- Do not include playtest cards. ALeP cards and scenarios come after the official ones (Product); until then the RingsDB sync skips ALeP packs. Easy, Normal and Nightmare (user decision, 2026-10-30; Nightmare, below). Easy Mode also puts 1 resource on each hero during setup (`easy-mode.easy-mode`).
- Do not commit card scans, booklet PDFs, or `catalog.db`. `data/` holds card text, rules text and raw source snapshots on purpose, so every session has them. The card text, art and rules belong to FFG/Asmodee (`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`). The phone receives `catalog.db` inside the binary. The release build also carries every booklet PDF (`BOOKLET_SOURCES`, `scripts/build/pack-booklets.ts`): `pnpm booklets:pack` (run by `eas-build-post-install`) copies each pinned PDF from `.cache/pdf`, or fetches it from its pinned URL and checks the sha256 in `data/sources.json`, with a map that requires them into `assets/booklets/` (gitignored); Metro bundles that map in place of the empty stub `src/screens/booklets/bundled-map.ts`. `pnpm data:sync rules` and `pnpm android` pack too. The app never fetches a booklet at runtime. A build without them still lets the player add each PDF from their files.
- Do not paste the MEC109 narrative prose into screens. Encode procedures. Short original labels are fine ("Hide test", "Ring-bearer's life").
- Do not ship a WebView of any existing card-table site.
- Do not build a collection tracker, an encounter-card deck editor, or a second game.

## Decisions

| Decision | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Expo app, one package, no monorepo | One person, one binary. A second package adds a build graph with nothing to share yet. |
| Rules and campaign code cannot import React Native | The game has to be tested in Node. An Oxlint import rule (`.oxlintrc.json`) fails the build if `src/rules` or `src/campaign` imports `react-native`, `expo-*`, or anything under `src/play` or `src/screens`. The same rule fails an `src/effects` import from `src/rules`; `src/campaign`, `src/play` and `src/screens` import `src/effects` directly, and `src/rules` receives the records as an argument. |
| Campaign registry, not a Fellowship-named core | The next box is a folder plus a catalog rebuild. The core round and the deck builder do not mention Fellowship by name. |
| Full official player pool from the first install | Deckbuilding is not gated on campaign progress or on effect coverage. |
| Two SQLite files | `catalog.db` is reference data baked into the binary and replaceable. `user.db` is decks and saves and is never replaced by a catalog update. |
| The engine plays the game | Rules, keywords, phases, effects, damage, resources, threat, and progress are applied by the app. The player only chooses among options the current rules allow. |
| That engine stays inside the app | One device, one player, airplane mode. A rules API adds a host and a network hop. It does not make the math more correct. |
| Effects are records, not parsed English | A template writes a record only when it matches a whole sentence; an agent drafts the rest from a bundle. A partial parse would mistime Forced and Response. A sentence with no effect record is inactive. It is not handed to the player to resolve. |
| Campaign-dealt cards are fully automated before the scenario is playable | Quests, encounters, boons, burdens, and The One Ring have no inactive-text path. |
| Every card is automated (user decision, 2026-10-26) | Every deck-legal player card and every card a scenario deals gets effect records for all its printed lines. The Messenger of the King cards (the `(MotK)` hero versions of allies and the Messenger of the King contract) are left out of the card pool (user decision, 2026-10-26; `outOfScope` in `data/overrides.json`). Until a card's last line has a record, printed cost and combat numbers, plus implemented keywords, still work, the open line is listed in `src/effects/open-lines.ts` (the burn-down list), and the player sees it marked "Not applied". An event with no record cannot be played (`rr.triggered-ability`). |
| Every card's picture is bundled in the app | No network at runtime but the RingsDB deck import (user decisions, 2026-10-29 and 2026-11-07). The pictures are the 600 dpi scans and FFG's campaign-card PDFs, scaled to 1200 px on the long side; see Card images. |
| Feel outranks the next feature | Drag, scroll, and taps stay smooth on a phone. A correct rule that stalls the frame does not ship. |
| Identifiers are RingsDB codes (`141001`), never DragnCards UUIDs | That is the public catalog. |
| Errata wins | When RingsDB sets `has_errata`, store and display the errata text. |
| Booklet and quest card beat this file | This file paraphrases. A wrong paraphrase must not ship. |
| No card-text NLP, and no manual resolver | A wrong Forced effect is worse than leaving the sentence inactive. |

EAS Build profiles for development and e2e builds |

Install Reanimated, `react-native-worklets`, Gesture Handler, Skia, FlashList, `expo-sqlite`, `expo-image`, `expo-file-system` and `expo-haptics` with `pnpm expo install`. Worklets is the Reanimated 4 peer. Stay on the Skia and FlashList versions that command selects. Expo Go ships those binaries.

Do not add TanStack Query, Redux, a Phoenix client, or a server framework.

The rules engine is the pure TypeScript reducer in `src/rules`, called in-process by the screen. Vitest imports that same module. Maestro drives the built app and asserts the phase rail, sheets, and saves. It does not replace those unit tests, and it does not judge a drag. Those flows wait until the last phase. An earlier phase is not open because `maestro test` has not been run. Do not wrap it in HTTP, a localhost server on the phone, a second package, or a remote API. The UI already cannot apply damage or resources. Moving the reducer off the device would not add a rule the module cannot express, and it would break the airplane-mode exit check.

A server is the right shape for a second human who must not see hidden cards, or for many clients sharing one live table. This product has neither. Cross-device save sync, if it is ever wanted, copies `user.db` rows. It still does not run the round.

## Performance and feel

This applies to the table, the card pool, the deck editor, the campaign path, the briefing, the log, and the card pictures. A later scenario does not start while the current screen hitches. Do not leave smoothness for a polish pass.

The new architecture stays on. Reanimated is the only animation library. `react-native-worklets` is its peer. A drag, a card lift, and a sheet run on the UI thread. A finger moving across the table does not cross into JavaScript to reposition a card. The reducer runs when the gesture ends or a choice is committed, not on every movement sample. `legalIntents` is computed when the window changes, not per pixel. It does not build the cross product of every card and every target up front. The table computes targets for the card the player selected.

The table is one Skia canvas. It is not a React Native view per card. Skia decodes the bundled pictures. It does not call `makeImageFromView`. A Zustand subscriber reads the slice it draws. Reducer updates keep the same object references for zones that did not change. A threat change does not re-render the hand, the pool, or the log. Skia images that leave the table are released.

Lists virtualize. The pool loads card rows when that screen opens, then filters in memory. Painting every row is not. The campaign list reads campaigns and saves. It does not scan `cards`. `expo-image` decodes a list card at its row size and the inspect screen at up to the bundled size, 1200 px on the long side. The table is one Skia canvas and decodes with Skia. Nothing decodes every picture. Do not make the first paint wait on a picture.

The first screen paints from local SQLite. Startup does not count the catalog or the pictures before it draws.

A tap shows a pressed state in the same frame. Phase changes and sheets animate, and the animation does not gate the rule. If the engine has already resolved, the motion shows the result. A new gesture cancels the animation in progress. When the OS asks for reduced motion, the same transitions snap.

Tap targets are at least 44 points. The phase rail and the choice buttons sit where a thumb reaches. Card text on the table is the picture. Text in sheets is set in type large enough to read at arm's length. There is no desktop table shrunk into the phone, and no phone layout stretched across a tablet. Layouts follow size classes (the window's width in points), not devices (`docs/design-system.md`, Standard 8 and Tablets). A phone is portrait everywhere except the table (landscape only) and the card, booklet, glossary and rule screens, which follow how the phone is held so the table is not turned back; on its side it gets the wider layouts there. A tablet (a shorter side of 600 points or more) rotates every route but the table.

Profile the pool fling and the table drag on a physical phone before closing a change to the pool or the table. A hitch during either is a failed exit. The simulator does not count for that check.

`src/rules` and `src/campaign` have zero React imports. `src/play` sends player intents. It does not assign threat, damage, resources, progress, or log fields.

## Domain

### Campaign

A save is one playthrough of a campaign. The table is discarded when the scenario ends. The log is not.

```ts
type Save = {
  id: string;                    // this playthrough
  campaignId: string;            // any id data/campaigns/index.json holds, such as "fellowship-of-the-ring"
  seatCount: 1 | 2;              // chosen once, at the start of the save
  log: CampaignLog;
  activeScenario: ActiveScenario | null;
};

// The open scenario is not stored as a table. Loading rebuilds the start from the log and the decks, then replays
// the intents through the reducer (src/campaign/replay.ts).
type ActiveScenario = {
  engineVersion: number;         // ENGINE_VERSION in src/rules/engine/version.ts
  dataHash: number;              // the catalog version the intents were played against
  seed: number;
  intents: PlayerIntent[];       // every intent sent, Keep included, in order
};


type CampaignLog = {
  heroesBySeat: string[][];      // RingsDB codes, length seatCount
  fallenHeroesBySeat: string[][];
  threatPenalty: number;         // permanent, one number for every player
  campaignPool: PoolCard[];      // earned boons and burdens, attached or not
  captives: { seat: number; hero: string }[];
  completed: string[];           // scenario ids
  earned: Record<string, string[]>; // the codes each won scenario's Resolution recorded; the path shows them on a completed node
  scenarios: Record<string, { rounds: number; victoryPoints: number; score: number }>;
  score: number;                 // the sum of the scenario scores, computed, never typed
  notes: string;
  // Structured campaign facts, in place of the notes the Mirkwood campaign cards ask for. An older log reads with these defaults.
  prisoner: string | null;       // the hero code the players named the prisoner
  usedOnce: string[];            // once-per-campaign uses ("Record in the Campaign Log when used"): the code of each card used
  counters: Record<string, number>; // counters on a campaign card, by its code, as the last won scenario left them
  carryOver: PoolCard[];         // cards this campaign hands to the next one (a Resolution's CarryOver)
  carryIn: PoolCard[];           // cards a new save took in from a finished campaign; its first checklist moves them into the pool
  // Dream-chaser's XP (see Dream-chaser below). An older log reads with these defaults.
  xpGained: Record<string, number>; // the XP each won scenario's Resolution awarded, by scenario id; the unspent XP is this less the boxes marked, never stored
  marks: Record<string, number>;    // boxes marked beside each scalable and upgradable card, by the card's code (src/campaign/xp.ts)
  modes: Record<string, "hard" | "expert">; // the Hard/Expert Mode card each won scenario was played with
  modeDefault: "hard" | "expert" | null;    // the card the campaign setup chose to offer each briefing first
  // Ered Mithrin's columns (see Ered Mithrin below). An older log reads with these defaults.
  treasures: string[];           // the codes of the treasures discovered (emce.treasure)
  allyLines: ("turayn" | "melanwar" | "isolde" | "beldis")[]; // the "Unique Allies Protecting Wilderland" lines recorded (emce.boon-allies)
  beldisUses: number[];          // the boxes marked beside Beldis for each seat, 0 to 4
  encounterPool: Record<string, number>; // the encounter cards in the Campaign Pool and their copies (emce.campaign-pool-encounter)
  // The Two Towers' columns (see The Two Towers below). An older log reads with these defaults.
  threatPenaltyBySeat: number[]; // the permanent starting threat penalty each seat takes of its own, on top of threatPenalty
  marshesChoice: ("penalty" | "searchingEye" | null)[]; // what each seat chose in The Passage of the Marshes
  blackGate: Record<string, number>; // the enemies under The Black Gate by card code and quantity, shown with the notes
  scoreBase: number;             // the Campaign Total a continued save carried on from the finished Fellowship save
};

type PoolCard = {
  code: string;
  back: "player" | "encounter";  // which deck it joins (Campaign Setup Checklist steps 7 and 8)
  burdenSet: string | null;      // a burden belongs to a burden set; later cards remove burdens by set
  attachedTo: string | null;     // a hero code, for a Permanent card or one a Resolution attaches
  seat: number | null;
};
```

The paper sheet also has these labels: Heroes, Fallen Heroes, Threat Modifiers, Boons, Burdens, Scenarios Completed, Score, Notes, Campaign Total. Notes are the only free text. The Learn to Play states the score of a won scenario: the final threat of each player, plus the threat cost of each destroyed hero, plus the damage on each surviving hero, plus 10 per completed round, minus the victory points earned (`ltp.scoring`, `rr.scoring`). Lower is better. The Rules Reference words the second term as the threat penalty on defeated heroes; the Learn to Play, which the log sheet follows, says their threat cost, and this file uses that. The engine computes it on a win. The campaign total is the sum.

Campaign play follows the Learn to Play and Rules Reference campaign rules (`rr.campaign-mode`, `ltp.campaign-mode`, `rr.campaign-cards`, `rr.campaign-pool`, `ltp.campaign-pool`). Each scenario starts with the Campaign Setup Checklist (`rr.campaign-setup-checklist`, `ltp.campaign-setup-checklist`; eight steps): heroes from the log, fallen heroes replaced, +1 threat penalty per voluntary hero change unless a campaign card waives it, the penalty applied, Permanent cards attached, Setup boons and burdens put into play, player-backed pool cards shuffled into decks, and encounter-backed pool cards shuffled into the encounter deck. Each scenario's campaign card prints its Setup on the front and its Resolution on the back. Those lines are effect records like any other. A burden belongs to a burden set, not an encounter set. Later campaign cards remove burdens by burden set. A player uses the heroes on the log and may trade 1 of them for a new hero between scenarios; a fallen hero may be replaced or left out, and a hero still alive is never left out without that trade (`ltp.fellowship-of-heroes`). Cards a campaign card says the players earned join the pool even when nobody chose them (142019's leftover boons, in the first player's deck). Only a Permanent card stays recorded on a hero. Heroes are logged under the seat that owns them, so a Ring-bearer another seat controls at the end is still its owner's.

The campaign pool holds several copies of one code: "each player earns 1 copy" is one entry for each player, marked `perPlayer` and carrying that player's `seat`, and a card removed from the pool ("remove it from the Campaign Pool") removes one entry (the removing player's copy when the game says whose it was). Any other earn is one copy of its code however often it is earned and whichever seat wrote it, as in the Fellowship: The Ring Draws Them chosen in scenario 1 and earned again by Flight to the Ford is one card. An eliminated player still has a seat for "each player". The structured facts reach the engine at the start of a scenario in Campaign Mode (`StartScenario.facts`), and a won game writes them back; a standalone game has none.

The log shows the six Fellowship rows. Each later saga box is its own campaign and save with the same log type, never a second one; a new save of The Two Towers may take in a finished Fellowship save's log (The Two Towers below, user decision 2026-11-07). Fellowship's six, in order, are fixed:

1. A Shadow of the Past
2. A Knife in the Dark
3. Flight to the Ford
4. The Ring Goes South
5. Journey in the Dark
6. Breaking of the Fellowship

A lost scenario writes nothing to the log and stays current; it does write a run (History, `runs` in Persistence), which is a record beside the log and never read by the checklist. A won scenario runs its resolution, then unlocks the next id. The booklet's "players defeat the scenario" means won. Replaying a won scenario does not grant the reward a second time unless that scenario's script says so. The chosen card is added to the pool, or attached when it is Permanent. The unchosen card is not added. Leave it where the quest card prints. Do not add a sweep the card does not print.

### Mirkwood campaign cards and carry-over

The Revised Core and The Dark of Mirkwood campaign cards print Setup lines the Fellowship's do not. The Campaign Setup Checklist and the start deal them (`src/campaign/checklist.ts`, `start.ts`; verbs in `src/catalog/families/campaign.ts`), in Campaign Mode only:

- "Put Mendor into play" (`PutObjectiveAllyIntoPlay`): the objective ally enters play under the first player, with no reveal and no cost. A pool card whose own record says it is put into play at the start of each scenario (Mendor, once an earlier Resolution has carried him over) is put into play instead of being shuffled into the encounter deck: it skips checklist step 8.
- "Set each copy of X aside, out of play" (`SetAside`), "Shuffle the X burden into the encounter deck" (`ShuffleIntoEncounterDeck`).
- "Attach X to a hero of the first player's choice" (`AttachChosen`): the first player chooses any hero at the table; the card is not added to the pool.
- "Each player adds a copy of X to their hand after drawing their starting hand (including taking a mulligan)" (`AddToHandAfterMulligan`): the card is dealt once every seat has kept or taken its mulligan, so a mulligan does not shuffle it away.
- "Add X to the Campaign Pool" (`AddToPool`, a copy for each player when it says each): recorded in the log when the scenario is won. This scenario already has the card, so steps 7 and 8 do not deal it again.
- "Set each copy of Valor and Scarred aside" (`SetAsideEachPlayer`, a copy for each player), taken later by `AttachSetAside` (a set-aside copy attached to the hero a Pick named). Its Resolution verbs: `EarnAttached` (each copy of the named cards attached to a hero in play joins the pool on that hero, one entry for each copy) and `NamePrisoner` (the hero with the most damage, a tie settled by the seed, is written to the log's `prisoner` fact).

A Resolution can hand cards to the next campaign (`CarryOver`: Mendor, each player's Mendor's Support, Ranger Sense). They are written on the log's `carryOver`; a lost scenario writes nothing. A copy for each player is one entry marked `perPlayer`, with no seat: the new playthrough gives one copy to each of its own seats, whatever the finished one had. A new playthrough (any campaign) offers the carry-over of each finished playthrough of any campaign (`src/campaign/carry-over.ts`): the player takes one or starts fresh. A finished playthrough's carry-over is taken by one new playthrough (its log records `carriedFrom`): the offer is not made again until that playthrough is deleted. The cards go on the new log's `carryIn`, and the first Campaign Setup Checklist moves them into the pool (step 1) and clears `carryIn`. Within one campaign nothing is carried: part 4 of Mirkwood Paths continues the same log. The Dark of Mirkwood mini campaign starts on a blank log (`tdom.mini-campaign`), and so does any new playthrough that takes nothing in.

Loadout rules follow the campaign's `saga` flag and, in The Two Towers, the scenario's own saga start (The Two Towers below). In a saga (the Fellowship of the Ring) the Ring-bearer rules run: a Ring-bearer for the first seat, one Fellowship-sphere hero, no second Frodo, no One Ring in a deck, a fourth hero only as the Ring-bearer. Outside a saga none of them runs, there is no Ring-bearer slot, and a Fellowship-sphere hero is refused: "Heroes belonging to the Fellowship sphere can only be used when playing the scenarios in the saga expansions" (`mec109.the-fellowship-sphere`). The same hero in two seats is still refused (`rr.unique-cards`).

### Standalone play

A standalone game is one deal of one scenario, outside any campaign (`src/campaign/standalone.ts`, user decision 2026-10-25). It is not a save: it has no log, and nothing it does reaches one.

- The saga rules follow the box. The Ring-bearer, The One Ring and the Fellowship sphere are the saga's setup (`mec109.setting-up-the-game`), not the campaign's, so a Fellowship or Two Towers scenario's briefing applies the same loadout rules (`validateLoadout`: the Ring-bearer, or the ☺ hero the scenario grants, and the Two Towers title bans) and the same deck rules (one to three heroes per saved deck, `rr.deckbuilding`) as a campaign deal. A scenario of any other box is not a saga: no Ring-bearer slot, and a Fellowship-sphere hero is refused (`mec109.the-fellowship-sphere`). The box a scenario is printed in gives the flag (`src/screens/data/campaign-data.ts`, `BOXES`).
- No campaign card. It is placed only "while setting up a scenario in Campaign Mode", and its Resolution is followed only after a campaign win (`rr.campaign-cards`, `ltp.campaign-cards`). Its Setup does not run (A Shadow of the Past deals no Mr. Underhill, Gandalf's Delay, Gildor Inglorion or The Ring Draws Them; its Normal encounter deck is the scenario's own 40 cards), and a win has no choice to make: the engine's definition carries no `campaignCard` and an empty `resolution`.
- No boons or burdens. They are earned in Campaign Mode and are not in a game until then (`rr.boons`, `rr.burdens`). No campaign pool, no Campaign Setup Checklist, no threat penalty (each player starts at the printed threat of their heroes), no fallen heroes, no captives.
- A win is scored by the Learn to Play's system, which is for "players who enjoy playing through the same scenario multiple times" (`ltp.scoring`, `rr.scoring`, `faq.scoring`; `src/campaign/score.ts`), and the score is kept with the game. A loss keeps the engine's reason and the completed rounds.
- Only a playable scenario is dealt: one whose dealt cards have an effect record for every printed line (`src/campaign/playable.ts`, kept honest by the coverage test). Campaign-dealt cards have no inactive-text path, so an unplayable scenario is listed and refused, not dealt with inactive encounter text.
- A printed line conditioned on Campaign Mode (141062:A:1, "Use this action only if you are playing campaign mode") reads whether the campaign card is in the game: the condition `campaign`. The engine places a campaign card next to the quest deck (zone `campaign`, in play) when it has an ability that works while the scenario is played, such as 141056's Response; a standalone game's definition carries no `campaignCard`, so none is placed and the line is not usable. A campaign card with only a Setup and a Resolution is not placed.
- Each game can be resumed (its seed and intents are replayed, as a save's open scenario is), archived from History and restored as it was, or deleted after a second, explicit confirm. A finished game can be played again: a new briefing with the same seats, difficulty and Ring-bearer, and the same saved decks picked by id as they are now (a deck deleted since is not picked). A game from another engine version or data hash is dropped and its row kept.

### Scenario state

Zones are real objects, not a map of string-keyed piles copied four times.

- Seat: threat, deck, hand, discard, heroes, play area (allies and attachments), engaged enemies. From Breaking of the Fellowship stage 2B, also that seat's staging area, quest stage and active location (`Seat.staging`, `Seat.stage`, `Seat.active`; Breaking of the Fellowship, stage 2B).
- Shared, until a script says otherwise: staging area, active location (a second active-location slot only when the script says so), quest deck and current stage, encounter deck and discard, victory display, set aside, removed from the game, the campaign card next to the quest deck (zone `campaign`, only while it has an ability that works during the scenario).
- Optional, declared by the script: burden deck, Ring-bearer life tracker, extra encounter deck, the captive zone (the Ring-bearer and its attachments facedown under the Watcher in the Water).
- A seat's player side quest sits in its quest area (zone `seatQuest`), and a contract is in its play area from the start (Player side quests, contracts and saga cards, below). The full list is `ZoneName` in `src/rules/core/state.ts`.
- Created by a card line: cards facedown under a location. Weather Hills (141049:A:1) prints "place 1 non-unique enemy in play facedown under this location. While an enemy is under this location, it is out of play", and 141049:A:2 returns each one to the staging area face up after the location leaves play. The engine holds such a card in the zone `under`, with the location as its host; it is out of play and is not one of the location's attachments. It is not a widget: the table draws nothing for it.

A card instance has: instance id, catalog code, zone, exhausted, damage, progress, resources, host, attachment ids, committed to the quest, shadow card id if it is an enemy, face, controller. Control is not the same thing as in play. The Ring-bearer can be in play, facedown, under Watcher in the Water, and controlled by nobody. Tokens on that hero are discarded when the quest card says to discard them. Attached cards go with the hero and are also uncontrolled while the hero is facedown.

### The engine plays the game

The app applies rules, keywords, phases, effects, and the numbers those produce. The player chooses among options the current rules allow. A choice carries a number only when the rule asks the player to distribute a total the engine has already computed, such as archery.

Legal player intents are a closed set. The UI asks `legalIntents(state)` and renders only that set.

- Keep or mulligan.
- Commit a set of legal characters to the quest, or to a Hide test.
- Play a card the current window allows, naming which legal resource pools pay its printed cost.
- Trigger an action or response the engine has judged legal, and pick legal targets.
- Pass an optional window. Seats pass in order. That is what closes an action window. The phase machine then advances on its own.
- Travel or decline. Optionally engage or decline. Declare a legal defender, or leave the attack undefended. Declare legal attackers.
- Distribute a fixed total (archery, or an effect that says "assign N damage") across legal recipients so the assigned amounts sum to N.
- Choose one of the resolution options the scenario script offers.

Everything else is an engine step. The UI cannot dispatch it, and no control on the table types it in.

- Setup, staging, shadow cards, refresh, threat 50, and "The One Ring left play" run because the framework reached them.
- Easy mode (`ltp.easy-mode`, `easy-mode.easy-mode`): the encounter deck holds the Easy counts, and each hero starts with one resource in its pool, added in Setup step 2, "Place Heroes and Set Initial Threat Levels" (`rr.setup`). The Learn to Play says only "during setup"; the Easy Mode sheet names step 2; they agree. Normal adds nothing.
- Nightmare (user request, 2026-10-30; Nightmare, below): a scenario with a Nightmare deck is played with its setup card's recipe. Begin with the standard quest and encounter decks, remove the cards side B lists (each copy from the pile it is in: the encounter deck first, then the piles the quest's Setup fills), shuffle in the Nightmare deck's cards, and place the setup card next to the quest deck, side A faceup. Its lines stay active all game. A Nightmare card takes the Setup slot of a removed card of its title (Nightmare Fell Rider is staged one per player). With none, the slot stays empty (Journey in the Dark's staged Mines of Moria). The setup card is in its own in-play zone (`nightmare`), not the campaign card's. Nightmare adds no starting resources, and it is played in Campaign Mode and standalone alike. The briefing offers it, marked with the Eye of Sauron, only for a scenario in `NIGHTMARE_PLAYABLE` (`src/campaign/playable.ts`): every printed line of its Nightmare deck and setup card has a record. The Oath and The Caves of Nibin-Dûm have no Nightmare deck.
- Each hero gains one resource of its own sphere in the resource phase. Saga Frodo's resource is that same gain. His pool may then pay Fellowship and neutral costs. Seats draw one card in seat order. No other gain or draw happens unless an effect instruction says so.
- Paying a cost removes exactly the printed cost. The chosen pools must sum to that cost, and each pool must be allowed to pay that card's sphere. Overpay, underpay, a wrong sphere, and spending outside a payment are rejected.
- Quest resolution compares committed willpower with staging threat. The active location is not in staging and does not add threat. Progress that would go on the quest goes on the active location first. Overflow goes on the quest. Progress beyond the current stage's quest points does not carry over: the next stage starts with none, so one quest resolution advances one stage at most (`rr.quest-advancement`, `ltp.quest-advancement`); a stage that cannot advance takes all of it (`faq.progress-without-advancing`), and advances as soon as nothing holds it back, with no more progress: the engine checks between two steps of the round, with no window open, no effect resolving and no attack under way (To the River... once no Hill Troll is in play, 01126:B:2; `rr.quest-advancement`, "immediately"; `ltp.quest-advancement`, a stage is completed when it holds its quest points). This also completes a stage whose quest points drop to its progress (Surprise Attack, 41005:B:2, when an enemy leaves play) once it holds any progress. The quest card's preview names what holds a full stage back. When an intent the player sent advances the quest, the table shows the stage it reached on its B side, large, with Progress to go back to the table, or the stage's When Revealed question when it asks one (user request, 2026-10-29; `docs/design-system-table.md`, Quest advance). A shortfall raises the threat of every player still in the game by the difference (Learn to Play p12-13); an eliminated seat is not raised. Breaking of the Fellowship stage 2B may override this. The core round does not.
- A defended attack places `max(0, attack − defense)` damage after modifiers. An undefended attack places the attack value. Willpower and damage read a modifier list. A `constant` effect appends a record to that list. Toughness X, on an enemy, then reduces what is placed. An indestructible enemy is not destroyed. A character at damage equal to its hit points is destroyed. The player does not enter the result. Adding a card to a zone does not reveal it. Hide, surge, and When Revealed run on a reveal.
- Forced, When Revealed, Setup, shadow, surge, doomed, and Hide run on their timing. The player does not confirm that they happened.
- A revealed treachery is resolved, then placed in the encounter discard pile unless its text says otherwise (`ltp.revealed-treachery`: "resolve the text on the card, and then place it in the encounter deck discard pile, unless the card specifically states otherwise"; `rr.treachery-cards`, `rr.step-3-3`). A canceled treachery goes there too (`rr.cancel`: "it is still placed in the encounter discard pile"). A treachery whose text attached it (a Condition, Weapon or Mount: 141043, 141055, 141070, 141074, 141080) stays attached; one whose "if able" attach found no host is discarded. Its surge resolves after that (`rr.surge`, "immediately after resolving the staging of the encounter card"). Enemies, locations and objectives stay in staging (`rr.step-3-3`). No encounter card in this box is a treasure: RingsDB types the boons and burdens `treasure`, and the build gives each its printed type (a burden treachery is a treachery), so no treasure rule applies.

An intent outside the current window, with an illegal target, or with a number the rule did not ask for, is rejected with a reason. The table has no damage stepper, resource stepper, threat dial, or phase scrubber. Drop targets are zone ids the legality function returned. There is no freeform x/y table, and a drop does not move a card until the reducer accepts it.

Undo stores the intent log and the engine steps that followed, and rebuilds state, with a snapshot at each phase boundary. Replaying from that snapshot restores the state and the seed cursor. Do not diff a giant JSON tree. The interpreter and the reducer are pure. Shuffles advance the seed cursor stored on the scenario. No clock, no network, no `Math.random`.

The table's Undo (user request, 2026-10-25) takes back the last accepted intent, one at a time, while that intent showed the player nothing they had not seen: no card left a deck (a draw, a reveal, a card dealt face up), no card turned face up (a shadow card, a facedown card, a Lost Island turned to its real side, a Grotto location turned to its Underwater side), no card in a deck or face down was named by the window it opened (a Look, a Pick among the top cards, a search, a look at a Lost Island's facedown side), the top of no player's deck changed, no random result was drawn (a shuffle, a random discard: the seed cursor did not move), and no card came into being. An intent that showed a hidden card, or that won or lost the scenario, cannot be taken back, and neither can any intent before it. Taking an intent back restores the state before it, seed cursor included, and removes it from the save, so sending it again gives the same result and a relaunch replays the same game. Undo is a labeled button in the table's dock, never a gesture, and it is shown only while there is something to take back. While a pick is being made, Undo puts back the pick first.

Decisions this file owns, because the sources are silent or the user chose:

- A `ReduceOpeningHand` Setup record (141078 Gandalf's Delay) runs with player Setup, before the opening hand, so the hand and a mulligan redraw are 6 minus the reduction. The booklet orders player Setup before the hand and encounter Setup before the quest card's Setup, and says nothing about encounter Setup against the hand. User-accepted reading.
- Saga staging reveals one encounter card per seat, in seat order. "You" on an encounter card is the seat that revealed it. Surge: the same seat reveals another card. Doomed X raises every seat's threat. Peril: the other seats cannot act or trigger responses while that card's staging resolves; a Response on a card nobody controls (the quest card) goes to the revealing seat meanwhile, and an enemy attack the card starts is open to every seat (`faq.peril-attacks`). The lock ends before that card's surge.
- The encounter deck is shuffled back from the encounter discard pile when it is out of cards during the quest phase, once the revealed card's staging is resolved, or as soon as an effect needs a card (`rr.running-out-of-cards`, `faq.last-encounter-card`). Outside the quest phase it stays empty.
- An engagement check engages the enemy in staging with the highest engagement cost at or below the seat's threat. The first player chooses among ties.
- Shadow cards are dealt to each seat's enemies, highest engagement cost first (`rr.step-6-2`). A shadow card stays with its enemy after it resolves; it is discarded when the enemy leaves play, and every one at the end of combat (`ltp.shadow-discard`). An enemy that makes an attack outside the combat phase, or an additional attack, is dealt a new shadow card, after any it had is discarded (`rr.attacks-outside-the-combat-phase`, `faq.additional-attacks`); outside combat that card is discarded when the attack ends (`faq.enemy-attacks-outside-combat`).
- Each seat declares at most one attack against each enemy in the combat phase; an attack a card makes (Quick Strike) is extra (`faq.limitations-on-attacks`). A ranged character may join an attack another seat declares (`rr.ranged`, `rr.step-6-8-1`). A defender that left play before damage leaves the attack undefended (`rr.step-6-4-3`).
- A character or enemy whose damage reaches its hit points is destroyed whenever that happens, a lost hit point bonus included (`rr.hit-points`). A card enters play ready and with no tokens (`rr.ready`).
- Simultaneous effects: when two conflicting effects would occur at once, the first player decides their order (`faq.simultaneous-effects`, FAQ 1.02). Responses to one game occurrence share one window, and the player uses them in any order (`rr.triggering-condition`: "those triggering conditions are handled with a single response window in which abilities ... may be used in any order"; user decision 2026-10-26). A commit of several characters is one occurrence: each committed character's Responses are offered together, first player first. A Response whose resource cost cannot be paid when the window opens waits in it and is listed once another Response has paid for it (Théodred adds the resource Aragorn spends). With nothing usable, no window opens. Forced and delayed effects that conflict still resolve in a fixed order (the order their triggers queue); that part stays open in `docs/phases/STATUS.md`.
- An eliminated seat's hand, deck and cards it controls go to their owners' discard piles, its engaged enemies return to staging with their damage, an attack on it ends with the action window between its steps, a Peril lock it held is released, and the first player token passes on at once if it held it (`rr.player-elimination`, `faq.first-player-elimination`, `mec109.peril`).

Decisions this file owns:

- Restricted: a character cannot have more than 2 attachments that carry the keyword. When a third is attached, the character's controller chooses one of them and it moves at once to its owner's discard pile (`rr.restricted`). The keyword is read from the card; the "Attach to ..." sentence is a constant `AttachesTo` record.
- Action windows: every action window of the framework chart opens (`rr.framework-steps`, `rr.attack-resolution-chart`; user decision 2026-10-25): after the resource draw, in planning, at quest commit, after staging, after quest resolution, after travel, after optional engagement, after the engagement checks, after the shadow cards are dealt, between the steps of each enemy attack (before the defender, before the shadow card, before damage, before the attack ends; out of combat too) and of each player attack (after the attackers are declared, after damage), and after the first player token passes. A window in which no seat has an action it could take changes nothing and is skipped. An Action, and an event with an Action, can be used in any of them, the Hide test's action window included. Allies and attachments are played in the planning phase only, by the player whose planning window it is: each seat has its own window in player order (`rr.step-2-2`, `rr.step-2-3`). A window closes when every seat has passed in a row; a play or an action means the seats that passed are asked again (`rr.action-windows`). A card of a sphere needs a hero of that sphere even at cost 0 (`rr.resource-match`). An event with no legal target for a required choice cannot be played (`rr.initiating-abilities-playing-cards`, step 1). An event stays in the hand while its effect resolves and goes to its owner's discard pile after, whether or not the effect was canceled (`rr.cancel`).
- Cancel: a Response that cancels (A Test of Will, Hasty Stroke) is offered before the effect it answers begins (`rr.cancel`): when a card with "when revealed" effects is revealed, and when the shadow card dealt to an attacking enemy has a shadow effect. A canceled effect is still used; the revealed card stays revealed. A card with nothing to cancel offers nothing. "Look at the top card" (Henamarth Riversong) opens a look window that the player closes with a pass; it changes nothing. A search puts the cards looked at back on top, in the order the player chooses, one position at a time.
- Playing from and into other zones: an ally put into play from a hand or a discard pile (Sneak Attack, Stand and Fight, Elf-stone) cannot be a unique card already in play. An ally taken from another player's discard pile is under the new controller until it leaves play, and goes to its owner's discard pile. X on an event (Stand and Fight) is whatever the player pays. Sneak Attack's return and Beorn's shuffle are delayed effects that resolve at the end of the phase they were made in (`rr.delayed-effects`).
- Responses to engagements, attacks and plays: a Response that says "After you engage an enemy with a higher engagement cost than your threat" (Sam Gamgee, Pippin) is offered when the engagement is done, to the player who engaged only (`rr.response`). "After Merry participates in an attack that destroys an enemy" is offered to each attacker once the damage has destroyed the enemy. A Response to a card entering play or being played is offered once the card is in play. A Response is offered immediately after its triggering condition has occurred and resolved (`rr.response`; user decision 2026-10-25), first player first: over any window the framework opened since (an action window, a defense, archery, the choice of an enemy, the next attack, the optional engagement, the travel choice), which comes back after. It waits only while an effect or a revealed card is still resolving and asks for a choice or a Hide commit, and while another trigger's Responses or cancel are being offered.
- Gandalf (hero, 142002): the top card of his controller's deck is faceup, and once per phase that player may play it as if it were in their hand, in the windows that card could be played in from the hand. While it is paid for, Gandalf counts as having the Leadership, Lore, Tactics and Spirit icons; the resources are taken from the heroes that can pay. The new top card is not offered again that phase.
- Per-player limits: Éowyn's Action "may be triggered by each player once each round". Any player may trigger it, from their own hand, and each player once a round. The engine keeps one use per player per round.
- Archery X: the engine totals X across every archery card as the combat phase begins, before the shadow cards are dealt, and the player distributes that total across legal characters; the first seat decides disputes; defense does not block it.
- Indestructible and toughness X are defined here for enemies only. Extend them to another card type only when a card prints the keyword there, and cite that card.
- A Permanent boon or burden is attached to one hero when earned and recorded on the log; it cannot be discarded while that hero is in play, and it leaves the game with the hero.
- A "cannot attack" effect (Feint, Mr. Underhill 141017:A:3, every `BarAttack` record) used on an enemy already declared as an attacker against that player, before the attack deals its damage, ends that attack as a canceled one does (user decision, 2026-10-31): Mr. Underhill's only window against a Black Rider is after a failed Hide test has made it attack. An attack against another player goes on, and once the damage is dealt the attack ends normally.

## Fellowship of the Ring

MEC109 reprints **The Black Riders** (RingsDB pack `TBR`, 2013) and **The Road Darkens** (pack `TRD`, 2014) as one campaign. There is no RingsDB pack code for the 2022 box. The campaign id is `fellowship-of-the-ring`. The box is 329 cards: 7 heroes, 57 player cards, 22 quests, 209 encounters, 6 campaign cards, 28 boons and burdens.

Every scenario in the box starts the same way. The engine does it. Do not ask the player to remember the booklet.

- The first seat must control a Fellowship-sphere hero with the Ring-bearer trait. That hero is one the player put in the deck. Do not insert a Frodo the player did not choose.
- Attach The One Ring to that hero. The One Ring is an objective granted by setup. It is not a deck card and it does not count against the 50-card minimum.

- Fellowship-sphere heroes cannot be used outside saga scenarios. The deck may still be saved. The briefing refuses them.
- Two-handed: when the first-player token passes in refresh, the new first seat gains control of the attached Ring-bearer, the resources in that hero's pool, and every card attached to that hero. If that transfer leaves a seat with no heroes, that seat is eliminated immediately.

### Scenario 1 resolution

This is a transaction, not a notes field. On a win the player chooses one of:

- `Gandalf's Delay` or `The Ring Draws Them`, then
- `Gildor Inglorion` or `Mr. Underhill`.

Each chosen card is added to the campaign pool and recorded on the log.

The choice is printed on the back of the campaign card 141030. Each campaign card's Setup and Resolution lines are effect records owned by the card's ledger lines, with timings `campaignSetup` and `resolution`. ### Breaking of the Fellowship, stage 2B

Stage 2A removes the Ring-bearer and its attachments from the game, with The One Ring's text blank while that resolves: the cards go to the removed zone without being destroyed or discarded, so The One Ring's loss line has nothing to fire. A seat left with no hero is eliminated. Each enemy's engagement cost is 0 during that round's encounter phase, and that round has no travel phase (`engagementCostZero`, `skipTravel`). Stage 1B skips the encounter phase (`skipEncounter`, 142061:B:1); the combat phase still follows.

At the end of the refresh phase on stage 2B, Seat of Seeing and Frodo's Choice are shuffled into the encounter discard pile, which goes under the encounter deck. Each seat, starting with the first, creates its own staging area and advances to a different stage 3A of its choice (142063 to 142066; a true solo player chooses among all four), then the seats move the shared encounter cards to their own areas one at a time in player order, each seat choosing its own card (the last card is taken without asking). Stage 2B is then discarded to the set-aside pile as a defeated stage is, and each seat hears it for its own stage 3A, in player order, as one occurrence: a stage 3A reveals one encounter card for its seat when the total Threat in that seat's staging area is under 4. Phases still resolve in seat order, starting with the first seat.

The state: `ScenarioState.separate` is true from the first `StartSeparateStage`, `Seat.staging` lists that seat's encounter cards and `Seat.stage` is its quest stage; `ScenarioState.questStage` is then null. Every card in an area stays in the zone `staging`, and `ScenarioState.staging` keeps listing every staging card of every area (plus any in no area), so code that does not care which area reads it as before. The engine asks per seat through `viewOf(state, seat)` (`src/rules/families/breaking/areas.ts`): the state with that seat's area as `staging` and its stage as `questStage`. An instruction runs in the view of the seat that acts, so "the staging area", "this stage" and "the quest" are that seat's; a pick's `stagingArea` says `own` (the default), `shared` or `any` ("in any staging area"). A card that enters "the staging area" goes to the acting or revealing seat's area; an enemy that returns from being engaged goes to the area of the seat it was engaged with.

After a seat commits to its own stage, it reveals its encounter card into its own area, then compares its own committed willpower with the threat in its own staging area. Progress goes on its stage (or on the active location first), or its own threat rises by the difference. Another seat does not raise threat for that failure. Each seat's quest resolves in player order; "the players quested successfully" for a delayed effect is true when every living seat did.

Engagement checks and optional engagement use only that seat's staging area. "At this stage" does not reach another seat's cards. Archery is totaled per stage (the seat's staging area, the enemies engaged with it and its stage card), each stage in player order with its own window, and only that seat assigns it, to its own characters. Cards that say "the staging area" or "the quest" mean that seat's. Seats may still affect each other through ordinary card effects that do not use those phrases.

If a seat is eliminated while stages are separate, that stage and the encounter cards at it are discarded: the cards in its area, and the enemies that were engaged with it (they return to its area first), go to the encounter discard pile, and its stage to the set-aside pile.

Each seat has an active location of its own at its stage. The active location buffers the quest (`rr.active-location`), and "the quest only applies to your quest stage" (`mec109.multiple-staging-areas`); 142079 prints "a player at this staging area ... travel here" and 142075 "While The Argonath is the active location". `Seat.active` is that seat's active location (the card keeps the zone `activeLocation`); `ScenarioState.activeLocation` and `secondActiveLocation` mirror seat 0's and seat 1's, so code outside a seat's view still sees them, and in `viewOf(state, seat)` `activeLocation` is that seat's and `secondActiveLocation` is null. In the travel phase each living seat, in player order, has its own opportunity (`ScenarioState.travelDone`, `rr.travel`, `rr.step-4-2`): it may travel to a location of its own staging area, even when another seat has an active location, and only a seat with none can; the phase's opportunity ends with the last seat's. A seat's questing progress goes on its own active location first. The Travel effects of 142073, 142078 and 142079 resolve for the seat that travels. Before stage 2B the active location is shared; one still active then goes back to the staging area as the areas are created, and a location a seat's area holds when that seat is eliminated is discarded with it.

Stage 3 (142063 to 142066). Each 3B holds `progressDiscards` while Frodo's Choice is not in play: progress that would be placed on it discards that many cards from the top of the encounter deck instead, once the progress step is over (`ScenarioState.progressOwed`, `payOwed`). Frodo's Choice (142068:A:2) takes the first player token for its revealer, who cannot lose it: the token does not pass in the refresh phase (`firstPlayerLocked`; `rr.first-player`). Archery X on 142070 is the players at its stage (one once stages are separate); 142078 and 142082 add to the archery total of the stage that holds them. 142077 raises the threat of the seat whose area holds it at the end of the travel phase, then moves to the next living seat's area. 142080 and its Shadow move an enemy to the first player's staging area. The Argonath has Threat X for the players in the game, cannot be traveled to while Sarn Gebir is in play, and skips the combat phase while it is the active location (`skipCombat`). 142073 keeps the players from winning while it is in play, as a card that holds a stage back does (`Restrict advance`). 142074 (Seat of Seeing), once explored, removes a burden card in play, in the encounter deck or in its discard pile from the game and, in Campaign Mode, from the campaign pool.

Fallen Into Evil (142083) attaches to a non-Fellowship hero, the first player choosing among all of them, and the hero becomes an enemy card in the first player's staging area with Threat equal to its Willpower and engagement cost equal to its threat cost; it keeps its attack, defense and hit points and its damage, and its game text is blank while it is an enemy. In the engine it is the same card with the code `<code>~enemy` (`FALLEN_SUFFIX`, `src/rules/core/printed.ts`), which a table draws with the hero's art. A seat left with no hero is eliminated. When Fallen Into Evil leaves play the hero returns among its owner's heroes; one defeated as an enemy goes to its owner's discard pile. The campaign card's Resolution logs the hero Fallen Into Evil still holds as fallen, and a hero that is no longer among its player's heroes is not offered as a captive.

## Ered Mithrin

The Ered Mithrin campaign is nine scenarios played in order: The Wilds of Rhovanion (Journey Up the Anduin, Lost in Mirkwood, The King's Quest), the six Adventure Packs (The Withered Heath, Roam Across Rhovanion, Fire in the Night, The Ghost of Framsburg, Mount Gundabad, The Fate of Wilderland) and, for campaign mode, the Ered Mithrin Campaign Expansion (MEC115, rules doc `emce`, RingsDB `EMCE`), which adds the nine campaign cards, the boons, the burdens and the treasures (user decision, 2026-11-05: all nine scenarios; the first two deal the Revised Core sets). The campaign is registered (`data/campaigns/ered-mithrin.json`: `saga` false, no `xp`, `bookletId` `ered-mithrin`, each scenario's page of the book) and its nine scenarios are one box on the Scenarios tab, `ered-mithrin`. Which of them are playable is `src/campaign/playable.ts`; one that is not is coming soon, as in Dream-chaser. There is no XP and no Hard/Expert Mode card: the campaign runs on treasures, boon allies and encounter cards in the pool. Nightmare and the MEC115 Achievements are not built.

## Deckbuilding

The builder is a root route beside the campaign list.

`deck_legal` is set at catalog build for `hero`, `ally`, `attachment`, `event`, `contract`, `player-side-quest` and `treasure` rows (`src/catalog/deck-legal.ts`; a treasure typed `Campaign` is not). `Campaign`, encounter, quest, and nightmare rows are not deck-legal. TBR's pack file mixes heroes with `type_name: "Campaign"`. Filter those out in the build script, not in the UI.

A deck saves at any stage, so a deck can be built over several sittings (user decision, 2026-10-25). The deck list and the editor name the first rule a deck that is not ready breaks ("Needs 3 more cards"); a ready deck carries no mark, and the list keeps its Ready and Not ready filters (user instruction, 2026-11-05). The briefing does not offer a deck that is not ready. Adding a copy past the copy limit, a hero twice, or a fourth hero is refused at the tap, with a sentence naming the card and the rule, and does not change the deck.

Universal rules, which decide whether a deck is ready to play, are the Rules Reference's (`rr.deckbuilding`; user decision 2026-10-25):

- One to three heroes, no two with the same title.
- At least 50 other cards.
- At most three copies of a title, across its printings, and never more than the card's `deck_limit`. `is_unique` does not lower that cap. A second copy of a unique title cannot enter play; that is not a save check. A contract in the deck is no deck card and does not count toward the minimum; at most one. The deckbuilding rules printed on its side A are `DeckRule` instructions on its records (no allies, copies by title, one starting hero or four, different spheres, an exact or larger size, cards per sphere) and change these checks (`src/catalog/deck-rules.ts`, `deck-save.ts`).
- A card's sphere does not have to match a hero to be saved. It needs a hero of that sphere to be played (`rr.resource-match`), which the table checks. Saga rules still run on the briefing loadout, not on this save.
- A restriction record on a card (trait, sphere, campaign) is enforced when that record exists. If the printed deckbuilding limit has no record yet, the card can still be saved and carries the inactive-text mark. Do not invent a restriction, and do not ask the player to enforce it at the table.

Fellowship rules (Fellowship of the Ring, above) run on the briefing loadout, not on the saved deck. MEC109 says Frodo is the only Ring-bearer in this box.

- The hero count runs on the saved deck. The saga rules run on the loadout.
- The Ring-bearer slot is required when the saved deck has no Ring-bearer, and unused when it already has one. A fourth hero is legal only when the extra hero is that Ring-bearer.
- Two-handed play requires a single Ring-bearer across the table. It does not reject two decks that share some other unique card.

A saved deck does not have to be legal for any particular campaign. The briefing picks one saved deck per seat, offers only a deck that is ready to play, and refuses to deal when the loadout fails. The error names the card and the rule.

The deck list is one list, the deck saved last first; the two starter decks are ordinary rows, not pinned (user request, 2026-10-25). It shows each deck as a card with its heroes' pictures or as a compact row, and filters by a search over deck and hero names, by ready or not ready, and by a hero's sphere. Manage offers Duplicate and Move up / Move down (a reorder with no drag) on every deck, Delete on the player's own (after a second, explicit tap; playthroughs keep the cards their loadout copied), and Reset on a starter, which puts it back to the printed list: a starter is put back on launch when it is missing, so it is never deleted.

Import accepts a RingsDB decklist id or a `ringsdb.com` URL: a decklist or a personal deck, by its page (with or without its slug) or its API URL, via `GET /api/public/decklist/{id}.json` or `GET /api/public/deck/{id}.json` (user instruction, 2026-11-05). A personal deck is returned only while its owner shares it; when RingsDB does not return one, the screen says to make it public in its RingsDB sharing settings. Both payloads list each hero in `slots` as well as in `heroes`; the slot entry is the same hero, not a second copy. Cards that are not in the official pool are reported by name and dropped. The result is an ordinary saved deck, saved at once and opened in the editor, which names what was dropped and what the deck still lacks.

The import screen also lists the pre-built decks, offline: every decklist an official product prints. They are data: `data/prebuilt-decks.json` names each one and `pnpm data:build` writes `data/decks/prebuilt.json`. Pre-built decks are not seeded, and are not in the printed-text or effect scope.

- The four core-set decks are the ones the Learn to Play prints (`ltp.starter-decks`): the three heroes it names, every copy of the Revised Core Set's cards in its collector range, and two Gandalf (73). That is 47 cards, so the editor shows "At least 50 cards." until three are added, and the deck's note says why; DragnCards' deck of the same sphere (the 2011 core set's 30-card list) is a cross-check on the heroes and titles.
- Every other deck is a `decklist.*` span read from the product's own PDF (pinned with the rules in `data/sources.json`, decklist pages only): the four 2019 Starter Decks (whose deck and sideboard must also equal DragnCards' lists and the product's RingsDB contents), the two starter decklists in each of the Angmar Awakened, Dream-chaser and Ered Mithrin Hero Expansions and The Two Towers and The Return of the King Saga Expansions, the decklists in the six original saga rulesheets and the two Hobbit rulesheets, and FFG's three Community Deck Lists.
- A row is resolved by its name, type and collector number within the products the list names (a Community Deck List row: the products it prints), through Hall of Beorn. Where Hall of Beorn numbers a revised product's reprints differently from the print (The Two Towers, The Return of the King, Ered Mithrin), the deck is marked `renumbered` and a number that matches no printing falls back to the name, only when one card has it.
- A printed misspelling, short name or wrong collector number is an alias with its reason; a heading total that disagrees with its rows is a recorded misprint with its reason; a printed condition (Valor and Vigilance's 0-3x rows) is a chosen count, and the condition shows as the editor's note.
- The screen is one list: the RingsDB field and Import scroll away above the decks, which sit under sticky full-width group headings (Core Set, Starter Decks, Hero Expansions, Saga Expansions, Original Saga Expansions, The Hobbit, Community Deck Lists); each row shows the heroes' spheres and names.
- Tapping a pre-built deck opens it in the editor as a new deck (heroes and cards, without the sideboard), with its note (a printed condition, or why a core deck is 47 cards) on the editor's note line; Save is the one action, so a tap never saves a second copy.

Filters on the pool screen: name, sphere, type, pack or cycle, trait, cost, unique, and whether the card's text is applied (the Applied only chip). Opening a card shows stats, text, and the errata line. A line the app does not apply is labeled Not applied, with what that costs the player (an ability not applied, a card that cannot be played). Nothing on that screen writes a campaign.

## Effects

### Three layers

1. **Keywords**, above.
2. **Unique card text.** An effect record:

```ts
type Effect = {
  id: string; // "<code>:<timing>:<n>", unique
  cardCode: string;
  lines: string[]; // the ledger lines it implements, "<code>:<side>:<n>"; at least one
  timing:
    | "setup"
    | "forced"
    | "triggered"
    | "whenRevealed"
    | "response"
    | "action"
    | "shadow"
    | "constant"
    | "questResolution"
    | "campaignSetup"  // the Setup printed on a campaign card
    | "resolution"     // the Resolution printed on a campaign card
    | "travel"
    | "engagement"
    | "beginningOfPhase"
    | "endOfPhase"
    | "endOfRound";
  trigger?: Trigger;  // the engine event it answers; the list is `triggerSchema` plus the families' TRIGGERS
  limit?: Limit;      // oncePerPhase, oncePerRound, oncePerRoundEachPlayer, twicePerPhase, oncePerAttack, oncePerGame, ... (`limitSchema`)
  cost?: Cost | Cost[];
  subject?: "self" | "host" | PickSpec;  // which card the event must be about
  anyPlayer?: boolean;                   // any player may use this Action or Response
  fromHand?: true;                       // used from the owner's hand only
  cancel?: { cost: number; sphere?: Sphere; unless?: Condition }; // the first player may pay to cancel it
  condition?: Condition;
  instructions: Instruction[];
};
```

`forced` and `triggered` effects name the engine event they answer in `trigger`. A `triggered` effect is mandatory and not printed as Forced. It resolves before the Forced effects on the same event, and those are collected after it resolves. Forced effects then resolve oldest card in play first, each in its own frame. A setup record for a card that the scenario definition or the campaign script already puts out describes that placement. It never places the card a second time.

`constant` is a while-in-play modifier. It appends a record to the modifier list. The engine rebuilds those records from the effect list after every intent, so they follow the source card and its condition. Hide numbers (the X, the discarded threat, the committed willpower) are held by the table or by a seat in the same list. A constant that forbids something (`Restrict`: advance, travel, attach, hero limit, defend) is checked where the thing happens; a bar that falls on other cards (cannot attack, take damage, take non-combat damage, be engaged, be optionally engaged) is a flag on the modifier list of each card it bars. A card's stat below 0 counts as 0, and a stat set to a value (`SetStat`) overrides every other modifier (`rr.modifiers`). A constant whose condition is "while this card is in the victory display" works from there (141046:A:2, `rr.in-play-and-out-of-play`). A lasting effect of an encounter card on "each ally" also holds for allies that enter play later (`rr.lasting-effects`). A response names the event it answers in `trigger`.

An Action or Response may name a `cost` (exhaust or discard the card, add it to the victory display, or pay resources from the heroes the player names) and a `limit`. The cost is paid when the effect is triggered, and the use is recorded for the limit. A Response window offers each Response a seat may use after an event; a pass gives up all of that seat's offers for it. Peril limits who is offered. Instructions are engine verbs. The vocabulary (verbs, triggers, stat flags) is split by mechanic family: `src/catalog/families/<family>.ts` declares each family's share and `index.ts` composes and lists them; `src/rules/families/<family>/` runs them and `src/effects/authored/families/<family>.ts` holds their records (`docs/plans/effect-authoring.md`). The verb list grows when a dealt card or a starter card needs a verb. That growth stays inside this interpreter. There is no second language.

The Zod schemas for `Effect`, `Instruction`, `Condition`, `Cost`, and `ScenarioDefinition` live in `src/catalog`. `src/effects` exports the generated records plus the authored ones, an authored id replacing a generated one. The interpreter takes the effect list as an argument and does not import `src/effects`; `src/campaign`, `src/play` and `src/screens` import it and pass it in. There is no effects table in `catalog.db`.

Gandalf's Delay (141078) has two Setup records: the placement in staging, and `ReduceOpeningHand` amount 1. It is cited to MEC109 #46 (user-accepted). RingsDB's row for 141078 differs from the MEC109 printing on the starting-hand rule; the MEC109 printing wins (user decision). It applies with player Setup (Core round). The same holds in a later scenario, where the campaign pool has shuffled it into the encounter deck: it is in the encounter deck at the beginning of the game, so its hand sentence counts before the hands are drawn and the encounter search places it after them (`mec109.setup`).

Frodo Baggins (141001) is in every game as the Ring-bearer, so his Response is a record like a dealt card's: after a card is revealed from the encounter deck, before any of its effects (keywords included) resolve, his controller may spend 1 Fellowship resource and exhaust The One Ring to cancel them; the card is shuffled back and the same seat reveals another. The other saga Frodo (142001) has a record too: when he defends, exhaust The One Ring and spend a Fellowship resource, and the attack deals no damage.

3. **Player cards with an open line.** Every deck-legal player card is automated (user decision, 2026-10-26). A card outside the box has no scan-read ledger entry: its printed lines are its source text written to the ledger's conventions (`src/catalog/pool-lines.ts`, read by `scripts/data/printed/pool.ts`: one ability per line, icons as words), and its records live in `src/effects/authored/pool/<pack>.ts`. Templates read the pool's lines like the box's. While a line has no record it is listed in `src/effects/open-lines.ts` (`pnpm effects:open` rewrites it; the coverage test checks it): the card can be paid for, played, quested, defended, and attacked with using printed numbers and implemented keywords. The line is shown, it is not offered, and there is no sheet on which the player applies it. When the record exists, the ability becomes a normal legal intent or a normal engine step. An event with no record is the exception: as the engine applies it, its Action could not change the game state, so it is not played and opens no action window (`rr.triggered-ability`: "A triggered ability on a player card can only be initiated if its effect has the potential to change the game state on its own"; user decision 2026-10-26). The mark is per printed line (`src/catalog/active-text.ts`): a line is applied when a record names it in `lines`, when it is only keywords the engine reads, or when the ledger marks it inert. A card the shipped ledger does not hold is read from its source text with the pool's reading, so the app names exactly the lines of `open-lines.ts` (the coverage test checks the two agree). The player sees the words "Not applied", never "inactive".

A player card outside the box that the player's own decks play can be brought into scope on request: it joins `PLAYER_REQUESTS` in `scripts/data/printed/scope.ts`, `pnpm data:sync scans` pins its scan, its lines are read into the ledger, and it gets a record like any other (user request, 2026-10-25). One is The Shirefolk (19121): "Play only if each of your heroes is a Hobbit" is the `heroesAll` condition on its Action, so it cannot be played otherwise, and it reduces its player's threat by 4.

The four core-set Learn to Play decks are in scope the same way (user request, 2026-10-26): their cards with text are in `PLAYER_REQUESTS`, read from the Revised Core Set scans, and every printed line is owned (the coverage test holds the requests to that). Their records are the pool's and the mechanic families' (`src/effects/authored/pool/Core.ts`, `src/effects/authored/families/`), one record per printed line; the core decks' tests (`src/campaign/starter/{leadership,lore,spirit,tactics}.test.ts`) play them. Readings: Brok Ironfist's Response is not offered while a Brok Ironfist is in play, since putting him into play then could not change the game state (`rr.triggered-ability`, `rr.unique-cards`; the record's `intoPlay` condition); Thalin damages only a card the encounter deck reveals (staging, a surge, the top card; `rr.staging`), not one a player searches for and reveals, as such a card is not "revealed from the encounter deck" for a cancel either (the record's `byEncounterDeck`); Dark Knowledge also answers the shadow card dealt for an attack outside the combat phase (`rr.attacks-outside-the-combat-phase`); an Action or Response whose effect has nothing to act on (no damaged hero for Daughter of the Nimrodel, no Condition attachment for Miner of the Iron Hills, no damage on Self Preservation's host) is not offered (`rr.triggered-ability`); and an encounter card that "counts as a Condition attachment" (141043, 141055) is one for "a Condition attachment" (`CountsAs`), so Miner of the Iron Hills can discard it. Stand Together's extra defenders are the chosen player's own characters, as printed: their defense is pooled and the defending player puts all the damage on one of them (`rr.multiple-defenders`).

Campaign-dealt cards cannot be inactive. That includes encounter cards, quest cards, boons, burdens, and The One Ring. A missing effect id there fails the coverage test, and the scenario stays unplayable.

### Player side quests, contracts and saga cards

These are supported by the engine and the catalog (families `sidequests` and `misc`; every deck-legal card is in scope, user decision 2026-10-26). The Rules Reference in `data/rules` has no span for side quests or contracts, so the rules follow the printed cards and the Learn to Play; the Battle, Siege and Guarded (X) keywords follow `hon.battle`, `hon.siege` and `hon.archery` (the Heirs of Númenor rulesheet) and `wor.guarded-x` (the Wilds of Rhovanion rulesheet).

- A player side quest sits in its player's quest area (zone `seatQuest`). Characters commit to it instead of the current quest; their Willpower (or attack for Battle, defense for Siege, `QuestsWith`) is placed on it as progress, not compared with staging threat, and it is defeated when its progress reaches its quest points.
- A contract starts in its player's play area, outside the deck, showing side A; its own abilities flip it (`FlipCard`), and each side's records carry a `faceUp` condition. Its side A deckbuilding rules are checked on save (Deckbuilding, above).
- A card with the Encounter keyword is set aside at the start and a player card effect shuffles it into the encounter deck, where it is revealed like an encounter card (`EncounterKeyword`). `VictoryLimit` and `InPlayLimit` cap copies in the victory display and in play.
- Saga cards the box does not deal (The One Ring and Master cards, collection heroes) are records in the `misc` family.
- Messenger of the King: the `(MotK)` hero versions of unique allies (pack `MotKA`) and the Messenger of the King contract (22134) are not in the card pool (`outOfScope` in `data/overrides.json`, user decision 2026-10-26).

### Window stack

Forced effects in the current window run in timestamp order. Responses are offered and may be passed. A Hide test, a shadow reveal, or a When Revealed pushes a frame and pops it before the parent continues. Do not flatten these into one modal queue with no stack. The Hide frame is under Hide tests, above; its action window comes before success or failure is decided.

### Coverage test

`scripts/checks/effect-coverage.test.ts` reads the printed lines from `data/cards` and the dealt cards from `data/scenarios`, with no database. It checks line ownership against the ledger: every printed line is owned by a record, an implemented keyword, or an `inert` ledger entry with a reason. A campaign is not marked playable unless that test finds an effect record for every printed line on its quest deck, encounter deck, burden deck, and the seeded starter decks, and for every Setup, Forced, When Revealed, Response, Action, Travel and shadow label by timing. `pnpm effects:draft <scenario | starter deck id | pack | codes>` writes a bundle under `.agent/drafts` for each line no template reads; the `effect-author` agent turns a bundle into authored records. A player deck that contains other official cards does not fail this test; the open-lines list names what is left, and it is meant to reach empty (user decision, 2026-10-26). If no rule id or quest card fixes an effect's timing, do not write the effect: leave the coverage test red and name the card.

## Data sources

`data/sources.json` lists every source with its URL, its pin (a sha256, or a git commit for the DragnCards plugin) and when it was fetched. `pnpm data:sync` is the only command that uses the network; it refreshes `data/raw/` (verbatim snapshots) and `.cache/pdf/` (the rule PDFs, not committed), and `pnpm data:build` turns them into `data/cards`, `data/scenarios`, `data/campaigns`, `data/rules` and `src/effects/generated` without it. `pnpm data:check` fails when a fresh build differs from what is committed.

| Need | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Packs, player cards, codes, errata, RingsDB images | RingsDB public API |
| Every card as typed JSON, the MEC109 printing, Normal, Easy and Nightmare counts per scenario | Hall of Beorn export |
| Card database, scenario load lists by pile, framework steps, automation snippets, card art | DragnCards LOTR plugin |

Sources disagree. The build cross-checks them (Hall of Beorn counts against the DragnCards load lists, piles against the quest card's Setup text) and a scenario that fails a check is not written. Each resolved disagreement is a row in `data/overrides.json` with its reason and source. Do not hand-edit generated data such as `data/scenarios/*.json`: fix the source or add an override with its reason.

RingsDB states that the texts are copyrighted by Fantasy Flight Games and that the API is for tools that complement the game.

`data/sources.json` records the fetch date; `catalog.db` carries it in a `meta` row.

Snapshot the card codes a scenario actually dealt into the save at scenario start, so a later catalog rebuild cannot rewrite a game in progress.

The Nightmare decks' cards (the `Nightmare` pack) are not the `pictures` source's: `pnpm data:sync nightmare` pins each side from the Nightmare proxies on Google Drive the user named (2026-10-30; `scripts/data/sources/nightmare.ts`, about 2348×3200, the setup card's side A and side B both), cached in `.cache/pictures` beside the others, and `pnpm scans:pack` bundles them the same way. The same pictures are the scans their ledger entries are read from.
4. For a card none of those has (the Messenger of the King allies, a few campaign cards): its RingsDB picture, then its Hall of Beorn or DragnCards one, about 430×600. A card of the scan scope keeps its scan instead.

No source has a picture of MEC109's own printing (JvZijp and cardgame.tools reuse The Black Riders' and The Road Darkens'), so the nine cards MEC109 rewords (`MEC109_CHANGED`) keep their scan.

The `scans` source stays what the printed-text ledger was read from; a picture is only shown. `scripts/build/pack-scans.ts` writes side A of every catalog card, and side B of every card pinned with one (the quest stages and the campaign cards), into `assets/scans`, scaled to at most 1200 px on its long side (`sharp`), with the map `src/screens/images/scan-map.ts`, which records each picture's width and height so a screen lays it out before it decodes (side B keyed `<code>.B`: a quest stage turned to its B side shows that side on the table and in its preview, `src/play/canvas/picture-key.ts`; a card with no bundled side B picture is drawn as text, never from side A's picture, and a development build downloads its side B scan, the catalog's `back_imagesrc`, in the meantime: a stage that showed 1A while on 1B read as stuck (user report, 2026-10-31)). JvZijp files quest cards and side quests upright; the pack lays them down as printed (a quest turned clockwise, a side quest the other way). The first quest card of each scenario in `data/scenarios` also gives its scene on its own, `<code>.scene`, cut at the source's size and not scaled (about 1540 × 470 from JvZijp's files), with a 1.2 px blur on a print scan (1600 px wide or more) to take out the halftone screen (user report, 2026-10-30). The binary carries them. The Ered Mithrin Campaign Expansion's own cards (120165 and 12166–12204) have no `pictures` pin, since neither JvZijp's Drive nor cardgame.tools carries them, so the pack bundles their `scans` file: Hall of Beorn's digital render, about 1020 × 1451, side B included where the card has one. The pins hold the full-size files (about 5 GB); a build machine without the cache (EAS, the release workflow) fetches them and checks each sha256. Do not commit the pictures.

A bundled picture wins over a downloaded file. A release phone build fetches no picture: a card it did not bundle is drawn as its text, so the RingsDB deck import is its only network request (user decision, 2026-11-07; `PRIVACY.md`). A development build keeps the download queue (`File.createDownloadTask` into the document directory, excluded from backup) for a build run without `pnpm scans:pack`; it skips every bundled card. The web build has no bundle and shows the remote file. A card with no picture is drawn as its text.

The inspect screen shows the bundled picture and can zoom it. The table decodes it at the card's on-screen size and releases the full decode. Sniff the magic bytes of any source file, because a URL ending in `.png` often holds a JPEG.

## Persistence and shipping

Open `catalog.db` and `user.db` as two handles, both reachable from screens. Do not nest `SQLiteProvider` and read both through `useSQLiteContext`. That hook returns the nearest provider, and the suspense helper keeps one module-level database and closes the previous file.

`catalog.db` is a Metro asset. `forceOverwrite` defaults to false. Pass true only when the bundled `CATALOG_VERSION` differs from the version stored in `user.db`, or when the stamp of the catalog the phone holds is not this build's version. `CATALOG_VERSION` is a hash of the catalog rows: `pnpm catalog:build` writes it to `src/catalog/version.ts` (a test checks it) and into `catalog.db`'s SQLite `user_version` (folded to a signed 32-bit number), so a build that once shipped a stale `catalog.db` heals on the next one. On first launch the applied version is absent, so the copy runs. `catalog.db` is not committed, so `pnpm start`, `android`, `ios`, `web` and `eas-build-post-install` run `pnpm catalog:build` first: a local build never ships a catalog older than the code.

`user.db` tables: `decks`, `saves`, `standalone_games`, `runs`, `image_files`, `booklets`, `settings`, `meta` (applied catalog version). Drizzle migrations for these are committed and run on open.

- `settings`: one JSON value per key; `table` holds the app's settings: the theme (Dark, Light or System), the UI scale (Small, Medium, Large or Extra large), haptics, the Reduce motion override, keeping the screen awake, animation speed, the combat spotlight, the table's stops, the free Responses always used, the readability options and the first-game guide (user request, 2026-10-28), the card picture quality (High or Low) and whether the fog moves (user request, 2026-10-31); `sections` maps each foldable section's id to whether it is folded.
- `saves.activeScenario` is `{ engineVersion, dataHash, seed, intents }`, replayed through the reducer on load. When the engine version or the data hash differs, or the reducer refuses an intent, drop the in-progress scenario and keep the log, the decks, and the image index. Do not migrate a half-played game during this box. It also carries `startedAt`, when the scenario was dealt (absent on a scenario dealt before `runs` was added), and `activeMs`, the foreground time the table counted (the table's play clock folds it into each write and writes once more when the table is left or the app goes to the background, never for a finished scenario; absent before it was counted).
- `decks.position` is where the player dragged the deck in the Decks tab (0 first): null until a reorder writes every deck's position (`writeDeckOrder`); a deck with none (new, imported, duplicated) leads the list, and saving a deck keeps its position.
- `standalone_games` holds one row per standalone game: the box, the scenario, the seat count, the dealt loadout, the open game as the same `{ engineVersion, dataHash, seed, intents }`, the result, when it was dealt, and when it was archived. It has no log or pool column.
- `runs` holds one row for each scenario that ended, written once and never rewritten: a campaign scenario's win or loss, a standalone game's win or loss; a game abandoned or still open is not a run. It sits beside the campaign log, not in it, so a loss writes a run and no log entry. A row has its kind (`campaign` or `standalone`), the save or game id, the campaign (null for standalone), the box, the scenario, the result, the loss cause the engine reports, the seat count, each seat's heroes, the rounds completed, each seat's final threat, on a win the victory points and the score, on a campaign win the reward chosen, when it started and ended, the foreground time the table counted (`active_ms`, null when it was not counted: History shows it as played time, and the rest of the span as time away), and the engine version. Every number comes from the engine's final state, never typed. It has no foreign key: deleting a save or a game keeps its runs, and History no longer lists them. When `runs` was added, one pass wrote a row for each won scenario on every save's log and each standalone game with a result (a campaign loss was never stored, so none is invented); a field it could not know (the end time, the threats, the engine version, a campaign win's start) is null and the screen leaves it out.

`booklets` is one row per book: id, path, original filename, byte size, sha256, page count, and last page. One file per book lives in the document directory under `booklets/`. Those bytes stay eligible for backup. A catalog update does not delete the row or the file. The picker cache is not the store. Copy the picked file into `booklets/` before recording the row. The ids are `fellowship-of-the-ring` (MEC109), `learn-to-play` (the core-set book), `rules-reference` (MEC101.pdf, pinned as `rr`) `the-dark-of-mirkwood` (`dark_of_mirkwood_rulebook_v2.pdf`, pinned as `tdom`) `dream-chaser` (MEC111, `mec111_dream-chaser_campaign_expansion.pdf`, pinned as `dcce`, 31 MB: the app bundles it, user decision 2026-10-30) and `ered-mithrin` (MEC115, `mec115_rules-web.pdf`, pinned as `emce`, 10 MB: the app bundles it, user decision 2026-11-05). A later campaign adds its own id. A campaign opens the book its data names (`bookletId`): the Fellowship its MEC109 book, Mirkwood Paths the Learn to Play, The Dark of Mirkwood, Dream-chaser and Ered Mithrin each their own rulebook. When the build carries the book and no stored file is there, opening the reader copies the bundled PDF into `booklets/` through the same path as a picked file. A book the player added stays. Gitignore the PDFs.

`catalog.db` tables: `packs`, `cards`, `campaigns`, `meta`. No decks, no saves, no image bytes, no effects (records are code in `src/effects`) and no scenarios (they are `data/scenarios`, read by `src/campaign`). `cards.deck_legal` is true for builder types. `cards.campaign_id` is set only for rows a campaign owns. One `drizzle-orm/sqlite-core` schema in `src/catalog` defines those tables. The script opens it with `better-sqlite3`. The phone opens the same tables with `expo-sqlite`, read-only. RingsDB payloads are cached in a gitignored directory. A re-run with that cache present does not refetch. CI fetches on a miss.

Schema changes to `catalog.db` are a new file, not an in-place migration. Schema changes to `user.db` are migrations.

The release workflow builds the Android APK players sideload from the Releases page, signed with the one release key so each release installs over the last, and an unsigned iOS IPA that SideStore or AltStore signs on the player's device with their own Apple ID, offered as updates through the `source.json` each release carries; with its Apple setup in place it also uploads the iOS build to TestFlight. EAS Build makes the development and e2e builds. A physical iPhone needs Apple signing (SideStore or AltStore, TestFlight, or a build from Xcode). The simulator does not.

Development gotcha: the copy from the asset into the app sandbox runs once per database name. After changing `assets/catalog.db` during development, rebuild it with `pnpm catalog:build`, which changes `CATALOG_VERSION`, or delete the app's storage. Do not leave `forceOverwrite` unconditionally true.

The phone plays with no network: the catalog, the booklets and every card's picture are in the binary.
