# Design system — Carved Stone

Visual authority for chrome. Product rules stay in [brief.md](brief.md). Structure stays in [architecture.md](architecture.md). Every screen uses these tokens and components. Do not invent a palette, a type scale, a radius, or a second styling system.

Carved Stone is the design for every screen, in a Dark and a Light theme. Its values come from the Carved Stone handoff (a README and `.dc.html` mocks for phone, tablet landscape and tablet portrait); where a mock and the handoff's tokens disagree, the tokens win. Where an older phase file or plan names Cinzel, gold, felt or a framed card, this file supersedes it.

## Direction

A dark, gritty, atmospheric game, not a form. The page is near-black stone under layered fog: cold moonlight, pale wisps, and red ember wherever the enemy is. Accents are silver, glows take the sphere's colour, and there are almost no borders. Every screen opens on the picture of what it is about (the scenario's scene, the fellowship's cards, the card's painting); the words support the pictures.

- **The ground and the fog** are the page (Backdrops). Content sits on them. A surface is earned only by a layer that floats over the page (the dock, tray, popover, sheet or `ActionBar`), by a tappable row or tile (a soft wash and a chevron), or by parchment, and it never holds another surface. Surfaces are set off by shadow and a 1 pt lit top edge, never by an outline. List rows are told apart by the space between them, board zones by soft coloured haze.
- **Wraith-light is the one thing to do.** The accent (silver on Dark, elven green on Light) marks the primary, the current step, and what is selected (the silver ring and wash). Beside the one primary, the accent is the word of a secondary or text button and the icon of a glass button.
- **Every control is a capsule and sits flat.** A button, chip, toggle, segmented track and thumb, field, stepper and badge has a radius of half its height and a flat fill: no glow, lit edge, shadow or ring. Containers take 20 pt corners and items 12 (Spacing, radius, border). There are four button styles and no others: Primary, Secondary, Text and Glass (Components) (user instruction, 2026-10-30).
- **The section colour names a section** (`moon`: a grey-green on Dark, an amber on Light): Heroes, Difficulty, Other journeys. It is information, never an action.
- **Red is the enemy**: the threat dial, the engaged area's haze, the attacking enemy's ring, a failure's banner, Short on the quest meter, the Eye of Sauron that marks Nightmare.
- **Parchment is what gets recorded or decided**: the campaign log and every choice sheet on the table are weathered paper with ink, the same on both themes.
- **Card art does the illustrating**, cropped by rule (Card art) and dimmed behind words. Do not draw characters or scenery beyond the fog. The game's printed icons are the exception (Icons).
- **Few words.** A label says what something is, in plain words: no codes, no JSON-ish labels. The collector code shows only on the card screen and as row detail in a pack.
- **Borders that stay** carry meaning: the selected item (silver ring), the attacking or engaged enemy (red ring), the sphere rings on hero portraits, the combat banner's 3 pt red top edge, and the rings of the quest dials.

## Standards

What every screen does. A screen that breaks one is a finding in review.

1. **Art first.** A screen about a scenario opens on its scene; one about a deck on its heroes' cards; one about a card on its picture. A campaign or a game names its fellowship with faces; the hero run on Campaigns and Scenarios shows them as their paintings instead (`HeroTile`), and a run row (Other journeys, Other games, a History card) leads with the first seat's three heroes as small cards (`HeroTrio`, no ring). While a file is missing the art is a dark surface with the words over it: nothing waits on a picture and nothing shows a hole.
2. **One clear next move, in thumb reach.** A screen's primary sits in its `ActionBar` or floating panel at the foot, with the sentence it needs above it. A hub's primary that belongs to one thing (Resume on its hero) stays on that thing, and a hub holds only that one.
3. **Status at a glance.** Progress is a track or a bar (`ProgressBar`, the path's road), counts and stats are numbers over their small-capital words (`StatTile` on a tile where several are the screen's point), a party is faces, a deck is a donut and a curve. Numbers are big and in the title face; their words are small capitals. A sentence says what a number cannot.
4. **Show the decision, fold the rest.** Manage and Import are one quiet control away, and archived runs are in History. Nothing is hidden behind a gesture alone, except the Cards tab's filters, which fold away on a scroll down and come back on a scroll up (Card pool; user decision, 2026-11-19).
5. **Back, the book, the gear and the menu are round glass buttons at the top** (`IconButton`, `NavBar`), each with a spoken label ("Back to Decks", "Open the booklet", "Settings").
6. **Every target is 44 pt.** A smaller visible mark keeps its 44 pt target with a hit slop or a larger pressable.
7. **Feedback in the same frame.** A press darkens a button about 8% (`PRESSED_FILTER`, `surfaces.ts`: its own fill, darker, on Dark and Light alike; a text button, which has no fill, takes a capsule of shade) and the primary also sinks 1 pt; a row and a panel take the pressed fill. Nothing animates on the scroll path, except the fold of the Cards tab's filters, which lifts and fades them and changes no layout (Card pool; user decision, 2026-11-19).
8. **Size classes, not devices.** A layout asks the window's width in logical points (its points over the UI scale), never the device: compact under 600, medium 600 to 839, expanded 840 and up (`sizeClass`, `useSizeClass`, `src/ui/size-class.ts`). See Tablets.
9. **Click, not tap, on the web and the desktop.** The words say Tap for a finger. On the web and the desktop an instruction in the app's own text says Click ("Click a glowing card to play it, or drag it.", "Click again: planning ends"): `src/ui/web/text.canvaskit.tsx` words it on its way to the screen (`click-words.ts`), so the sentences are written once. A screen reader's labels and hints keep the platform's words.

## Performance

Feel outranks decoration. These are exit conditions.

- A list is a FlashList. A row is one pressable, text, at most one small picture and small game icons. Inside a FlashList row nothing casts and nothing is filtered: fills are flat washes (`rowSurface`), no `boxShadow`, no `filter`, no blur. Floating panels, tiles and their shadows belong to a ScrollView. The card pool's and the deck editor's grids are the lists of large pictures, each decoded at its drawn size and recycled by FlashList (`pool/grid.ts`).
- The backdrop is one Skia canvas per screen (`backdrop.native.tsx`; the fog's data and its draw are `src/ui/fog/`, which the combat spotlight's black fog shares), behind the content (Campaigns adds one more over its hero art, `FogVeil` on Dark or `LightVeil` on Light): the fog is Skia `FractalNoise` coloured by a matrix, drawn once per texture into a stitched tile, then repeated and faded by elliptical masks in one draw per layer, with no offscreen layer; a layer whose mask fades out draws only inside its ellipse's bounds (`layerBox`). Only the fog moves (Dark fog, below), the canvas redrawing about 30 times a second, only on the screen in front and never under reduced motion; on Light only the table has fog, so a Light page's canvas is drawn once per size. It never sits inside a list row or scrolls. The web build draws the same scene in CSS (`backdrop.tsx`), each fog texture an SVG `feTurbulence` tile with stitched edges, drawn once into a picture that the backgrounds repeat (`web/baked-tiles.ts`: the filter itself as a background runs again whenever the browser paints a part of a layer), its drift a CSS transform animation of a tile layer one tile wider than its box, inside a still box that holds the layer's mask, blend and opacity, so the drift runs on the compositor and repaints nothing.
- A gradient is a style from `src/ui/surfaces.ts` (`backgroundImage`, which React Native draws natively and react-native-web hands to the browser) over a solid or translucent fill of the same token. Depth is `boxShadow` (RN 0.88 draws inset and multiple shadows); a scene behind words is dimmed with `filter` (`ART_FILTER`, `art.tsx`). The card screen's blurred backdrop is its one blurred image.
- **Motion** has one home: `src/ui/motion-tokens.ts` (`docs/design-system-table.md`, Motion), and honours the Animation speed setting (Quick is ×0.6, Slow ×1.5). Screens add no motion beyond what is listed here; the combat spotlight is the table's one large motion (`docs/design-system-table.md`, Spotlight). A fold is a state change, not a motion: it snaps.
- The first screen paints from SQLite. Marcellus SC and the four Barlow Semi Condensed files are embedded at build time by the `expo-font` config plugin (`app.json`), so there is no runtime font load (the table canvas loads them through Skia, with the system face standing in for the moment it takes). No icon font is loaded at runtime: the game icons are committed SVG and PNG files (Icons).
- The table is one Skia canvas. Card pictures stay on that canvas. Chrome text and buttons are React Native. `src/play` cannot import `src/screens`.

## Color

Values live in `src/screens/kit/palette.js`. `tailwind.config.js` maps them to utilities, and `src/ui/tokens.ts` copies the ones the table draws with (`play-tokens.test.ts` fails when a copy drifts). A screen uses the utility, `palette`, or a surface from `src/ui/surfaces.ts`. It does not write a new hex. The only other colour values are the scene art in `src/screens/art/backdrop-scenes.ts` (the fog, Light's sunlight and rust wash, and the grit) and the combat spotlight's black fog (`src/play/spotlight/fog.ts`). Two themes: **Ringwraiths**, the Dark default, and **Rivendell**, its Light twin. The Theme setting is Dark, Light or System; System follows the phone, and Dark when the phone reports none. The root layout (`src/screens/routes/_layout.tsx`) resolves the theme once, writes the palette as CSS variables with NativeWind `vars()` and sets `colorScheme`, so one class paints both themes. A token name is a role, not a hue: `night` is the ground and `bone` the main text in either theme, so `bone` is dark ink on Rivendell, and `accent` is silver on Ringwraiths and elven green on Rivendell.

**Strong outlines** (Follow system, Always, Off; stored as `highContrast`, where an older build's `true` reads Always and `false` Follow system) draws the effect pills and token counts with stronger outlines. Follow system turns them on while the phone's Increase Contrast (iOS) or High contrast text (Android) is on (`strongOutlines` in `table-settings.ts`, `useStrongOutlines` in `display-prefs.ts`); the web build has neither, so it stays off.

Contrast (`contrast.test.ts` checks every pair in both themes, and the words of a button on its tonal and tinted fills; body text needs 4.5:1, a graphic 3:1). Rivendell meets 4.5 on every text pair. Two Ringwraiths pairs are held at their real values: faint on pressed, 4.35:1 (faint sits on the pressed fill only for the moment of a press), and onThreat on threat, 3.10:1, used only for the large dial numerals. The text of a red pill on the table (the engagement cost, a danger pill, an attack number) is the table-only token `onThreatPill` (`src/ui/tokens.ts`: night on Dark, the pale onThreat on Light), 5.0:1 on threat in both themes. accentLine holds 3:1 against night and raised in both themes.

Colour vision (`colour-vision.test.ts`): the sphere tints against one another and against threat, danger against ok and the glows against the ground must differ by CIEDE2000 10 or more under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia simulation in both themes, or the pair is listed with the non-colour cue that carries it (a sphere icon beside its word, a sentence, a check ring); an entry nothing needs fails (WCAG 1.4.1).

The table's copy of the tokens (`tokens.ts`) carries keys that are not colours of the palette: `lit` (the lit edge), `depth` ("dark" or "light": how surfaces.ts builds depth), `shade` (the shadow colour: black on Dark, a warm brown on Light) `primaryTop`/`primaryMid` (the primary's lit top and middle), and the dock's gold for an answer that reaches past one window: `phase` (its word and armed line on the ground), `phaseTop`/`phaseMid`/`phaseDeep` (the gold primary's fill) and `onPhase` (its word); see Two taps in `docs/design-system-table.md`.

## Surfaces

`src/ui/surfaces.ts` builds every surface from the tokens; screens and the table both read it. While the phone's Reduce Transparency is on (iOS; no setting), `useTableTokens` hands out the same tokens with `veil` solid (`tokensFor`, `tokens.ts`), and every fill drawn at 90% or less (`panelSurface`, `rowSurface`, `glassSurface`, the table's last-move line, the spotlight's HUD pill) is drawn opaque (`fillAlpha`); `floatingSurface`, already 98%, and the peek pill, which is opaque `raised`, do not change. A panel is a fill, a gradient over it and a `boxShadow`; a control is a flat fill with no shadow. Depth differs in kind between the themes: Dark lifts a panel with black shadow and a faint green haze, Light with a warm brown shade.

## Type

Two faces from Google Fonts (OFL), embedded: **Marcellus SC** (`font-display`) for display: screen titles, names, button labels, numbers on dials, tiles and badges, the dock's and the rail's labels, section headings. Its lower case is small capitals, so a name reads as carved capitals while its string stays as written (a screen reader sees "Current", not "CURRENT"); it has one weight. **Barlow Semi Condensed** (`font-body`) at 400, 600, 700 and italic 400 for everything read: body, labels, overlines (bold caps), log notes (italic). React Native has no default family, so every screen and the table's chrome import `Text` and `TextInput` from `src/ui/text.tsx`, which give a text the body face unless it asks for the display one; a weight class (`font-semibold`, `font-bold`) or `italic` picks Barlow's file (one family on both platforms; `app.json` registers it as an Android XML family). The table canvas draws names and small words in Barlow and numbers in Marcellus SC (`table-fonts.native.ts`).

**Title face** (setting, Carved by default): Plain draws every `font-display` text in Barlow Semi Condensed semibold instead of Marcellus SC, the canvas's numbers and the spotlight's included, with the string as written (never uppercased for it). Barlow is narrower than Marcellus SC at every size, so the layouts that count Marcellus's width (`top-bar-layout.ts`, `dock/budget.ts`) hold with it. **Bold Text** (iOS; no setting) draws the body face one Barlow weight heavier, 400 to 600 and 600 to 700 (`faces.ts`; React Native does not bold a custom font itself); Marcellus SC has one weight and the canvas keeps its faces. Under Plain a section heading (`SectionHeading`, the fold header) is drawn 17 / 22 untracked so it stays above the 15 pt semibold row names under it (Carved: 15 / 20, tracked 1), and the rail's phase word is tracked 0.5 instead of 4, since its letters are not capitals. Both are applied once in `Text` and `TextInput` (`text.tsx`); the phone's setting is read from the one accessibility store (`src/ui/accessibility.ts`) through `useSyncExternalStore`.

The sizes are Medium's, written in rem: one rem is 14 pt times the **UI scale** (Small 0.875, Medium 1, Large 1.15, Extra large 1.3; `src/ui/ui-scale.ts`), set once by the root layout through NativeWind. Code that draws a size by hand multiplies by `useUiScale()`.

**The phone's own text size** (iOS Larger Text, Android font size; WCAG 1.4.4, 1.4.10) multiplies React Native text on top of the UI scale, and nothing else: the table canvas draws with Skia and follows the UI scale only. No text opts out with `allowFontScaling`. The list table, the dock, the choice's rows and the card preview hold the phone's text size up to 200% with nothing cut or covered, except the dock on a phone under 400 pt tall, whose buttons at 200% pass the room under the top bar (`ACCESSIBILITY.md`, Known gaps); the drawn canvas follows the UI scale only, so a player who needs larger text plays through Table as a list or the card preview. A cap or a shrink-to-fit stays only where the text fills a slot that other layout counts on, and says so in a comment: the peek pill's two words (36 pt tall, at most 1.3 times), the rail's phase word, the Day and Night disc's word (one line in the 38 pt disc, shrunk to it), the dock's and the rail's tab words and the carousel's round label (one line in a fixed width, shrunk to it), the dock's title and folded line (budgeted lines, the rest behind the "i"), a dense dock's result (one line while the rows are open) and refusal (three lines), cut text whole behind the "i", and its armed line (a slot of the longest caution's lines). A line cap that only kept Medium's rows tidy (a row's name, the primary's label, the tokens line, and the readout's result and the refusal off a dense dock) is `capLines` (`src/ui/ui-scale.ts`): it holds at a font scale of 1 and is lifted above it, so the row grows. Every other text wraps or grows its row. A budget that estimates text (the dock's words window, `dock/budget.ts`, the choice's least room, `trayRoom`, the armed line's room, `armedRoom`, the preview's strip, `previewStrip`) takes the font scale as `text` and counts characters and line heights that much larger; `dock/fit.ts` counts the dock's pinned block from what its components draw in the real column (`dockColumn`, the top bar and the foot) and `dock/text-size-fit.test.ts` pins it, with the reading aids folded behind More only where that takes room back; a segmented switch whose words no longer fit one line wraps its options.

## Spacing, radius, border

Tailwind's 4-point steps. Phone gutters 14 for panels and lists inside the 24 pt page inset, 22 for hero text over art; tablet gutters 32 to 40. Gaps 6 to 8 inside controls, 10 to 14 between cards, 16 to 20 between sections. Minimum target 44 pt at every scale (`min-h-touch`, `touch(s, n)`, `touchSlop`); the primary is 52 tall (44 compact, 46 in the table's command column); a secondary button, a glass capsule and a round glass button are 44.

| Radius | Class | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| capsule (half the height) | `rounded-control`; a `Segmented` track and thumb and a toggle chip compute it in their style instead, since Android drew a class radius square when the fill changed on a pick | Every control: buttons, chips, toggles, segmented tracks and thumbs, fields, steppers, badges, switches, dials, portraits, the round glass button |
| 12 | `rounded-item` | An item in a container: list rows, radio tiles, stat tiles, covers, poster and book tiles |
| 20 | `rounded-container` | A floating container: the popover, phase carousel, reading panels, toast, banners, the quest-advance overlay, a panel |
| 24 | `rounded-sheet`, `rounded-t-sheet` | The top of a parchment sheet |
| `cardCorner` | by hand | A card picture (below) |

`src/ui/radius.ts` writes the same four numbers for a style written by hand (times the UI scale; the control is 999); `tailwind-config.test.ts` fails when they drift. A pressable control takes the control radius, a row, tile or cover the item radius, a floating container the container radius; a shape drawn on the table's canvas is not chrome.

Every other card picture in React Native views takes the card screen's share of its shorter side, 10 on 236, never under 2 (`cardCorner`, `src/ui/card-corner.ts`), clipped to the picture itself (`CardImage`, `src/ui/card-image.tsx`), so a picture whose shape differs from its box is rounded too. The table canvas's cards, backs, piles and their marks take the same corner (about 2.3 pt on a phone's 54 pt card, 4 on a tablet's), so a long press does not go from a round card to a square one; 6 pt is only for shapes that are not cards. Until a picture has loaded it is contained in its box, never cropped.

No hairline borders: a panel, a tile, a row, a chip, an input and a list draw no edge (Direction). A selected thing wears `selectedRingOf`, drawn as a shadow so it moves nothing.

## Backdrops

`<Screen scene="…">` draws one scene behind the content (`backdrop-scenes.ts`), in this order: the ground; on Light, a wash (a pale rust where the enemy is, or the table's deeper parchment); the fog; on Light, the morning light; the vignette (clear in the middle, the ground at the rim); and the grit (fine noise, overlay at 18% on Dark, multiply at 14% on Light). An optional `art` layer (`HeroArt`) sits between the scene and the content. On Dark it is screened onto the scene and fades to black; on Light it is multiplied onto the scene and fades to white (`useArtGround`, `art.tsx`), so it ends on the ground itself, fog and all, with no band where it stops. The card screen's blurred backdrop does the same job with the card's own picture. The briefing's scene (`GroundedScene`) fades out instead: its own alpha falls to nothing at its foot and, in two panes, at its right edge (`sceneMask`), a CSS mask in the browser and a Skia canvas on a phone, where a view's blend does not reach the backdrop's canvas and a fade to black drew as a black line and band.

**Dark fog**: every fog is a bank of three fractal-noise layers in one elliptical mask (`banks`): a soft haze (low frequency, a gentle alpha), the banks (a steep alpha, so they have edges, and three octaves, since that alpha turns finer octaves into grain) and their lit crests (the same noise, paler, steeper and nudged 3 pt up). The moonlight is a pale, sickly green-grey, after Minas Morgul's corrupted moonlight; the enemy's ember is the same banks in red. The fog drifts sideways very slowly: the moon's banks 5 pt a second to the right and its haze 2, the ember 4 and 1.7 to the left, so the layers part a little. The masks stay put, and the table's fog drifts at half speed, under the cards. With Moving fog off every screen's fog holds still: the canvas is drawn once and runs no frame callback.

**Light**: Rivendell in the morning. A sun's glow in the top right corner and three soft beams slanting down from it, both screened over the parchment; on a page the enemy's side takes a pale rust multiplied in (`wash`). Only the table has fog (user decision, 2026-11-10): the Dark banks' noise as a golden morning mist from the fellowship's row down, on parchment multiplied a step deeper there so the mist shows, and as an umber smoke over the encounter row; the beams fall the whole height into the mist. It drifts as the Dark table's does, and Moving fog holds it still.

**A hub over its scene** (`hero`, Campaigns and Scenarios): the picture in three bands, the top falling to the ground behind the head, the middle clear with nothing over it, and from under the title down falling to the ground. Over it, a layer of its own (`heroVeil`): on Dark a bank of fog under the title (`FogVeil`), screened, that falls to black at the art's foot and edge; on Light the morning glow and beams (`LightVeil`) and a warm glow behind the title, drawn outside the art's multiply so they brighten. The `hero` scene keeps its own fog below the picture, so no fog meets the art's foot.

## Screen reader

VoiceOver and TalkBack drive every screen as it is drawn; what only a reader meets is added as accessibility props, never as a change to what is drawn or how a touch acts (user instruction, 2026-11-05: the reader path is separate from ordinary play; the table's own reader path is `docs/design-system-table.md`, Screen reader). The rules every screen follows, which a screen's own section departs from only where it says so:

- **Role, name and state.** Every control has a role (button, radio, checkbox, switch, adjustable, header), a spoken name (its label, or the words inside it) and its state (selected, checked, expanded, disabled). A choice among several is a `radio` in a `radiogroup` with a label (`Segmented`, `RadioTile`, the filter's sphere chips, which say `checked` alone); the state is `accessibilityState`, never ", selected" in the label. A row that picks nothing yet (the Ring-bearer candidates, "Choose Frodo Baggins, The Black Riders") is a button. A stop on the path says its name and where it stands, pressable or not (`road-speech.ts`).
- **A long press is also an action.** A control with an `onLongPress` offers it in the actions menu, named for what it does ("Show card", the same words for every card; "Manage decks"), with the hint "Hold to …", joined after the tap's own hint ("Opens the card. Hold to show card") (`longPressA11y`, `mergeA11yActions`, `src/ui/long-press-action.ts`; `TextButton` and `ListRow` do it themselves). Nothing is reachable by a gesture alone.
- **A sheet takes focus at its title and escapes.** A paper sheet's title is a header and the reader's focus lands on it when the sheet opens or its title changes (`PaperSheet`, `useOpenFocus`); a sheet and a popover close on the escape gesture (`SheetModal`, `PopoverFrame`, `onAccessibilityEscape`), as the scrim and Back do.
- **A picture is labelled or hidden.** A card's picture on its own page says the card's name; a picture beside the words that name it, a backdrop, a fog or a ring is hidden. `CardImage` hides itself unless it is given a label.
- **A group has a label.** A segmented switch, a radio group and a set of chips say what they choose ("Difficulty", "Number of players", "Sphere"). An `Eyebrow` that titles a group of rows or a block of text is a header (`header`).
- **A field has a name.** A text input carries an `accessibilityLabel`; its placeholder is not the name.
- **Nothing is said twice.** A state is spoken once, by its state, not by the label and the state; a word beside a picture that already names it is not repeated by the picture; a toast is an announcement or a live region, not both.
- **Keyboard focus.** What a hardware keyboard focuses wears the focus ring, drawn only while it is focused: an outline 2 pt wide and 2 pt off the control, in accentHigh, or in ink on paper and parchment, where accentHigh would not be seen (`src/ui/focus-ring.ts`; the web and the desktop draw it for a keyboard's focus and not a click's, `src/ui/web/focus-ring.canvaskit.ts`). A control is a `Pressable`, so the web shows the pointer cursor on it. The buttons (`PrimaryButton`, `SecondaryButton`, `TextButton`, `GlassIconButton`), `Segmented` options, popover rows and the sheet and tray `Tile` wear it on every screen; a control built on a raw `Pressable` elsewhere does not wear it yet (the table's other controls, and where the ring is clipped or covered: `docs/design-system-table.md`, Screen reader).

`scripts/checks/a11y.test.ts` (`a11y.ts`) holds these for every raw control in `src/**/*.tsx`: a `Pressable`, `TouchableOpacity` or `Text` with `onPress` and no role or no name, an `onLongPress` with no action, a `TextInput` with no label, and an `Image` or Skia `Canvas` neither labelled nor hidden. A control that is exempt is named in the test with its reason, and an exemption nothing needs fails the test.

## Card art

A player card's bundled picture is its illustration everywhere it appears; a quest card's bundled picture is the picture of a scenario. Every crop is a region of the printed layout, the same on every card of its kind (`src/screens/art/crop.ts`, `cropFrame`, tested): the picture is drawn at the size that makes the region cover the box, centred on the region, never leaving a gap at an edge.

| Region | Of | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PAINTING` | a player card's painting | A hero tile's painting with its name band |
| `COVER` | the heart of the painting, clear of the stat column and the gold border | Deck covers (three side by side, 1 pt of the ground between them, fading into the cover's foot), whose slices can be wider than tall; the Campaigns hero tiles |

## Tablets

Design for the window's width, not for iPad against iPhone. The numbers are in `src/ui/size-class.ts`; `useSizeClass()` gives a screen `{ size, width, height, s, rail, short }`.

| Class | Logical width | Draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| compact | under 600 | Every phone: the dock, full-width columns |
| medium | 600 to 839 | A tablet in portrait: the dock stays; reading screens sit in a 640 column; the portrait layouts (poster tiles three across on Campaigns and four on Scenarios, the deck editor's six-column grid, the path's road at 110 / 160 with its next panel laid out across) |
| expanded | 840 and up | A tablet in landscape: the rail, and two panes where a screen has two jobs (`TwoPane`): the hubs' hero and their other runs (`HubPage` and `HubListPage`, which split only with room), the path's road and its Completed and Next column, Cards' pool and its detail pane, the deck editor's head and its cards, Rules' list and its reading pane, Settings' categories and the open one |

- **Measured in logical points**: the width is the window's over the UI scale (`paneWidths`, `columnsFor`, `READABLE` 640, `WIDE` 1040, `RAIL` 88).
- **Two columns only with room**: a screen that splits in a `wide` scroll does so only when the measured width leaves its main column at least 400 (`split-columns.ts`) and the right column has something in it; else it keeps one readable column. A supporting column is 360 to 440 (`paneWidths("supporting")`).
- **The rail in a short window** (desktop only: a phone never shows a root screen this short, and the phone's tab bar is unchanged). Under 480 pt high (`isShortWindow`) the rail's items shrink from 72 pt to share the height with an 8 pt margin above and below (`compactItemHeight`, `src/screens/nav/rail-fit.ts`, never under 44 pt), icon over a one-line word, so the five tabs and Settings all show with no scroll at the smallest window, 844 by 390 (`src/screens/nav/tab-bar.canvaskit.tsx`).
- **Lists** keep their full width and inset their content to the 640 reading column (`useListGutter`, `list-gutter.ts`: the list measures itself, seeded from the window's width so the first frame is already in the column, and passes `gutter` as `contentContainerStyle`), so the scrollbar stays at the list's edge. A phone gets no style at all.
- **Orientation**: a tablet is a screen whose shorter side is 600 points or more (`useIsTablet`). Phone: portrait on every route except the table (landscape) and the card, booklet, glossary and rule screens. Tablet: any orientation on every route except the table (landscape only).

## Screens

Route files named below, such as `(tabs)/index.tsx` or `rule/[id].tsx`, are under `src/screens/routes/`. The flows, the testIDs and the words a screen reader hears are part of each screen's contract.

### Splash

Black (`#000000`) with the app icon's glowing white ring (`assets/images/splash-icon.png`, 120 wide), so the icon opens into the same picture. No card art.

While the app opens, the ring breathes. The native splash is a still picture, so the root layout draws the same frame over it (`SplashGlow`, `src/screens/kit/splash-glow.tsx`) and hides the native one once that ring has drawn. A blurred copy of the ring behind it comes in over `splashRampIn`, then swells and fades, one `splashBreath` each way, until the databases are open (or fail) and, on a launch that opens on a hub, every hub has mounted behind it (`docs/architecture.md`, Screens), so the first tap on the dock finds its tab ready. Then the halo flares (`splashFlare`) while the black fades over the first screen (`splashFade`), and the overlay is gone. Under reduced motion the halo holds a steady glow and the black goes in one step.

The breath must not stop while the app is busy. On a phone it runs on Reanimated's UI thread. A page has one thread, so the web and the desktop draw the same splash with CSS animations and transitions on opacity and transform, which the browser's compositor runs (`src/screens/kit/splash-glow.canvaskit.tsx`); the exported page shows the still ring until its script has read Reduce motion.

Black, with the Campaigns tab's One Ring (`oneRing`) in white, lit by a soft glow (white close in, `glow` `#BED6BA` further out). The adaptive icon background is `#000000` (`app.json`); the monochrome layer is the ring alone, with no glow. iOS uses `assets/expo.icon` (a black fill under `Assets/ring.png`).

## States

| State | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Empty | A muted sentence that says what is missing. The title stays. |
| Loading | The splash is the startup wait. A later wait shows the title and the word Loading in muted. No spinner package. A picture still loading is a dark surface with its words over it. |
| Error | Danger sentence naming the card, the book, or the failed open. |
| Locked | Faint text, a dark veil or a locked ring, the word Locked. Not pressable. |
| Coming soon | A scenario the app cannot deal yet: faint text, the dark veil, the words Coming soon. Not pressable; it is never the current stop, and nothing begins or resumes it. |
| Current | An outlined diamond or stop with a glow, Next or On the table in the accent, plus the word Current when it is not obvious. |
| Completed | A lit stop or a filled diamond, Won · score in green, the reward names as chips. |
| Selected | The selected ring (`selectedRingOf`) or the selected wash with its bar, plus the word Selected on a radio. |
| Refused | Danger sentence naming the card and the rule. The refused change does not happen. |
| Not ready | A deck that breaks a deckbuilding rule: the rule in danger ("Needs 3 more cards"), with no dot, in the deck list, the editor and the briefing's picker. It saves; the briefing does not offer it. A ready deck shows no status and no dot anywhere, and the Decks tab carries no badge (user instruction, 2026-11-05). |
| Failed test | The word Failed plus the engine's numbers. |
| Not applied | A card with a printed line the app does not apply: the struck ring on the table, "Not applied" with the loss in a list row (Ability not applied, Cannot be played, Text not applied), the lines under the Not applied overline on the card and in the preview, and the count on a deck ("2 cards with text not applied"). Never the word inactive. |
| Armed | A button that sends with nothing picked, after its first tap: the line above the buttons says what the second tap does in the accent, with a 2 pt bar running down (`docs/design-system-table.md`, Two taps), and a haptic tick. The button keeps its size, place and label. A moment with one answer arms nothing. |

An irreversible control (Deal, Confirm, a resolution choice) shows the consequence in a sentence directly above it. On the table no single tap sends an intent: a tap picks and a button confirms, and a button that sends with nothing picked is armed first; only a moment with one legal answer sends on one tap.
