# The Ghost of Framsburg

- **The Ghost of Framsburg** (part 7, 12189; `emce.setup-the-ghost-of-framsburg`, `emce.discover`, `emce.loot`). The campaign card's Setup waives the hero-change penalty, shuffles Ashen Brazier, Gundabad Hunter and Gundabad Stalker into the encounter deck, lets a player search their deck for a unique boon ally, and with Isolde's line has the first player remove 3 treacheries, with Beldis's line 3 non-unique locations; side A also ignores the Deep keyword. A Loot objective (`Loot`, a constant marker; Crypt Key, Old Lantern, Scatha's Teeth, Ashen Brazier) enters play only through Discover, is attached to the location as its guard, and is claimed by the first player once nothing guards it (`ClaimWhenFree`); "the Loot objectives the players control" counts the claimed ones (the pick fields `lootKeyword` and `controlled`). A Discover location prints a Forced effect on `becameActive`; a location the players travel to has the keyword held back to the end of the travel step (`discoverHeld`, `pushDiscover`, as Deep's frame goes under the Responses), so Crypt Key's Response ("before resolving it", `traveled`) adjusts the value first (the flag `discoverValue`, ±1 until the end of the phase, never below 0); the location then hears `discovered`, its amount 1 when a Hazard was revealed (Hidden Staircase reduces each player's threat by X on 0). `ResolveDiscover` (Abandoned Ruins) is the same steps for each player in turn, the player asked looking, choosing the Loot objective and revealing the Hazard; "If they cannot" is the active location having no Discover keyword (or the keyword ignored), and then Abandoned Ruins gains surge and doomed 2 once. X is the tens digit of the first player's threat whoever reads it (`firstPlayerTens`). Fram's Shade gets +1 Threat, Attack and Defense for each Loot objective, and is dealt that many shadow cards, at least 1, wherever it is dealt one (the flag `dealtShadows`, read by `dealOneShadow`: the combat phase and its attack from the staging area). The Cursed Tomb cannot be traveled to with fewer than 3 Loot objectives (`Restrict` travel on the card), and after the travel Fram's Shade is searched for in the deck and discard pile and attacks each player in turn order from where it is: from the staging area, or, already engaged, as an engaged enemy (`orEngaged` on `AttackFromStaging`, which other cards do not use). Stage 1 cannot be defeated with fewer than 1 Loot objective, stage 2 with fewer than 2 (`Restrict` advance), stage 3 without Fram's Shade in the victory display (defeating the last stage wins); while the Tomb is in the victory display Fram's Shade gains victory 6 (`gainsVictory`), so a destroyed Shade goes to the victory display. Gundabad Stalker is optionally engaged only by a player who has a unique enemy engaged with them (`optionalEngageUnique`, the family hook `optionalEngage`); the burdens print no engagement cost, so no engagement check engages them. Gundabad Hunter's When Revealed chooses, the first player answering, a player with a unique enemy engaged (the pick field `engagedWithUnique`) and attacks that player from the staging area; with none it attacks no one. Moonlit Passage in the staging area caps what player card effects reduce a player's threat by at 1 a round (`playerThreatReductionCap`, its own tally `playerThreatLoweredBy`): an encounter card's reduction is neither held nor counted, and two copies in the staging area do not add up (the smallest cap holds, `heldLeast`; each copy says "no more than 1", so the tightest of them is the limit). Haunted Hall's gained Forced effect hears the progress a player card's effect placed on a Dark location in the staging area (`progressPlaced` carries the player whose card effect placed the progress, stamped on the progress step only in a game with a record that reads `eventSeat`); progress the quest or an encounter card placed raises no one's threat. The Resolution discovers the treasures the players control, the first player chooses Gundabad Hunter or Gundabad Stalker for the pool (`ChooseCards`), and `ChooseHazardForPool` shuffles the discard pile into the encounter deck and discards from the top until 2 Hazard cards are (or the deck is empty); the first player answers for the group, two copies of one card are one choice, with a single choice there is no question, a burden joins the pool as a burden and any other card as one more copy among its encounter cards (`b:<id>` and `e:<id>` options). Readings (decided, none put to the user): (1) the Response on Fram's Shade (12189:B:2) is offered while the quest card is stage 1 or 2, the quest card being on its B side in play, and its group limit is `oncePerGameGroup`; the first player chooses of the 3 treasures; the treasure lies in the staging area and Fram's Shade, attached to it as its guard, stays engaged with the player it engaged (`guardWith` with `stayEngaged`, used by `AttachRandomTreasure` alone; the other effects that attach an enemy to an objective still return an engaged enemy to the staging area). Reason: 12189:B:2 attaches the treasure to Fram's Shade and nothing moves the Shade; `rr.guarded`'s staging rule is about a revealed guarded objective. The treasure is free of encounters, and the players claim it, once Fram's Shade leaves play. (2) Every player pays the Action's threat (12189:B:3) before the discard pile is shuffled, as Forked Passage's, and it is offered only with a card in the encounter discard pile. (3) "Return attacking enemy to the staging area" (Crypt Stalker's Shadow) is at once and ends that attack, the enemy no longer being engaged; "after this attack" (Gundabad Stalker) is after it. (4) Slammed Shut's players exhaust a ready character; Terrible Shadows' -1 holds for every character in play now and later this round (a class holder, `rr.lasting-effects`). (5) The Old Lantern's player is chosen by whoever uses it and then looks, takes and bottoms the cards themselves; it is offered only while the active location has a Discover value. (6) The Resolution's "2 Hazard cards" counts every card with the Hazard trait, unique or not: the campaign card names no exception and the Hazard trait is the only test (`discardHazards`), so a unique Hazard card in the encounter deck or discard pile counts toward the 2 and may be chosen for the pool, at most one collection's copies joining it (emce.campaign-pool-encounter). Fram's Shade (unique, Hazard) is in the victory display in any win, which the shuffle does not touch, so it is never discarded there. (7) Fram's Shade destroyed while The Cursed Tomb is not in the victory display goes to the encounter discard pile, not to the victory display (it gains victory 6 only while the Tomb is there); the Action, Searching the Ruins or the Resolution shuffles the pile back, so the players explore the Tomb before they fight the Shade. (8) The words: Discover's Loot choice says how many of the cards looked at are Loot objectives and that the rest, bar the Hazard card picked at random and revealed, go to the discard pile (a Hazard treachery resolves its When Revealed effect and is discarded, since only an enemy or a location is added to the staging area, `emce.discover`; two copies of one card are told as copies); Crypt Key's Response is told before the keyword, with the value before and after each option; the Response and Action of the campaign card say that every player pays, the cards shuffled in and, as a caution that arms the Use button, which players the threat would eliminate; Searching the Ruins' threat and the Crypt Stalker's Shadow arm the raise that eliminates a player or loses the game; the optional engagement says whether the player may engage Gundabad Stalker; a player card that reduces threat says Moonlit Passage's cap and what the round has left (each player's, when its player chooses whose threat goes down); the Resolution names Gundabad Hunter and Gundabad Stalker and the Hazard cards, says the other is not added (`only` on the record, so the words of other Resolutions' burden choices are unchanged) and, two-handed, that the first player chooses; a Hazard burden already earned in the Resolution is said to add nothing.
