# Mirkwood Paths and The Dark of Mirkwood

The rulings, readings and table pieces of Mirkwood Paths and The Dark of Mirkwood, scenario by scenario. Product rules, the Domain and the Core round are in `docs/brief.md`; the authority order is in `.claude/rules/authority.md`.

Two more official campaigns are built from the Revised Core Set (FFG product MEC101, RingsDB pack `RevCore`) and The Dark of Mirkwood (RingsDB pack `TDoM`). The Dark of Mirkwood rulebook (`tdom`, pinned in `data/sources.json`; `tdom.campaign-mode`, `tdom.mirkwood-paths`, `tdom.mini-campaign`) says how they fit together. Their data is built and the app knows more than one campaign: the campaign list shows one New playthrough tile for each campaign `data/campaigns/index.json` marks playable (`new-<campaignId>`), which opens that campaign's setup screen and its Start (`start-<id>`; `start-fellowship` for the Fellowship), the Scenarios tab one section for each box, and each save's progress line, path, log and booklet read their own campaign. A campaign is marked playable in the index once one of its scenarios is in `PLAYABLE_SCENARIOS`.

| Campaign id | Name | Scenarios, in order | Booklet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `mirkwood-paths` | Mirkwood Paths | `passage-through-mirkwood` (part 1), `journey-along-the-anduin` (2), `escape-from-dol-guldur` (3), `the-oath` (4), `the-caves-of-nibin-dum` (5) | `learn-to-play` (core campaign pages 30 and 31) |
| `the-dark-of-mirkwood` | The Dark of Mirkwood | `the-oath`, `the-caves-of-nibin-dum`, as a two-scenario mini campaign on a blank Campaign Log | `the-dark-of-mirkwood` |

The two campaigns share the scenario files of The Oath and The Caves of Nibin-Dûm. Neither is a saga: `saga` is false in their campaign files, so no Ring-bearer, no Fellowship-sphere hero and no prelude. Each campaign file names its booklet (`bookletId`) and, where the booklet prints a scenario's setup paragraph, its page (`bookletPages`; Mirkwood Paths has them for the three core scenarios, whose paragraphs are on Learn to Play p25, `ltp.setup-passage-through-mirkwood` and its two siblings).

Data rules, each with its reason in `data/overrides.json` or the code:

- The cards come from Hall of Beorn alone for these boxes, by `CardSet` ("Revised Core Set", "The Dark of Mirkwood"). Nightmare sets are different `CardSet`s, built as their own pack (Nightmare, below). Hall of Beorn's " (Campaign)" encounter-set suffix is not part of the set's name.
- The Revised Core Set reprints the 2011 core encounter cards under the same codes (01074 to 01131); the `RevCore` printing owns them. The Dark of Mirkwood's Hall of Beorn codes (020NN) are The Hunt for Gollum's, so its code is `410` and the two-digit collector number (Stalking Goblin 41041, as RingsDB lists it).
- Hall of Beorn's per-scenario counts lead. The quest card's printed Setup (from the scan-read ledger `data/printed/mirkwood-paths.json`) moves cards out of the encounter deck: searched for and staged, or set aside. DragnCards' decks (the 2011 core Q01/E01 and the Limited Collector's Edition Q00/E00 of The Oath and The Caves) are a cross-check: it compares each card's copy count (Normal and Easy), not which pile the copy is in. The Easy Mode sheet (`easy-mode.core-set`) lists the cards Easy removes from each Core Set encounter set and settles the Revised Core scenarios' Easy counts.
- The Learn to Play calls the second core scenario's set "Journey Down the Anduin"; the cards print "Journey Along the Anduin". The card wins; the booklet name is an alias (`encounterSetAliases`).

## Passage Through Mirkwood

Mirkwood Paths part 1, campaign card 01129. The Mirkwood Paths campaign deals it first, through the Campaign Setup Checklist with no saga setup (no Ring-bearer, no One Ring, no prelude); a standalone game has no campaign card and no Mendor. Every printed line of every dealt card has a record (`src/effects/authored/passage-through-mirkwood.ts`, `src/effects/authored/pool/RevCore.ts`); no widget, zone or counter is added to the table.

- Quest, stage 2B (01120:B:1, Forced): "proceed to one of the 2 'A Chosen Path' stages, at random". The quest's definition names the branch (`BranchStage`), and defeating stage 2 reveals one of 01121 and 01122 at random from the scenario's seed; the other leaves the game (`removed`). A stage a branch leads to is the last. The quest remembers the stages it reached for the Resolution (`stagesReached`, kept only by a quest that branches). Stage 3B "Don't Leave the Path!" (01121) prints 0 quest points: it cannot be advanced, each player searches the encounter deck and discard pile for a Spider and adds it to the staging area, and the players win when Ungoliant's Spawn is defeated. Stage 3B "Beorn's Path" (01122, 10 quest points) cannot be defeated while Ungoliant's Spawn is in play, and defeating it wins the game (`rr.winning-the-game`). Stage 1A (01119:A:1) searches for a Forest Spider and an Old Forest Road, the scenario's staging piles.
- Mendor (01135, objective ally with printed stats): 01129 puts him into play under the first player; the first player always controls him (the token passing moves him); a controlled objective ally with printed combat stats is an ally, so every "character you control" effect and every pick of an ally or a character reaches him (Hummerhorns, The Necromancer's Reach, Mendor's Support). The same holds for any controlled objective ally with printed combat stats, Gildor Inglorion (141077) too: an engine decision without a pinned rules span, tested in `passage-through-mirkwood/encounter.test.ts`. If he leaves play the players lose the game (01129:A:3; the loss names Mendor) and he is removed from the game (01135:A:4). "After a quest card is defeated" (01135:A:3, a Response) is heard when a stage of the main quest or a side quest is defeated, not when the last stage wins the game: Mendor readies and each player draws a card. While Enchanted Stream is the active location no player draws (01095:A:1), Mendor's draws included.
- Mendor's Support (01132): one copy a round (`PlayOnlyIf`); with no Mendor in the game it does nothing. Resolution (01129:B:1, only after a won scenario in Campaign Mode): each player earns one copy (so the pool holds one for each seat, two-handed or solo), and the burden of the stage 3B the quest reached: Ungoliant's Swarm (01136) after Beorn's Path (01122), Lingering Venom (01137) after "Don't Leave the Path!" (01121). The next scenario's checklist shuffles each Mendor's Support into its player's deck and the burden into the encounter deck.
- Ungoliant's Swarm (01136): the player it engages chooses and exhausts 2 characters he controls (ready ones); as a Shadow card it comes into play engaged with the defending player and is not discarded. Lingering Venom (01137): each player, first player first, chooses between exhausting each damaged character he controls and 2 damage on the hero he controls with the most damage (a tie is his to settle; with no damage every hero ties); as a Shadow card it exhausts each damaged character the defending player controls.
- A Travel cost of cards needs the cards: Great Forest Web (01077:A:1) has each player exhaust a hero (the players cannot travel there while one has none ready), Necromancer's Pass (01094:A:1) has the first player discard 2 cards at random (not with fewer), Mountains of Mirkwood (01078:A:1) reveals the top card of the encounter deck into the staging area (`rr.travel`). Explored, Mountains of Mirkwood lets each player search the top 5 cards of his deck for a card and add it to his hand; the rest is shuffled into the deck. Old Forest Road (01099:A:1) and Forest Gate (01100:A:1) are Responses of the first player to traveling there; Old Forest Road offers nothing when no character he controls is exhausted.
- Caught in a Web (01080:A:1): the player with the highest threat chooses one of his heroes and it is attached there (the first player chooses among the heroes of the players tied for the highest); it counts as a Condition attachment. Its hero does not ready in the refresh phase unless the player pays 2 resources from that hero's pool: at the start of the refresh phase, each such hero with 2 or more resources asks its controller once (pay, or keep it exhausted); with fewer there is nothing to ask. A second copy cannot go on a hero that already has one (the engine's rule for every attachment an encounter card attaches to itself).
- The revealed treachery waits in the staging area while it resolves, so "the staging area" in Driven by Shadow (01092:A:1) is the other cards there: with none, it gains surge. Its +1 Threat goes on every enemy and location in the staging area until the end of the phase. King Spider (01074:A:1) exhausts a ready character of each player, Black Forest Bats (01098:A:1) removes a committed character of each player from the quest (it stays exhausted), Eyes of the Forest (01079:A:1) discards every event card in each hand, Ungoliant's Spawn (01076:A:1) gives each committed character -1 Willpower until the end of the phase, Dol Guldur Orcs (01089:A:1) lets the first player choose the committed character that takes 2 damage, Hummerhorns (01075:A:1) deals 5 damage to one hero of the player it engages.
- Shadow lines that read "undefended" (01074, 01075, 01076, 01089, 01092, 01097): the first number is for a defended attack, the second for an undefended one, in one record each. Chieftan Ufthak (01090; the card prints "Chieftan", the ledger's lines read "Chieftain"; read against the scan, Hall of Beorn's "Chieftain" and "get" are transcription differences) gets +2 Attack for each resource token on him and a token after each of his attacks. Dol Guldur Beastmaster (01091:A:1) deals itself an additional shadow card when it attacks.
- Nightmare Ungoliant's Spawn (900102:A:1): the -2 Willpower and the discard at 0 both last until the end of the phase on the characters committed when it resolves (`discardAtZeroWillpower`, swept at each settle): a character whose Willpower falls to 0 later in the phase is discarded too. It is an encounter card's lasting effect, recalculated as the game changes (`faq.lasting-effects`), and the same words on the Redhorn Gate Snowstorm's Shadow are read this way (`docs/scenarios/dwarrowdelf.md`). `recommended`: no FAQ, Hall of Beorn ruling (https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Ungoliant's-Spawn-PTMN lists none) or forum thread rules on the wording; searches of the FAQ 1.8 and 1.7 texts, the Unofficial Official Rulings thread and the web found nothing. Alternative: the discard is checked once, when the effect resolves.
- Easy removals come from the Easy Mode sheet and the printed marks (`data/scenarios/passage-through-mirkwood.json`).

## Journey Along the Anduin

Campaign card 01130. It is the second part of Mirkwood Paths, dealt through the Campaign Setup Checklist with no saga setup: Passage Through Mirkwood's earned pool reaches it (each player's Mendor's Support is shuffled into their deck, the burden into the encounter deck). A standalone game has no campaign card, no Mendor, no Valor and no Scarred. Every printed line of every dealt card has a record (`src/effects/authored/journey-along-the-anduin.ts`, Valor and Scarred in `src/effects/authored/pool/RevCore.ts`); no widget, zone or counter is added to the table.

- Setup (01126:A:1, "Each player reveals 1 card from the top of the encounter deck, and adds it to the staging area"): the first quest card's Setup record runs after the deck is shuffled, in a frame of its own, one reveal for each player in turn (`mec109.setup`, `rr.reveal`; a card with Surge reveals one more). The stage then turns to side B and its When Revealed resolves (01126:B:1): the stage searches the encounter deck and discard pile (the engine's search) for 1 Hill Troll, unless one is in play, adds it to the staging area and shuffles. Stage 1B cannot be defeated while any Hill Troll is in play (01126:B:2). The start of the game runs the first stage's When Revealed for every scenario.
- Stage 2B (01127:B:1): the staging step reveals 1 additional card (the table holds `revealMore` while the stage is current) and no engagement checks are made in the encounter phase (the table holds `noEncounterEngagementChecks`, a bar limited to the encounter phase, where Daybreak's and the Safe locations' `noEngagementChecks` holds in every phase; an enemy's Ambush check in the quest phase is still made, but one in the encounter phase is not, as the stage says "during the encounter phase" and Ambush is an engagement check, `dwd.ambush`; `recommended`: no official or forum source rules on it; read https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR?EncounterSet=Journey+Down+the+Anduin, https://lotrlcgblog.wordpress.com/2018/04/23/journey-along-the-anduin-quest/, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2020/11/29/journey-along-the-anduin-2/); each player may still optionally engage 1 enemy. Stage 3B (01128:B:1 to 3): 2 encounter cards for each player are revealed into the staging area when it is revealed; the staging step of the quest phase is skipped (`skipStaging`, held while the stage is current, "for the remainder of the game"); the players win once no enemy is in play (a constant `WinGame`, read when nothing is resolving, so the reveals arrive first). Stage 3B prints 0 quest points and is won no other way, so progress cannot advance it (a `Restrict advance`, as 01121:B:2 in Passage Through Mirkwood).
- Hill Troll (01082:A:1): the combat damage its attack deals beyond the remaining hit points of the character it damages (damage dealt beyond `hit points - damage already there`, after Toughness) raises its controller's threat by that much; the damage itself still goes on the character (`excessToThreat`, `src/rules/families/defense/excess.ts`; every place an enemy attack places damage on a character goes through it; only damage that was placed counts, so a character that takes no damage or takes life instead has no excess). Damage a card later cancels does not call the threat back.
- Wolf Rider (01081:A:2, Shadow, ledger: "Shadow: Wolf Rider attacks the defending player. That player may declare 1 character as a defender. Deal Wolf Rider its own Shadow card. After combat, return Wolf Rider to the top of the encounter deck."): the shadow card attacks the defending player. One attack is in progress at a time, so it attacks as soon as the attack it was dealt to has ended, not inside the shadow step (an engine decision: no rules span orders an attack inside a shadow step): it comes into play engaged with the defending player, is dealt a shadow card of its own (`rr.attacks-outside-the-combat-phase`, `faq.enemy-attacks-outside-combat`), and the player may declare a defender. "After combat" is read plainly: Wolf Rider stays engaged until the end of the combat phase (a Delay to it, `rr.step-6-11`), and then, if it is still in play, returns to the top of the encounter deck; a Wolf Rider the players defeat first goes where a defeated enemy goes, and its shadow card is discarded with it (`ltp.shadow-discard`). It counts as having attacked this phase (it makes no second attack) and, as an attack an effect made, uses up no attack of its own. Two Wolf Riders dealt to one attack (Massing at Night) attack one after the other, each after the "after" instructions of the one before. Its defense sheet says "Wolf Rider's shadow effect: it attacks you now, and goes back to the top of the encounter deck after combat."
- Wargs (01085:A:1): when they attacked and were dealt a shadow card with no shadow effect, they return to the staging area after the attack (the condition `shadowBlank`; a card dealt none is not blank). Goblin Sniper (01083:A:1): while it is in the staging area with another enemy there, in the encounter phase, it cannot be optionally engaged; at the end of the combat phase in the staging area, each player deals 1 damage to 1 character he controls (01083:A:2). Marsh Adder (01084:A:1) raises the attacked player's threat by 1 each time it attacks. Despair (01086) removes 4 progress from the quest, or all it has; its Shadow sets the defending character's Defense to 0 for the attack. Misty Mountain Goblins (01111) remove 1 progress after attacking, and 1 (3 when undefended) as a Shadow. Massing at Night (01112): each player reveals 1 additional card (X additional cards, X the number of players); its Shadow deals the attacker 1 shadow card for each player. Eastern Crows (01115): shuffled back into the encounter deck when defeated; Pursued by Shadow (01117) raises each player's threat by 1 for each character he controls that is not committed; its Shadow returns an exhausted ally (a player's choice: Mendor is one) to its owner's hand, or raises the defender's threat by 3 with none. Evil Storm (01116) and Treacherous Fog (01118) read the player's threat from 35 up.
- Locations: Banks of the Anduin (01113:A:1) goes to the top of the encounter deck, not the discard pile, when it leaves play (explored too). The Brown Lands (01087:A:1) takes 1 progress when it becomes the active location by traveling (the engine's `becameActive`, which an effect that makes a location active also fires). The East Bight (01088:A:1): while there is no active location the players must travel to it, may not decline and may not travel elsewhere, if they can pay its (empty) cost (`Restrict travelOnly`). Gladden Fields (01114:A:1): each player raises threat by 1 more at the end of the refresh phase while it is the active location. Enchanted Stream (01095) is Passage Through Mirkwood's record.
- Campaign card 01130, Campaign Mode only. Setup: Mendor is put into play under the first player (as 01129), and a copy of Valor (01133) and of Scarred (01138) for each player is set aside (`SetAsideEachPlayer`; "each copy" is read as one for each player, since each trigger gives each player one). Response (01130:A:3): after a Hill Troll is defeated, each player attaches 1 set-aside Valor to a hero he controls, if able (the first player's Response, optional like any; a hero that already carries a Valor is not offered: Limit 1 per hero). Forced (01130:A:4): after a character is destroyed by a Hill Troll's attack (the combat damage of its attack; announced as the attack ends, so a lethal wound a Response could still cancel is settled first), each player attaches 1 set-aside Scarred to a hero he controls, once per game (a Forced effect with a limit spends it when it fires, `oncePerGame`). If Mendor leaves play the players lose the game. Valor (01133:A:2): Permanent; after its hero is declared as an attacker, the player may exhaust Valor to heal 1 damage from the hero and deal 1 damage to the defending enemy. Scarred (01138:A:2): Permanent; after a character you control is destroyed, you raise your threat by 1.
- Resolution (01130:B:1, a won scenario only): every Valor and Scarred attached to a hero in play is added to the Campaign Pool on that hero (one entry for each copy: two heroes of one player each keep theirs), and the next scenario's checklist attaches each to its hero again (step 5; a fallen hero's copies leave the pool). The hero in play with the most damage is taken prisoner, a tie settled at random with the scenario's seed (the pick is part of the replayed game), and the log's `prisoner` fact holds it: the Campaign Log screen shows a Prisoner row, and the Resolution screen's last sentence names the prisoner ("Confirming records Valor on Aragorn in the campaign and unlocks Escape from Dol Guldur. Aragorn, the hero with the most damage, is taken prisoner."; earned cards are listed with commas and an "and" only before the last). A hero that fell is not a candidate. A loss writes nothing.
- Nightmare Troll Attack (900208:A:2): the Shadow resolves the same attack against each player: each player not engaged with the Troll names a hero, which takes the Attack the Troll struck with (the attack notes it at the damage step) as combat damage of the Troll, an undefended attack's (`StrikeUndefended`). No second shadow card is dealt, as the card says "this attack"; `recommended`: Vision of the Palantir's Nightmare Journey Along the Anduin review confirms the attack is made against each other player and undefended for one not engaged with the Troll, but says nothing of the shadow card or of which hero takes it, and no FAQ, forum or Hall of Beorn page rules on either; read https://visionofthepalantir.com/2019/05/18/nightmare-journey-along-the-anduin, https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/The-Lower-Depths (no related ruling), the FFG LotR FAQ (searched through search results; its PDF address returned 404).
- Easy removals come from the Easy Mode sheet and the printed marks (`data/scenarios/journey-along-the-anduin.json`).

## Escape from Dol Guldur

Campaign card 01131. It is the third part of Mirkwood Paths, dealt through the Campaign Setup Checklist with no saga setup: Journey Along the Anduin's pool reaches it (each Valor and Scarred on the hero that carries it, attached again at step 5) and so does its `prisoner` fact. The campaign card's Setup puts Mendor into play (`PutObjectiveAllyIntoPlay`) and the first player chooses the hero that gets Appointed by Fate (01134, `AttachChosen`, asked on the briefing like any campaign choice; the card is not added to the pool). A standalone game has no campaign card, no Mendor and no Appointed by Fate, and its prisoner is chosen at random. Every printed line of every dealt card has a record (`src/effects/authored/escape-from-dol-guldur.ts`; Appointed by Fate and the pool Mendor in `src/effects/authored/pool/RevCore.ts`). Its families are `objectives`, `prisoner` and `dungeon` (`src/catalog/families`, `src/rules/families`).

- Guarded (`rr.guarded`, `ltp.guarded`; 01108 to 01110:A:1): an objective with the Guarded keyword gets the next card of the encounter deck attached when it is revealed (`GuardObjective`, a When Revealed record on the objective) and in Setup (01123:A:1: after the encounter deck is shuffled, each of the 3 objectives in the staging area gets one, run beside the quest card's other Setup). The next card that is not itself an objective guards. When the next card is an objective that also has the Guarded keyword, the card after it guards the first objective, and then the second objective is revealed and its own Guarded keyword takes the card after that (`rr.guarded`: "use the next card of the encounter deck to fulfill the original keyword effect, and then resolve the guarded keyword on the second card"). An objective without the keyword stays on top of the deck. A revealed enemy or location is placed in the staging area attached to the objective (`CardInstance.guard`): it counts its Threat there, is engaged, travelled to and attacked like any other, and is unlinked from the objective when it leaves play (defeated, explored, discarded). A treachery resolves as it is revealed and the objective gets no attachment (`ltp.guarded`; `rr.guarded` says the same in other words: dealt with when its effects resolve). The Setup also turns the Nazgûl faceup, out of play (`TurnFaceUp`, 01123:A:1).
- Claiming (01108 to 01110:A:2): an Action any player may use on an objective in the staging area that is free of encounters (no card attached; the condition `freeOfEncounters`). Its cost raises the acting player's threat by 2; they choose a hero they control and `ClaimObjective` attaches the objective to it (zone `attached`, controlled by that player, `claimed`; "Counts as an attachment" is a `CountsAs` record). Each is Restricted (`rr.restricted`): a hero holds two, so a third claim on one hero has its controller choose which Restricted attachment returns. These three print "If detached, return ... to the staging area" (the flag `returnsToStagingArea`), so a detached one returns to the staging area, controlled by nobody: its hero leaves play, a card discards it (Cavern Guardian's Shadow: "Discarded objective cards are returned to the staging area", 01103:A:2) or a Restricted slot is over. A claimed objective that prints no such return (Athelas 02063, the Clues of Rhudaur 10110, 10112, 10114) is an attachment: it is discarded with its hero (`rr.attachment-cards`) to the encounter discard pile, as the Gate Key (12071:A:1) is the only other card that prints its return (the semi-official FAQ 1.23, "Any objective card that attaches to another card is treated as an attachment in addition to its other card types", and 1.17, which returns only a guarded objective to the staging area, https://alongextendedparty.com/semi-official-errata-faq-1-10/; community, agent recommendation, not user-confirmed). `claimed` is announced about the objective (01131:B:1).
- The claimed objectives' own lines: Gandalf's Map (01108:A:3) stops its hero attacking and defending; Dungeon Torch (01109:A:3) raises the attached hero's controller's threat by 2 at the end of each round (`RaiseThreat` with `who: hostController`); Shadow Key (01110:A:3) puts 1 damage on its hero at the end of each round. An objective in the staging area does neither.
- Dungeon Jailor (01101:A:1): after the players quest unsuccessfully it shuffles 1 unclaimed objective of the first player's choice from the staging area into the encounter deck (its guard is discarded with it: an attached card leaves play with its host). The failed quest is announced once for each seat, so the record is limited to once a round: the Forced effect is one effect, whatever the number of players.
- Prisoner (`prisoner` family; 01123:B:1, 01124:B:1, 01131:A:3 and B:1):
  - The stage's When Revealed turns 1 hero chosen at random among all the heroes the players control (the game's seed) facedown (`TakePrisoner`). It and each card attached to it go to the captive zone the Ring-bearer under the Watcher uses (`south/captive.ts`), flagged `prisoner`: still in play, controlled by nobody, blank (`textBlank`), out of its player's heroes (so it is not picked, not damaged, collects no resources and no player attacks, defends or quests with it). The table draws it facedown in its player's play area, with Mendor under it.
  - Campaign Mode: the campaign card's Forced effect (01131:A:3) answers `prisonerTaken`: the hero the Campaign Log names (`prisoner` fact, Journey Along the Anduin's Resolution) is the prisoner instead when the players control it (`SelectLoggedPrisoner`: the random one comes back as it was; with no such hero the random one stays), the cards attached to it are turned facedown only in a campaign game ("Each card attached to that hero is turned facedown" is the campaign card's line 01131:A:3; the stage's 01123:B:1 turns the hero alone, so a standalone game keeps them faceup), Mendor is captured with it (`CaptureWithPrisoner`: facedown under the prisoner, still in play, blank) and the card flips to side B.
  - Its Response (01131:B:1) after the first objective is claimed (the condition: it is the only claimed objective) frees Mendor faceup with 1 damage under the first player (`FreeCaptive`, once a game).
  - The rescue is stage 2B's Response after any amount of progress is placed on it (`progressPlaced`, 01124:B:1): `RescuePrisoner` turns the hero faceup with 1 damage on it among its player's heroes, its attached cards faceup in that player's play area, and announces `prisonerRescued`. The Nazgûl of Dol Guldur, set aside faceup, hears it from out of play (the only trigger a set-aside card hears; `rr.in-play-and-out-of-play`) and moves into the staging area (01102:A:2).
- Stage rules: 1B (01123:B:2 and 3) and 2B (01124:B:2 and 3): the players, as a group, play at most 1 ally card each round (`LimitPlays`: the cards the reducer recorded as played this round, whichever seat played them; allies put into play are not played), and the stage cannot advance without a claimed objective (1B) or while a hero is the prisoner or with fewer than all 3 objectives claimed (2B; the condition `prisoner`). Stage 3B (01125:B:1 and 2): at the beginning of each quest phase each player's top deck card becomes an Orc Guard; it cannot be defeated while Nazgûl of Dol Guldur is in play, and the last stage's defeat is the win.
- Orc Guard (01107:A:1 after travelling to Tower Gate, 01125:B:1): `PlaceOrcGuard` takes the top card of a player's deck out of the deck, facedown, held under an enemy stand-in the engine makes (`orc-guard`, printed.ts: Attack 1, Defense 1, 1 hit point, no Threat, engaged with that player as if it had just engaged them). It attacks, is dealt shadow cards, counts as an engaged enemy, and when it leaves play (defeated or otherwise) the card under it goes faceup to its owner's discard pile and the stand-in leaves the game. The table names it "Orc Guard"; no card art is needed. A Forced effect "After travelling to ~" resolves before the travel's Responses (`rr.forced`): every real travel announces `traveled` to Forced effects as well.
- Iron Shackles (01105:A:1): attached to the top of the first player's deck, in play, controlled by nobody (`AttachToDeck`, `Seat.deckAttached`; the table draws it under the deck). The next time a player would draw 1 or more cards from that deck the draw does not happen and the card is discarded to the encounter discard pile instead: one draw action (Draw of any number of cards, the resource phase, DrawEach) replaced as a whole, one Shackles per draw action (a draw that does not happen, an eliminated player's or one a card forbids, discards nothing; cards drawn for a destroyed enemy are one draw action too: one check, in `drawMany`). Its Shadow effect resolves the When Revealed effect of the card. "Counts as a Condition attachment" is a record for the ledger line; nothing reads it for a card attached to a deck.
- Nazgûl of Dol Guldur (01102): no attachment can be played on it (`cannotHaveAttachments`); after a shadow effect dealt to it resolves (announced as `shadowEffectResolved` once the attack's shadow cards have resolved, before damage; a shadow card with no effect is not announced) the engaged player chooses and discards 1 character he controls (a destroy, so a seat with no hero left is eliminated). Under the Shadow (01104:A:1): until the end of the phase the staging area's total Threat is raised by the number of players (`RaiseStagingThreat`, the table's `moreStagingThreat`; `lessStagingThreat` still subtracts); its Shadow raises the defending player's threat by the number of enemies engaged with them, Orc Guards included.

## The Oath

Mirkwood Paths part 4 and The Dark of Mirkwood part 1, campaign card 41050. The Oath is dealt through the Campaign Setup Checklist with no saga setup, in two campaigns that share its scenario file: in Mirkwood Paths it continues the log Escape from Dol Guldur wrote (`tdom.mirkwood-paths`: the pool on its heroes, each Mendor's Support in its owner's deck, the burdens in the encounter deck, all through the usual steps), and a new `the-dark-of-mirkwood` playthrough starts it on a blank log (`tdom.mini-campaign`), taking in a finished campaign's carry-over (Mendor, Mendor's Support) when the player chooses. A standalone game has no campaign card, no Adaptation and no Stalking Goblin. Every printed line of every dealt card has a record (`src/effects/authored/the-oath.ts`; the Adaptations, Stalking Goblin and Guilty Conscience in `src/effects/authored/pool/TDoM.ts`; the Adaptations' "Setup. Attach to a hero." is the generated setup template, which reads the period as well as the colon).

- Campaign card, Setup (41050:A:2, Campaign Mode only): each seat in turn chooses 1 Adaptation boon (41044 The Fervor of Lore, 41045 The Might of Tactics, 41046 The Power of Spirit, 41047 The Secret of Leadership; the box holds one of each, so a seat cannot take another's) and a hero of its own to carry it (`ChooseCards`, asked on the briefing like any campaign choice). An Adaptation is not Permanent, so the log does not record its hero: the pool keeps it as the seat's player-backed card, and a later scenario's checklist shuffles it into that seat's deck, where its Setup line finds it and attaches it to a hero. Stalking Goblin (41041) is shuffled into the encounter deck now and added to the pool (`AddToPool`), so steps 7 and 8 do not deal it again this scenario; later scenarios shuffle it into the encounter deck from the pool. A lost scenario writes none of this.
- The Adaptations: the attached hero gains the Lore, Tactics, Spirit or Leadership resource icon (41044:A:2 to 41047:A:2). Each Action is "Limit once per campaign. Record in Campaign Log when used." (the limit `oncePerCampaign`): using it adds the card's code to the game's copy of the log's `usedOnce` fact, a won scenario writes that back, and a scenario that starts with the code in `usedOnce` does not offer the Action (a lost one writes nothing, so its uses do not stay). 41044: discard 3 cards from your hand (you choose them) to heal all damage from a hero; 41045: deal 1 damage to the attached hero to deal 3 damage to an enemy engaged with you; 41046: exhaust the attached hero to place 3 progress on a location; 41047: spend 1 resource from the attached hero's pool to ready a hero. An Action is offered only when its cost and its target can be had, and a used Adaptation's preview says "its Action is used for this campaign". A standalone game cannot hold an Adaptation.
- Campaign card, Response (41050:A:3): the ledger reads "Response: At the end of each round, place 1 resource token here" (the scan; Hall of Beorn's "Forced" is a transcription difference and RingsDB has no row for the card: confirmed against the scan). So it is optional: after the Forced effects of the end of the round, the first player is offered it, once a round, and may pass (`PlaceToken`; the tokens are the card's own, not a hero's pool). This is a round-end Response window (`endOfRound` heard by Responses); no other card hears it. The top bar shows the tokens ("Tokens", `campaign-tokens`) while the campaign card is in the game and has such a record. Resolution (41050:B:1, a won scenario in Campaign Mode only): with 6 or more tokens (the `tokens` condition reads the card's tokens) the Guilty Conscience burden is earned. The card prints no "record the tokens" line, so the log records no count (the log's `counters` field is unused by this card). The end-of-round Response sheet is headed "End of round" and its note says what the tokens lead to ("6 or more tokens at the end earn Guilty Conscience.", read from the record). A won game whose Resolution asks nothing (this one) docks "See the Resolution", not "Choose rewards"; the Resolution sentence also names the Adaptations it records as used this campaign.
- Guilty Conscience (41043, Surge, burden treachery): When Revealed it is attached to the hero with the highest printed threat cost among the heroes of every player (the first player chooses among those tied: `AttachSelf` `highestThreatCost`); it counts as an Adaptation attachment. Forced: at the end of each round its hero's controller raises threat by 2. Action: the hero's controller spends 2 resources from the hero's pool to discard it (an encounter card attached to a hero is used by that hero's controller).
- Stalking Goblin (41041:A:1): after it engages a player the encounter deck, then the discard pile, then "your collection" are searched for a Goblin Sniper (by title: Anduin's 01083 counts too) and it is added to the staging area, and the encounter deck is shuffled. The app tracks no collection, so the collection holds every card the game knows: a copy is made when neither the deck nor the pile has one (`SearchCollection`). As a Shadow card it is added to the staging area. Its stats are the scan's, not Hall of Beorn's: Threat 2 and engagement cost 10, Attack 2, Defense 2, 2 hit points (an override in `data/overrides.json`, which may set a card's `threat` and `engagementCost`: Hall of Beorn files the burden enemy's Threat icon as Willpower and has no engagement cost, so the engagement check could not engage it). Ungoliant's Swarm (01136) has the same gap and its own override (scan: Threat 3, engagement cost 33, Attack 2, Defense 2, 5 hit points).
- Quest. Stage 1 (41001:A:1) puts The Eaves of Mirkwood in the staging area and sets Goblin Troop aside (the scenario's piles deal it; the record describes it). Stage 2B (41002:B:1, B:2): each player finds a Forest location; the stage cannot be defeated unless a Goblin Trail is in the victory display. Stage 3 (41003:A:1, B:1): the first player adds the set-aside Goblin Troop and each other player (the condition `firstPlayer`, negated) finds a Goblin enemy; the stage cannot be defeated while Goblin Troop is in play, and defeating it wins the game. Goblin Trail's Response (41014:A:2, "place 6 progress on the current quest") puts the progress on the stage that is current when it resolves. Advancing is immediate and the new stage's When Revealed is part of it (rr.quest-advancement, rr.triggering-condition), so when the progress that explores the Trail also fills stage 2, stage 3's When Revealed (Goblin Troop into play) resolves before the Trail's Response and the 6 progress cannot clear stage 3 while Goblin Troop is set aside. The engine does this for an explored location whose Response puts progress on the quest (a record with `ProgressQuest` on `leftPlay`); every other explored location keeps offering its Responses before the stages it advanced to reveal, as Fellowship, Passage and Anduin games replay them.
- Locations and enemies: Forest Gate's Response (41012:A:1, The Dark of Mirkwood) reads "the first player may draw 2 cards" on the scan, which Hall of Beorn and DragnCards drop to "draws" on that printing only (the Core and Revised Core printings read "may draw" on both); Tangled Grove's Threat is the number of locations in the staging area (41011:A:1) and each player discards a card at random to travel there (the players cannot travel there while one has no card); Spider Den's Quest Action (41013:A:1) is any player's: search the deck and discard pile for a Spider enemy, it engages that player (`SearchMove` `engaged`: it enters the player's play area, so Forced effects that hear an engagement hear it), and Spider Den is discarded; Goblin Trail's Travel reveals a card (as Mountains of Mirkwood); Abandoned Camp's Travel discards the top encounter card and adds it to the staging area if it is an enemy, and its Response discards the first player's top card and puts it into play if it is an ally; The Eaves of Mirkwood, while the active location, keeps any Response that cancels a When Revealed or Shadow effect (`CancelEffect`) and any paid cancel from being offered (the table flag `encounterUncancelable`); the window stays open for a Response that does not cancel (Round Shield's Defense, 19118:response:1); Goblin Sniper, Caught in a Web, Driven by Shadow and Forest Gate read as their Anduin and Passage twins do. Goblins are Upon You! cannot be canceled while it resolves.
- Other encounter lines: Goblintown Scavengers discards the top card of each player's deck and its Threat rises by each printed cost for the phase (an empty deck adds nothing); Surprising Speed returns an engaged enemy of each player and, if none was returned, gains surge and doomed 1; Eyes in the Dark has each player choose between raising threat by 1 for each questing character and discarding one.
- Table (new pieces, each created by a line above): the campaign card's tokens on the top bar (41050:A:3); an Adaptation whose Action is used says so in its preview (41044:A:3); the Campaign Log shows "Used this campaign" (the cards whose once-per-campaign Action was used) once a scenario is won, and the path's Pool line and the Log's Boons row mark a spent Adaptation "· used" (41044:A:3). ## The Caves of Nibin-Dûm

Mirkwood Paths part 5 and The Dark of Mirkwood part 2, campaign card 41051: the last part of both campaigns, so a win completes each. `tdom.setup-the-caves-of-nibin-dum`. It is dealt through the Campaign Setup Checklist with no saga setup, on the log The Oath wrote (the Adaptations in the seats' decks, Stalking Goblin and Guilty Conscience in the encounter deck, the once-per-campaign uses in the game's facts) or, in The Dark of Mirkwood, on the log holding only The Oath. A standalone game has no campaign card, no "No Time to Waste!", no Shiny Distraction and no Ranger Sense, but the first quest card's own Setup still deals the Cave Torch. Every printed line of every dealt card has a record (`src/effects/authored/the-caves-of-nibin-dum.ts`; 41042, 41048 and 41049 in `src/effects/authored/pool/TDoM.ts`; the Goblins cards 41019 to 41026 are The Oath's). Its vocabulary is the `caves` family (`src/catalog/families/caves.ts`, `src/rules/families/caves/`).

- Campaign card, Setup (41051:A:2, Campaign Mode only): each player adds a "No Time to Waste!" to his hand once his starting hand and mulligan are done (`AddToHandAfterMulligan`), each copy joins the Campaign Pool, and Shiny Distraction is shuffled into the encounter deck and joins the pool. The scan's first line, "You are playing Campaign Mode", is inert.
- Cave Torch (41029:A:1): the Setup's attach follows `rr.restricted` like any attach. A hero that already holds 2 Restricted attachments discards one of the three at once, the choice of the hero's controller (`docs/brief.md`, Keywords implemented once).
- Campaign card, Resolution (41051:B:1): the final threat of the player with the highest threat (the condition `highestThreat`: every seat of the game, an eliminated player's threat raised to 50 by rr.scoring, so it never earns). Under 40, each player earns a Ranger Sense now (it joins the pool and is shuffled into his deck by the next checklist) and a Ranger Sense for each player is carried over to the next campaign (`CarryOver`, `eachPlayer`); at 40 or more nothing is earned or kept. A lost scenario writes nothing. The Resolution sentence on the confirm screen says the win completes the campaign when the scenario is the last of its campaign, and names the Ranger Sense kept (`settledSentence`).
- Quest. Stage 1 (41004:A:1): Goblin Chieftain and Cracked Pillar are set aside by the scenario's piles; the Cave Torch comes out of the encounter deck (the `attachedChosen` pile) and the first player attaches it to one of his heroes, dealt by the Setup record (`AttachSetAside`, run at the start of the game with the stage's When Revealed). Stage 1B: each player finds a different location. Stage 2 (41005): Goblin Chieftain joins the staging area and each player finds an enemy in the deck or discard pile; in the encounter phase every enemy's engagement cost is 0 (the table flag `engagementCostZero`, read by `modifiedStat`); the stage's quest points are 4 for each enemy in play. It prints 0 quest points, so **a stage that arrives with 0 quest points is not met on arrival**: its When Revealed has not resolved yet, and the check comes with the next progress (`advanceStage`; the two other stages that print 0, 01121 and 01128, are held back by their own Restrict). Stage 3 (41006): Goblin Chieftain is set aside again (`SetAsideInPlay`) and Cracked Pillar joins the staging area; one more card is revealed each quest phase; no progress goes on the Pillar in the staging area (`noProgressPlaced`); the stage prints no quest points and advances when the Pillar leaves play explored. Stage 4 (41007): Goblin Chieftain returns; with 8 progress on the stage it loses "Cannot take damage" (`losesNoDamage`); the stage cannot be defeated while it is in play, and defeating it wins.
- Goblin Chieftain (41027): Threat, Attack and Defense are the stage number (the amount `stage`: the card's place in the quest deck, 1 to 4); it cannot take damage or have attachments; after it attacks the first player chooses to remove X progress from the quest or return it to the staging area. Policy note for the golden: it only stays engaged to be killed when the stage keeps 8 progress after X come off.
- Cracked Pillar (41028): in the staging area the first player may attack it in the combat phase as if it were engaged with him, its Threat as its Defense (`attackableAsEngaged`, read by `canAttack`); it has no hit points, so damage only piles up on it; 4 damage are removed to travel there (the travel cost check reads it). The players' attack sheet says "enemy or location" when it is among the options.
- Cave Torch (41029): Action: exhaust it to place 1 to 3 progress on a Dark location ("up to 3": placing none is no use, so the choice starts at 1); Forced: after it exhausts, the top encounter card is discarded and an enemy goes to the staging area. Its exhaust is the Action's first instruction, not a paid cost; the Forced effect hears it either way, since every exhaustion (an exhaust cost, a commit to the quest, an attack, a defense) is announced to "After ~ exhausts" (`src/rules/windows/exhaust.ts`, rr.exhausted). Cavern Warg's Response, Lightless Passage's Travel cost and Lost in the Dark exhaust it the same way; Lost in the Dark is the choice of the player who controls the Torch (nobody chooses when nobody does).
- Encounter cards: Great Cave-troll's excess combat damage removes progress from the quest (`excessToQuestProgress`); Cavern Warg's Shadow returns it to the staging area after an attack that destroyed a character; Goblin Tunnels (in the staging area) takes a progress token off the quest when a Goblin is revealed from the deck; Lightless Passage's Shadow cancels the combat damage dealt to the attacker (`combatDamageCanceled`); Branching Paths adds 1 Threat to each Dark location in the staging area, and when explored the players choose 1 of the top 3 encounter cards to reveal, the others going to the bottom (`BottomOtherLooked`); Collapsed Mine limits progress on the quest to 4 each round (`questProgressCap`, the smallest cap held) and its Shadow raises the defender's threat by the damage the attack dealt (the damage is kept on the attack only while a Shadow effect waits to read it); Goblin Dungeon's explored Response puts an ally from the top 5 cards of the first player's deck into play; Crumbling Stairs takes no progress in the staging area and, explored, discards the top encounter card and puts a location into play as the active location; Crumbling Ruin has each player exhaust a character and discard a card, and discard the character when the card's printed cost reaches its remaining hit points; Lost in the Dark may stop progress on the quest for the phase (`noQuestProgress`, a lasting modifier the table holds: `ModifyStat` with `target: "table"`; the active location still takes it); Watchful Eyes counts as a Condition attachment and reveals a card at the end of the combat phase when its hero is exhausted.
- A finished campaign's path keeps its action bar: it says the campaign is won ("Ered Mithrin won. The players win the campaign!": the name and the campaign card's printed Resolution line, the name alone when the card prints none) with a Start a new campaign button that opens the Campaigns tab scrolled to New playthrough (`start=1`), where a campaign's setup screen offers the carry-over, and when the log holds a carry-over it says what waits for the next campaign ("A Ranger Sense for each player waits for your next campaign."). The campaign list, the history card and the save line call the finished playthrough "Campaign won". The Resolution screen of the last scenario shows the printed line under the title in heading type with "Ered Mithrin is complete: 9 of 9 scenarios won." under it, and labels Confirm "Finish the campaign". The Resolution sentence names the campaign it completes ("...and completes The Dark of Mirkwood.").
- Known readings and gaps. Lightless Passage's Shadow (41033:A:2) is recorded with the literal duration `endOfAttack` (rr.lasting-effects: a Shadow effect lasts until the end of the attack): an enemy attack ends before any character attacks that enemy in the combat phase, so the flag has expired when the attacker could take combat damage, the Shadow does nothing in practice, and only a unit test (`the-caves-of-nibin-dum/torch.test.ts`) shows the flag. Collapsed Mine's cap (41035:A:1) counts the progress that reaches the quest card: the card says "placed on the quest", and rr.active-location says progress that would be placed on the quest is instead placed on the active location, so the active location is outside the cap; this is the engine's reading of those two spans, not a ruling. The round's progress is noted from its start in a game whose records read a cap (`trackQuestPlaced`, set by `syncConstants`; `noteQuestPlaced` in `src/rules/families/caves/progress.ts`), so progress placed on the quest before Collapsed Mine entered the staging area counts against it.
- Shiny Distraction (41042, Surge, Doomed 1): each player may add 1 resource to each of his heroes' pools and then raises his threat by 5.
- "No Time to Waste!" (41048) and Ranger Sense (41049): "When playing this card, choose one" is the existing choice among an event's Actions on different lines; the first Action of each removes the card from the game and from the Campaign Pool (`RemoveSourceFromGame`, `RemoveFromCampaignPool`: the log drops that seat's own entry when the scenario is won), the second draws a card. "No Time to Waste!" is a Quest Action (threat down by 3 for each player, an encounter card revealed).
- Table (new pieces, each created by a line above): none that needs a widget. The Pillar among the attack choices (41028:A:1) and the words of its choices (`src/play/words/scenarios/caves.ts`) are its only pieces under `src/play`; the quest card's title reads the quest points the engine reads (41005:B:2).
- Easy removals come from the Easy Mode sheet and the printed marks (`data/scenarios/the-caves-of-nibin-dum.json`).
