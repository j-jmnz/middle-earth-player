# Standalone scenarios

The rulings, readings and table pieces of Standalone scenarios, scenario by scenario. Product rules, the Domain and the Core round are in `docs/brief.md`; the authority order is in `.claude/rules/authority.md`.

The official scenarios printed on their own, outside a cycle or a saga (RingsDB's cycle 50 packs that are not A Long-extended Party's). The cycle is `kind: "cycle"` (`data/campaigns/standalone-scenarios.json`), built one scenario at a time: each is one product line in `scripts/data/campaign-defs.ts`, and the cycle's tile counts the scenarios built so far. Like Shadows of Mirkwood (above) it has no campaign card, no booklet and no Campaign Mode, so every game of it is a standalone game (Standalone play). `src/campaign/playable.ts` says which scenarios are playable; every scenario of the cycle is built. The Scenarios tab shows the cycle as one fold named "Standalone scenarios", its scenarios in release order ("Standalone scenarios · Scenario 1").

## The Massing at Osgiliath

The 2011 event scenario (pack `TMaO`, Hall of Beorn lists it under the year `2011`), Normal and Easy. No Nightmare deck exists for it: the Nightmare sets of the Nightmare source name Assault on Osgiliath, a different scenario (`scripts/data/nightmare-defs.ts`). Its cards have codes `00001` to `00019` (Hall of Beorn's own numbering, which every later standalone pack repeats: The Battle of Lake-town and The Stone of Erech take a `productCodes` rule, below).

- **Data.** The piles are DragnCards' deck Q99.01, checked against the quest card's printed Setup (`00016:A:1`). DragnCards has no Easy deck for it and Hall of Beorn's Easy counts equal the Normal ones, so Easy loads the same deck (community: Easy and Normal both deal the 40 encounter cards, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/09/01/the-massing-at-osgiliath/; no official source says otherwise). The 12 Scout cards (4 each of Snaga Scouts, Wolves from Mordor and Wainriders) are the staged ones, one copy of each title per player, the rest staying in the encounter deck (`data/overrides.json`, `scenarioPiles`); The Witch-king is set aside. The scenario has no rulesheet, so no `setupSpan`: the encounter set is Hall of Beorn's, checked against the load list, and the quest card prints its own rules.
- **Readings.** No official ruling or `faq.*` span covers the scenario (searched: the FFG forum archive, Hall of Beorn's card pages, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/09/01/the-massing-at-osgiliath/, https://ratdorg.blogspot.com/2011/09/lotr-lcg-massing-at-osgiliath-review.html). The records are `src/effects/authored/standalone-scenarios/the-massing-at-osgiliath.ts`; each reading below is marked as `.claude/rules/authority.md` (Open points) asks.
  - "The players have crossed the Anduin" is stage 4, Race to Minas Tirith (`00019:B:2`, literal): the condition `onStage` 4, read live by every card that says it (`00006:A:1`, `00006:A:2`, `00007:A:1`, `00005:A:1`, `00010:A:1`, `00011:A:1`, `00014:A:2`).
  - Snaga Scouts (`00002:A:1`): at the beginning of the encounter phase every copy in play, in the staging area or engaged with another player, engages the player with the lowest threat; the first player chooses once among tied players (`EngageLowestThreat`). Agent recommendation (no ruling found); the alternative, only staged copies, is not what "all copies" says.
  - Wainriders (`00004:A:1`): each point of damage its attack places on a character, after cancels and shields, raises the defending player's threat by 1; the defending player is the one the attack is against even when another player's Sentinel defends (`rr.defending-player`, span-settled), an undefended attack counts, a shadow card's damage does not (agent recommendation for the amount; no ruling found).
  - Wolves from Mordor (`00003:A:1`): any character, hero or ally, destroyed by damage from its own attack; it is shuffled into the encounter deck from play. A character a shadow effect destroys is not destroyed by its attack's damage (agent recommendation; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/09/01/the-massing-at-osgiliath/ leaves the case open).
  - Wainrider Captain (`00005:A:1`): "the top Scout enemy" is the most recently discarded enemy with the Scout trait in the encounter discard pile (the top 2 once crossed, fewer when fewer are there); it goes to the staging area and is not revealed, so no When Revealed, surge or Doomed applies (agent recommendation; the card text and the blog agree on "top"). `00005:A:2`: the +2 is for the attacking enemy, only when the attack is undefended (literal).
  - Uruk Vanguard (`00006:A:2`) and Captured Watchtower (`00007:A:1`): "+2 instead" is a total of +2, not +1 and +2; the numbers are read live (literal). Captured Watchtower's Shadow (`00007:A:2`) works as Rockslide's (`02092:A:2`): the defenders are removed from combat, stay exhausted and take no damage, and the attack is undefended (literal).
  - Emyn Arnen Overlook (`00008:A:1`): while it is in play, staging or active, the first Scout enemy revealed from the encounter deck each round, by the staging step, an effect or surge, gains surge and Doomed 2, which adds to a printed Doomed (`rr.keywords`, span-settled). A card moved by Wainrider Captain or the Witch-king added from the set-aside area is not revealed (literal).
  - Morgulduin (`00009:A:1`): while it is the active location, each character that commits to the quest takes 1 damage, the Ranger of Ithilien that enters committed included; the character discarded to commit on stage 3 is not committing (agent recommendation; no ruling found, https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Morgulduin-TMaO). `00009:A:2`: only before the players cross, the active location returns to the staging area with its progress and the shadow card itself becomes the active location; after crossing it does nothing (literal, `rr.active-location`).
  - Pelennor Fields (`00010:A:1`): once crossed, with a copy in the staging area and no active location, declining to travel or traveling to a location with another title raises each player's threat by 3; traveling to a location named Pelennor Fields costs nothing. Raising the threat satisfies the "must", as Streets of Umbar's resources do (`TravelOrRaiseThreat`); two copies are charged once. Agent recommendation (one unconfirmed forum answer, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/76075-two-massing-at-osgiliath-questions-pelennor-fields-and-ranger-of-ithilien, says traveling to one copy still costs 3 for the other).
  - Cut Off (`00012:A:2`, `00012:A:3`): the When Revealed makes each player discard every ally in his hand, none if he holds none. The Shadow's discard option is offered only when the defending player's hand holds that many ally cards (1, or 2 when the attack is undefended), otherwise only the +3 Attack is left (agent recommendation; no ruling found, `rr.cost` says a cost must be performable).
  - Dark Pursuit (`00013:A:1`): the total Threat of the staging area is raised, until the end of the phase, by 1 for each Scout enemy in play, in the staging area or engaged, counted live (an encounter card's lasting effect is recalculated, `faq.lasting-effects`, span-settled); with no Scout enemy in play when it resolves it gains surge.
  - Massing at Osgiliath (`00014:A:2`): each card the encounter deck reveals for the rest of the phase, surge reveals included and the card itself excluded, gains Doomed 1 (3 once crossed), recalculated as each is revealed (`faq.lasting-effects`) and stacking with a printed Doomed (`rr.keywords`); a shadow card dealt is not revealed (span-settled).
  - Ranger of Ithilien (`00015:A:1`): the first player takes control of it exhausted and, in the quest phase, committed (its Willpower counts and Morgulduin hears it); revealed in another phase it only enters exhausted; then it gains surge. Researched, community only (https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/75616-massing-at-osgilliath-3b, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/76075-two-massing-at-osgiliath-questions-pelennor-fields-and-ranger-of-ithilien). `00015:A:2`: 2 damage to the attacking enemy always; then the defending player may exhaust a ready character he controls to take control of it, which enters play ready under him and stops being the enemy's shadow card, otherwise it is discarded like any shadow card (agent recommendation; no ruling found, https://ratdorg.blogspot.com/2011/09/lotr-lcg-massing-at-osgiliath-review.html describes it).
  - Ruins of Osgiliath (`00011:A:1`): +3 Threat until the players have crossed (literal). The Witch-king (`00001:A:1` to `A:3`): players cannot play attachments on him, which bars player card attachments only (`noPlayerAttachments`, as Nightmare Stuart's Trolls), so an encounter card may still attach; -1 Willpower to each character while he is in the staging area; after any attack he makes, defended or not, the defending player raises his threat by 3 (even if that eliminates him) or he returns to the staging area; he leaves the staging area only by an engagement check or an optional engagement (literal; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/09/01/the-massing-at-osgiliath/ agrees).
  - Through the Ruins (`00017:B:2`): every card a player plays from his hand, ally, attachment or event, and every card an effect puts into play from his hand counts toward his 1 for the round; cards from the deck or the discard pile do not, and a card put into play by an event counts besides the event (`LimitHandPlaysPerRound`). Agent recommendation (no ruling; https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/165046-vilya and the blog say Vilya and A Very Good Tale get around it).
  - Anduin Crossing (`00018:B:1`): progress from card effects, a player card's or an encounter card's, cannot go on the quest card or the active location; the progress a successful quest places is not an effect and goes on the active location first, and a location in the staging area still takes effect progress (literal). `00018:B:3`: each player, before committing any character, chooses a hero or a Ranger of Ithilien he controls and discards it (the owner's discard pile or the encounter discard pile; a hero leaving play does not lower threat), or commits nothing and pays nothing; one card a player a round (researched, community only, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/59608-anduin-crossing; "once per round" is an agent recommendation). The engine asks at the beginning of the quest phase, which is the same moment for the player and lets the choice windows serve it (agent recommendation).
  - Travel bars (`00016:B:1`, `00017:B:1`, `00018:B:2`, `00019:B:2`): West Bank locations on stages 1 and 2, East Bank and West Bank on stage 3, East Bank on stage 4 (literal). Race to Minas Tirith (`00019:B:1`, `B:3`): the set-aside Witch-king joins the staging area and is not revealed; defeating the stage wins the game.

## The Battle of Lake-town

The 2012 event scenario (pack `TBoLT`, Hall of Beorn lists it under the year `2012`), Normal and Easy. No Nightmare deck exists for it: the Nightmare source has no set for it (`scripts/data/nightmare-defs.ts`), Hall of Beorn lists no Nightmare card set for it, and its quest cards' Nightmare column only repeats the Normal and Easy "1" (https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Scenarios/The-Battle-of-Lake-town). Only the quest cards and the encounter set exist; it has no rulesheet (no rules insert is pinned); the Smaug deck and Burn X are on no card and come from the Quest Companion, a community source (Readings, below).

- **Data.** Hall of Beorn numbers its 24 cards `00001` to `00024`, which are the Massing's `00001` to `00019` and RingsDB lists no card of it, so `overrides.productCodes` gives them `502` and the two-digit collector number: `50201` to `50203` the quest stages, `50204` to `50206` the three Smaug cards, `50207` Lake-town, `50208` to `50224` the encounter cards (50 is RingsDB's cycle 50, 2 the pack's position in it; a later standalone pack that collides takes 50 and its own position). The Massing keeps its codes. The piles are DragnCards' deck Q99.02 (its pack is spelled "Lake-Town", `overrides.dragnPackAliases`); it has no Easy deck, and Hall of Beorn's Easy counts equal the Normal ones, so Easy loads the same deck (community: 36 encounter cards in both modes, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/06/26/the-battle-of-lake-town/; no official source says otherwise). The encounter deck is the 36 cards of the set, Lake-town (`50207`) is the one staged card (`overrides.scenarioPiles`) and the 9 Smaug cards (3 copies of each title) are the separate Smaug deck (`smaugDeck`, with its own discard pile `smaugDiscard`: `scripts/data/normalize/setup-standalone-scenarios.ts` reads the Setup step, `overrides.scenarioPiles` moves the three cards from the DragnCards set-aside group), which `50201:A:1` shuffles together. Neither pile is touched by an encounter deck or discard effect.
- **Ledger.** The 27 printed sides (`data/printed/standalone-scenarios.json`) are read from the scans, not from Hall of Beorn's text, which differs on Smaug the Mighty ("shodow" for the printed "shadow"), Merchant's District ("to Lake-town" for the printed "on Lake-town") and a few commas and capitals.
- **Readings.** The Smaug deck and Burn X are not printed on any card: they come from the Quest Companion (http://www.lotr-lcg-quest-companion.gamersdungeon.net/home.html, Keywords: Burn X; Rulings: The Smaug Deck), a community source, corroborated by FFG's designer blog (https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/72798-the-battle-of-lake-town, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/75400-the-battle-of-laketown), https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/80112-the-battle-for-laketown-rules, https://talesfromthecards.wordpress.com/2013/07/11/surviving-the-battle-of-lake-town/ and https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/06/26/the-battle-of-lake-town/. No `faq.*` span or rules span covers the scenario. The records are `src/effects/authored/standalone-scenarios/the-battle-of-lake-town.ts`, the vocabulary `src/catalog/families/smaug.ts`; each reading below is marked as `.claude/rules/authority.md` (Open points) asks.
  - Smaug deck (`50201:A:1`; researched, Quest Companion): the 9 Smaug cards are shuffled into a separate deck with its own discard pile; Lake-town is staged by the data and each player reveals 1 card in Setup (an ordinary reveal: surge, Doomed and When Revealed apply). Smaug is not in play until round 1. At the beginning of every quest phase, round 1 included and before the commitments, the first player chooses the play area or the staging area blind, then reveals the top Smaug card and puts it there; in the play area he engages it at once, which is not an optional engagement (https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/184264-engaging-smaug-lake-town-quest), and a copy put there while one is already engaged engages again (https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/314017-battle-of-lake-town-and-reengaging-smaug). A Smaug already in play gives the new one ALL its damage as the new one is revealed, before its When Revealed, and goes to the Smaug discard pile; an empty deck is shuffled from its discard pile (`families/smaug/deck.ts`). All three are immune to player card effects, have 20 hit points and are defeated only at 0 hit points left.
  - Damage caps (`50201:B:1`, `50202:B:2`; literal, carry-over recommended): "cannot have more than 6 / 12 damage" blocks placing more (the excess of an attack is lost); damage already on him stays when the cap drops (stage 3 back to 2B), and the carried-over damage of a Smaug swap is moved, not placed. Smaug cannot be defeated before stage 3 (20 > 12). `50201:B:2`: 1 damage to Lake-town for each player (literal).
  - Smaug the Mighty (`50204:A:2`; literal): an immediate attack against each player in turn from the first player, whether he entered the staging area or engaged the first player, each dealt a shadow card (`faq.enemy-attacks-outside-combat`, span-settled); he still attacks the first player in the combat phase if engaged (no ruling asks it).
  - Smaug the Magnificent (`50206:A:2`; researched, community, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/137485-smaug-the-magnificent-lake-town): "excess damage" is the damage beyond what the defending character can still take; it goes on Lake-town and the character still takes damage up to its hit points. `50206:A:3`: after the Smaug swap's damage transfer, up to 3 damage moves from him to Lake-town.
  - Lake-town (`50207:A:1`; literal): 50 or more damage on it loses the game at once (`loseAtDamage`), and its damage has no cap. Damage on Lake-town is placed as tokens (`PlaceDamageOn`), not dealt.
  - Burn X (`50205:A:2`, `50210:A:1`, `50221:A:2`, `50214:A:2`, `50210:A:2`; researched, Quest Companion only, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/06/26/the-battle-of-lake-town/ agrees): "resolve all burn damage" adds up the Burn X of every location in play (staging area or an active slot, not the victory display), counted when it resolves; the numeric Burn stacks with West Pier's grant (`rr.keywords`, span-settled). Each player in turn from the first player may exhaust any number of ready characters he controls and assign any amount of the pool to them, each up to its remaining hit points (exact lethal allowed); the rest goes on Lake-town. The damage on characters is an encounter card effect's (agent recommendation; no source). Great House's own Burn 3 counts when it resolves its own When Revealed, because a revealed location is in the staging area before its When Revealed (`rr.staging-area`; agent recommendation for the order, no ruling). West Pier (`50215:A:1`, literal): each location in play, itself included, gains Burn 1 while it is in play.
  - Fire and Water (`50202:B:2`; literal and the blog): after a successful quest, the first player chooses how many of the progress tokens that would be placed on the quest card, after the active location took its share, are discarded to remove as much damage from Lake-town (at most the damage there); the rest goes on the quest. The Dragon's Wrath (`50203:B:1`, literal): 1 additional card in the staging step, not 1 per player. `50203:B:2` (researched, community, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/84877-lake-town-questions/page/1/): the Refresh Action is optional; it returns the players to stage 2B with no progress and without resolving a When Revealed again, and Smaug keeps his damage. `50203:B:3` (literal): the stage has no quest points and is defeated only by defeating Smaug; then the players win if the Victory points in the victory display are strictly more than the damage on Lake-town, else they lose (a tie loses).
  - Close to the Flame and Scorching Blast (`50209:A:2`, `50210:A:2`; literal, offer order recommended): any player may discard a hero he controls to cancel the Shadow effect, offered from the first player on, one cancel enough; the cost must be performable (`rr.cost`); a hero leaving play does not lower threat. `50209:A:1`: each hero and ally of every player, exhausted or not.
  - Town-Baiting (`50211:A:1`; researched, community, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/301263-the-battle-of-lake-town-questions, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/79514-town-baiting-threachery-question-battle-of-the-lake-town, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/136485-smaug-town-baiting-and-esgaroth-wharf-battle-of-lake-town, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/282317-lake-town-questions): with no Smaug in play (a Setup reveal) it does nothing; otherwise Smaug, in the staging area or engaged, attacks Lake-town with a shadow card (`faq.enemy-attacks-outside-combat`); the last player (the last in turn order from the first player, the only player in solo) may declare one defender, with none the attack is undefended and all its damage goes on Lake-town (so Merchant's District's Shadow deals 4); a defender takes damage up to its hit points and the excess goes on Lake-town. The effect cannot be canceled. The defending player for a shadow's text (The Master's Manor) is the last player: agent recommendation, no source.
  - Dire Magnificence (`50212:A:1`, `A:2`; literal, `faq.next-player`, span-settled): each player discards an ally he chooses if he controls one, 2 damage on Lake-town for each who cannot; the Shadow engages the next player and attacks at once, with one player there is none. Catching Fire (`50213:A:1`; researched, https://talesfromthecards.wordpress.com/2013/07/11/surviving-the-battle-of-lake-town/): the first player chooses the Pier location from the victory display that is shuffled into the encounter deck (it leaves the display); with none it gains surge. `50213:A:2`: -2 Defense to the defending character, nothing when undefended.
  - Reckless in His Rage (`50214:A:1`; literal, `faq.lasting-effects`, span-settled): only with Smaug in play, +2 Threat, +2 Attack, -2 Defense and allies cannot be declared as defenders against him until the end of the round, recalculated live; it binds the Town-Baiting attack too.
  - Locations (agent recommendation unless marked). South Pier (`50217:A:2`; researched, community, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/282317-lake-town-questions): while it is in play, in the staging area or active, the players cannot travel to any other location, Fisherman's Dock's second slot included; a Travel Action is not traveling and stays allowed. North Pier (`50218:A:2`): while in play no damage can be placed on Smaug (attacks, shadows, effects); the carry-over of a Smaug swap is not damage placed and still moves (agent recommendation, no source). Merchant's District (`50219:A:2`; literal, timing researched, the same thread): a Travel Action taken in the action window after the travel option, several allowed besides normal travel, costing 2 damage per player on Lake-town, always payable, and the location goes to the victory display from the staging area or the active location; `50219:A:3`: 1 damage on Lake-town, 4 if the attack is undefended. East Pier (`50220:A:2`; literal): while in play, in the staging area or active, no "When Revealed" effect of any card can be canceled (the blog's "while staged" is narrower than the card). Great Bridge (`50222:A:2`; literal): each player discards the top X cards of his deck, X the damage on Lake-town when it resolves; surge only if no card at all was discarded. Fisherman's Dock (`50223:A:2`; researched, official for the progress split, FFG's Caleb Grace, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/73401-the-battle-of-lake-town-question-spoilers-for-one-of-the-cards-text, and community, the same thread as South Pier): with exactly 1 active location, exhausting a hero (its cost) lets the players travel here although one is active, giving 2 active locations; with none active nothing is exhausted; it is still the round's one travel; progress from questing is split freely between the two, each treated separately: the first player chooses how much goes on the first active location and the rest goes on the second (`SplitProgress`, `families/smaug/split.ts`; asked only when the progress does not fill both); its Shadow adds it to the staging area, not revealed. Esgaroth Wharf (`50224:A:2`, `A:3`; literal, additional attack recommended, https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/136485-smaug-town-baiting-and-esgaroth-wharf-battle-of-lake-town): all locations in play are immune to player card effects; the Shadow gives +2 Attack and an additional attack (`faq.additional-attacks`), which in a Town-Baiting attack is against Lake-town again. The Master's Manor (`50216:A:2`, `A:3`; literal): all damage on Smaug moves to Lake-town, nothing without Smaug; if the Shadow's attack destroys a character, the defending player discards his hand.
  - The Old Thrush (`50208:A:1`, `A:2`; literal, the printed lines; attachment reading researched, https://talesfromthecards.wordpress.com/2013/07/11/surviving-the-battle-of-lake-town/; duration agent recommendation): Guarded; an Action exhausts a ready hero to claim it when it is free of encounters and attach it to that hero; the Response after the attached hero is declared as an attacker against Smaug has its player choose X from his hand size, discard X cards and reduce Smaug's Defense by X until the end of that attack (it applies to every attacker in it). Smaug's immunity covers player cards only, and The Old Thrush is an encounter objective. It counts as an attachment while attached (`CountsAs` with no trait, so a card that counts or targets attachments finds it) and, as printed ("If detached, discard The Old Thrush"), any detach discards it to the encounter discard pile instead of returning it to the staging area: its host leaving play, or a card discarding it (the flag `discardedWhenDetached`, `src/rules/families/objectives/claimed.ts`).
  - The table (the Table step, `docs/agents/agent-brief.md`, Scenario procedure; lines `50201:A:1`, `50201:B:1`, `50202:B:1`, `50203:B:3`, `50205:A:2`, `50207:A:1`): the Smaug deck and the Smaug discard pile are piles of the second slot (`smaugDeck`, `smaugDiscard`, `src/play/store/pile-ids.ts`), with their counts, and the discard pile opens its list. The top bar holds a Lake-town dial, the damage on Lake-town against the 50 that loses the game, which warms within 10 and turns critical within 3 (`src/play/readouts/standalone-scenarios/lake-town.ts`); on stage 3 a Victory points dial, the points in the victory display against that damage (more points wins, a tie loses); and a Smaug dial, his damage against the cap of the stage (6, 12) or, on stage 3, against his 20 hit points. A location's Burn X chip opens the keyword's definition from the Quest Companion (`src/play/cards/keyword-rules.ts`). Each Burn question names the player on each row, says on the Done row who is asked next or that the rest goes on Lake-town, says the share that destroys the character, and the keep row of a cancel offer says whether another player is asked next; says the pool and Lake-town's damage, and arms the share that destroys a character and Done when the rest has nobody to take it and Lake-town would reach 50 (`src/play/words/scenarios/standalone-scenarios/smaug.ts`); the Smaug question and stage 2B's discard of progress say the number, the player and what each row leaves (the asks entries are in `src/play/words/asks/the-battle-of-lake-town.ts`).
  - Not user-confirmed (community or agent recommendation only): the Smaug deck and Burn rules, the blind choice, Smaug Magnificent's excess, Burn's damage source and Great House order, the cancel offer order, Town-Baiting's defending player and additional attack, North Pier, Fisherman's Dock and the Old Thrush's duration.

## The Stone of Erech

The 2013 print-on-demand scenario (pack `TSoE`, Hall of Beorn lists it under the year `2013`), Normal and Easy. No Nightmare deck exists for it: the Nightmare source has no set for it (`scripts/data/nightmare-defs.ts`), Hall of Beorn lists no Nightmare card set for it, and its scenario page's Nightmare column only repeats the Normal and Easy quantities (https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Scenarios/The-Stone-of-Erech). Hall of Beorn lists no rulesheet for it (its resources are the Rules Reference alone) and `data/rules` holds none. The only source for its Night objective and Spectral rules is RulesPal's transcription of the scenario's rulesheet, a community source, so those readings are marked "researched, community (RulesPal transcription)".

- **Data.** Hall of Beorn numbers its 27 cards `00001` to `00027`, which are the earlier standalone packs' codes and RingsDB lists no card of it, so `overrides.productCodes` gives them `503` and the two-digit collector number: `50301` to `50303` the quest stages, `50304` Derufin, `50305` The Lord of the Dead, `50306` The Stone of Erech, `50307` Tarlang's Neck, `50308` to `50310` the Night objectives Eventide, Dusk and Midnight, `50311` Relic from the Dark Years, `50312` to `50327` the other encounter cards (50 is RingsDB's cycle 50, 3 the pack's position in it). The piles are DragnCards' deck Q99.03; it has no Easy deck, and Hall of Beorn's Easy counts equal the Normal ones, so Easy loads the same deck (community: 45 encounter cards in both modes, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2020/07/17/the-stone-of-erech/; no official source says otherwise). The encounter deck is the 38 cards of the set that no Setup line sets aside, Relic from the Dark Years among them. Tarlang's Neck is the active location (`overrides.scenarioPiles`, `50301:A:1`); Derufin, the Lord of the Dead, the Stone and the three Night objectives are set aside, out of play, and Eventide is placed into play next to the current quest (`scripts/data/normalize/setup-standalone-scenarios.ts` reads both Setup sentences).

  - **Night objectives** (researched, community: RulesPal transcription). One Night objective is in play at a time, next to the current quest, and the players cannot claim it. "Dusk/Midnight." means Dusk or Midnight, not Eventide.
  - **Spectral** (researched, community: RulesPal transcription). Which modifiers count (Willpower modifiers yes, Attack bonuses no) is an agent recommendation, mirroring the engine's Battle. The Relic from the Dark Years (Midnight Action by any player, attached to a hero that player controls) removes Spectral from an Oathbreaker the attached hero attacks, for that attack and until the end of the phase (researched, community; "this very attack" is an agent recommendation).

Stage 1B cannot be defeated while Tarlang's Neck is in play, and excess progress when a stage is defeated is lost (researched, Caleb Grace, FAQ 3.169).
  - **Derufin** (recommended: agent recommendation, no ruling). After the players travel to The Stone of Erech the first player takes control of him: he leaves the Stone and enters their area ready as an ally. If he leaves play, wherever he is, the players lose.
  - **Group choices** (recommended: agent recommendation; the first player decides, as elsewhere in the app): stage 2B's choice is one per round (all players skip this round's planning phase, or no hero collects resources this round); Tarlang's Neck's Forced is one choice (end the encounter phase, or engage all enemies in the staging area, offered even with none).

## The Ruins of Belegost

The 2015 print-on-demand scenario (pack `TRoB`, RingsDB's code for it, position 6 of cycle 50), Normal and Easy, the sixth of the cycle. Hall of Beorn files it under the product `2015` beside Murder at the Prancing Pony, so each product line names `scenarioTitle`. No Nightmare deck exists for it: `scripts/data/nightmare-defs.ts` and `data/raw/nightmare` have no set for it, and Hall of Beorn's Nightmare counts only repeat Normal (https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Scenarios/The-Ruins-of-Belegost). Hall of Beorn lists no rulesheet and `data/rules` holds none; the scenario's rules are the quest cards and the four-page Scenario Rules insert, whose pages 2 to 4 are the DragnCards rules cards (read from their pictures, below).

- **Data.** Hall of Beorn numbers its cards `00001` to `00030`, which collide with The Massing at Osgiliath's; RingsDB lists none of the pack's cards. `overrides.productCodes` gives them `506` and the collector number, as the Stone of Erech's are `503` (`50601` to `50607` the seven quest cards, `50608` Stalking the Ruins, `50609` to `50614` the six Loot objectives, `50615` Naurlhûg, `50616` Lurker of the Depths, `50621` Naurlhûg's Lair, `50617` to `50620` and `50622` to `50630` the rest of the encounter deck). Gimli (01004, tagged with the box in Hall of Beorn's player cards) stays the Core Set hero. The piles are DragnCards' deck Q99.06 for Normal and its own Easy twin E99.06 for Easy, checked against the quest card's printed Setup (`50601:A:1`): Stalking the Ruins and one Abandoned Mine for each player are staged (`perPlayer`), and Lurker of the Depths, Naurlhûg, Naurlhûg's Lair, the Sword of Belegost and the five other Loot objectives (Map of Ered Luin, Keys of Belegost, Book of Aulë's Children, Dwarven Torch, Blue Mountain Gem) wait in the set-aside pile; the other 12 titles (the encounter deck, 31 cards on Normal) are dealt as the Setup says by the record. The Setup's "Randomly select 1 of the 5 remaining Loot objectives and place it facedown underneath Stalking the Ruins ... Shuffle the others into the encounter deck" is read by two Setup steps (`hideLoot`, `shuffleOthersIn`, `scripts/data/normalize/setup-standalone-scenarios.ts`); the five stay in the set-aside pile for the scenario's record to hide and shuffle (the Sword is set aside by the line before). DragnCards' two "Scenario Rules" rows are reminder cards, not cards the scenario deals, and the build skips rows of type Rules.
- **Easy.** E99.06 differs from Q99.06 by the three cards Hall of Beorn gives no Easy copies: Flooded Hall, Old Stone Troll and Spawn of Thangorodrim (2 copies each on Normal, 0 on Easy), so the data agrees with Hall of Beorn's counts.
- **Stages.** The quest deck branches: 1B goes to 2C when Keys of Belegost is attached to a hero, else 2A; 2B to 3C when the Book of Aulë's Children is attached, else 3A; 2D to 3E when the Book is attached, else 3C; 3B to 4A when the Dwarven Torch is attached, else 3C; 3D to 4A when the Map of Ered Luin is attached, else 3E; 3F to 4A when the Blue Mountain Gem is attached, else 3A. The data lists the seven cards in printed order (`1A`, `2A`, `2C`, `3A`, `3C`, `3E`, `4A`); the branches are the scenario author's.
- **Scenario Rules cards.** Pages 2 to 4 of the insert (DragnCards' pictures `Scenario Rules 1` and `2`, read from the pictures, researched: the card pictures are the source; page 1 is not available online). Discover X: when a location with the Discover keyword becomes the active location, the first player resolves in order: look at the top X cards of the encounter deck, X being the active location's Discover value; choose 1 looked at objective with the Loot keyword and attach it to the active location as a guarded objective; randomly select 1 looked at card with the Hazard trait, reveal it and add it to the staging area; place any remaining looked at cards in the encounter discard pile. If fewer than X cards remain in the encounter deck when resolving Discover, shuffle the encounter discard pile into the encounter deck and continue. Loot: objectives with the Loot keyword can only enter play by resolving the Discover keyword on a location; a Loot card revealed from the encounter deck is discarded and another card is revealed from the top. Taking control of Loot: when the encounter card guarding a Loot objective leaves play, the first player must immediately attach the Loot objective to a hero he controls; a Loot objective attached to the active location when the players make enough progress to explore it and advance to the next stage is taken by the first player before advancing. Immune to player card effects: such a card ignores the effects of all player cards and cannot be chosen as a target of player card effects.
- **Ledger.** The 37 printed sides are read from the scans (`data/printed/standalone-scenarios.json`). Hall of Beorn's text has: "The player cannot advance" for the printed "The players cannot advance" (50601 B, 50603 B), "advace" and "Stage 2A" for "advance" and "stage 2A" (50601 B), "Otherwise advance" for "Otherwise, advance" (50604 B), "on the active location" for "of the active location" (50605 B), "Shuffle the Sword of Belegost" for "Shuffle Sword of Belegost" (50607 A), single quotes for the printed double quotes (50607 B, 50624 A), "1 progress token" for "1 resource" on Stalking the Ruins (50608 A), "fist player" for "first player" (50610 to 50614), "that hero's resource pool" for "that hero's pool" (50611), a comma for the full stop in "Immune to player card effects. Cannot take damage." (50615), and Lurker of the Depths missing its first two lines, "Cannot have attachments." and "cannot be engaged or take more than 4 damage each round." (50616). Two printed typos are kept under `misprints`: the full stop missing at the end of Naurlhûg's Forced line (50615 A, line 3) and "Blue Mountain Goblins gains surge" (50620 A, line 1).
- **Readings.** The scenario shares Discover X and Loot with The Ghost of Framsburg (`emce.discover`, `emce.loot`), whose records the scenario reuses (`Discover`, `Loot`, `ClaimWhenFree`, `GuardObjective`, the pick fields `lootKeyword` and `controlled`; Ered Mithrin, The Ghost of Framsburg, above). The Scenario Rules insert's steps are word for word the `emce.discover` steps, so no new Discover text applies (researched: the insert pictures, `emce.discover`, `emce.loot`; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2021/01/04/the-ruins-of-belegost lists the same four steps and the same shuffle of the discard pile when fewer than X cards remain). No FAQ span, Hall of Beorn ruling, designer answer or forum thread rules on this scenario (searched Hall of Beorn's scenario page, the FFG news post, BGG and Reddit through search; none found), so every reading below is the card's text or `recommended` (agent recommendation, not user-confirmed; the section Readings to confirm, at the end of this file, lists them).

**Readings.**

1. **The opposing team in a cooperative game** (`researched`): "When a card says 'the opposing team chooses' or 'the opposing team searches', the first player resolves it in the manner that will provide the greatest challenge for the players", and an effect that counts players counts only the active team's players (`https://rulespal.com/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-card-game-the-wizards-quest/rulebook`, Cooperative Play and Rules Clarifications; `https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2018/5/4/the-wizards-quest/`). The app resolves each such effect for the first player with a fixed policy and no prompt (reading 3 gives it line by line).
2. **Which encounter sets and stages are used** (`recommended`): the rulebook's Quick-build says "choosing 7 different encounter sets" and "choosing one stage 2 and one stage 3" and adds that quest cards and sets may be chosen at random (`https://rulespal.com/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-card-game-the-wizards-quest/rulebook`, `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/04/12/the-wizards-quest/`). No source settles how a solo app should choose. Recommendation: the app deals 7 of the 14 sets at random from the game's seed (35 cards) and the first player chooses the stage 2 and stage 3 cards, as the Attack on Dol Guldur players choose a stage 3. Not user-confirmed. The rulebook itself allows a random choice of sets and quest cards in cooperative play ("If you wish to surprise yourself in cooperative play, you can randomly choose your encounter sets and quest cards"), so the random sets are `researched`; the first player's choice of stages is the recommendation. No source prints a fixed Quick-build list of sets. The other 7 sets stay out of the game (set aside, never dealt); the game has no cost limit or card-type minimum, which only Advanced-build has.
3. **The opposing team's choice, line by line** (`recommended`; agent recommendation, since the rulebook says only "the manner that will provide the greatest challenge" and names no measure; sources as reading 1 and `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/04/12/the-wizards-quest/`, which describes the opposing team as building the deck and resolving 2A and 3A, no other ruling found). The first player's choice is made by the engine, never asked, by the first rule that measures: a search for an enemy or location takes the card with the highest printed Threat (an enemy: the highest Attack when Threat is level), then the one nearest the top of the deck; a search that cannot be measured (2A and 3A, any card type) takes the legal card nearest the top; a choice among characters takes the one that costs the players most (the ally with the highest printed cost, the questing character with the highest Willpower, the Troll with the most hit points left for Smoking Blood, the card in hand with the highest printed cost for Necromancer's Pass, ties by seat order then play order); Power Sevenfold chooses exactly one card, the non-unique enemy or location in play with the lowest Threat. Alternative: the first player picks through the choice tray; it would make the card the player's own gift. A card that is not found changes nothing and the deck is still shuffled.
4. **Encounter cost** (`literal`, data): each encounter card prints a COST (0 to 3, bottom right, beside its type); Hall of Beorn gives none, so `overrides.cards` carries the 43 costs read from the scans. A search "for a card with cost N or less" reads that printed cost, a treachery included. "cost 2 or less if there are two or more players" counts the players of the game (reading 1).
5. **The 2A and 3A searches** (`literal`, rr.search): the search looks only at the top 5 cards of the encounter deck; the card found is set aside, the deck is shuffled and the card is revealed (an enemy or location enters the staging area, a treachery resolves and is discarded; surge applies as for any reveal). Its When Revealed effects cannot be canceled. With no eligible card nothing is revealed and the deck is shuffled.
6. **The 1A Setup** (`literal` and `span-settled` by the sibling Setup searches, 41004:B:1): the first sentence is the deal (Rhosgobel starts in the staging area). Per player, one card is found, a 0 cost enemy or location, each with a title that differs from the cards found before (`different`); they are added to the staging area, not revealed, and fewer cards are added when fewer exist. The search is of the encounter deck only (the discard pile is empty).
7. **Rhosgobel flips** (`literal`): 1B's Forced effect, when Rhosgobel is explored, turns the one two-sided card over: Rhosgobel does not go to the discard pile and is no longer in play as Rhosgobel, and Rider of Mirkwood enters the staging area as a fresh card (no tokens). Stage 1B can then be defeated at 10 progress once Rhosgobel is no longer in play. Rhosgobel's own Response runs before this, on the travel.
8. **Rider of Mirkwood** (`literal`): printed engagement cost 50, Threat X, Attack 6, Defense 3, 10 hit points, Victory 6. X (Threat) is the current stage number plus 1. "Stage 3B" is stage 3 once its B side is up, which is every time the stage is the current one outside the reveal of its A side. A New Terror Abroad's attack is an immediate attack from the staging area on the first player, with its shadow card, and Rider stays in the staging area. Return to Rhosgobel gives the Rider engagement cost 0, caps its damage at the progress on the stage and bars the stage from defeat while it is in play.
9. **Carried Away's guarded allies** (`recommended`: no source). Each player's ally is chosen by reading 3 (the highest printed cost; a player with no ally loses none). The ally leaves play (its attachments are discarded, tokens removed, as for any card that leaves play) and lies faceup under the stage as a guarded objective with no text; copies of its title cannot be played or put into play, by any player, while it is guarded; copies already in play stay. When the stage is defeated each returns to its owner's hand. Alternative: the ally keeps its attachments; rr.attachment-cards (the host leaves play) settles against it.
10. **Dungeons of the Necromancer's guarded heroes** (`recommended`: sibling precedent, Carried Away of Lost in Mirkwood, 18074, and `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/04/12/the-wizards-quest/`, which says the players choose the hero). Each player in seat order, the first player first, chooses the hero through the choice tray (it is a player's own card), then discards from the top of the encounter deck until an enemy is discarded and adds it to the staging area. Only the hero's tokens are discarded, as printed: its attachments stay on it. A guarded hero stays in play (rr.player-elimination: a hero is lost only when killed), is not controlled by anyone and cannot be eliminated; a player with one hero still plays on with no hero. Once the enemy leaves play in any way (the user ruling of 2026-11-05 for 18074:B:2), the hero returns exhausted under its owner's control. The stage cannot be defeated while any guarded objective is in play, the Rider included. If no enemy is found nothing is guarded.
11. **The Tower of Sorcery** (`recommended`: precedent of 51032:A:2): the Forced effect discards from the top of the encounter deck until a treachery is discarded and stops with the deck empty (the discard pile is not shuffled back for this); it reveals the treachery only when one was found.
12. **A Fork in the Road** (`literal`): the location found is the one marked by the resource; while the stage has quest points remaining (progress below 5) it is immune to player card effects and cannot be traveled to; progress goes to the stage because the active location cannot be chosen. The stage is not defeated while that location is in play. With none found nothing is marked and the stage is as printed.
13. **Competitive play** (`literal`): not built. Rules that name the opposing team count only the active team's players, here all players.
14. **"Raise your threat" on a Travel cost** (Pine Slopes 51022:A:2; `recommended`: no source; searched the FFG FAQ and rulespal listings and `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/04/12/the-wizards-quest/`, none names it): the first player travels (rr.step-4-2), so the threat of the first player rises, not that of each player. A line that prints "each player's" (Forest Stream 51050:A:2) raises each.
15. **"Each damaged character"** (Evil Storm 51027:A:1; `recommended`: no source): a lasting effect applies to every card that meets its criteria for the phase (rr.lasting-effects), so every damaged character gets -1 Willpower and cannot be healed, those damaged before Evil Storm too.
16. **Hunting Warg's +2 Attack** (51037:A:1; `recommended`: no source): the bonus is for the immediate attack in which the blank shadow card was dealt, and applies to that attack itself, not after it (the record is a constant read while that immediate attack is made).
17. **Necromancer's Pass** (51013:A:1; `recommended`: no source; the Hall of Beorn card page `https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Necromancer's-Pass-TWQ` only repeats the text): copies of the guarded card cannot be played (they may still be put into play by an effect). The guarded card returns to its owner's hand when the Pass is explored, as printed; if the Pass leaves play another way (a discard by a card effect), the card also returns to its owner's hand, so no player card is lost.
18. **Reveal in full** (`literal`, rr.reveal): "Reveal that treachery" (The Necromancer's Tower, The Sorcerer's Tower) reveals the card in full, its keywords (Doomed) too. "Attach to the main quest" (Under the Shadow) is the main quest card, never a side quest; Forest Stream removes progress from the main quest. Nightfall attaches to the first player's dial, and each copy adds 1 to its owner's refresh threat raise. Befouled Equipment goes to the character, hero or ally, with the most attachments among those that have no copy. Fastness of Mirkwood finds at most 2 locations.

8. **Spider Den flips into Mendor** (`literal`): 1B's Forced effect, when Spider Den is explored, turns the one two-sided card over: Spider Den does not go to the discard pile, and Mendor enters play ready, under the first player's control, with none of Spider Den's tokens. Stage 1B (10 quest points) cannot be defeated until the first player controls Mendor, so the players explore Spider Den first. Spider Den's Travel runs on the travel and its own immunity to player card effects keeps player effects from clearing or traveling to it.
9. **The stage 2B progress cap** (`recommended`, no source): the cap of 5 (10 while Mendor is committed to the quest) counts all progress placed on the stage this round, whatever places it, and is read at each placement, so removing Mendor from the quest mid-phase lowers it. "After characters are committed" is read as the cap holding from the end of the commit step; progress cannot normally be placed on the stage before it. `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/03/06/the-woodland-realm/` ("you can quest with Mendor in order to place double that amount of progress this turn") agrees. Alternative: the cap is fixed once at the commit step.
10. **Lost in the Dark** (`span-settled` by the same line of 50721:A:2, and the review above: "the Doomed 1 and immunity to cancelation only counts for the first treachery each round"): the first treachery revealed each round, here or in a search, gains Doomed 1 (added to a Doomed it already prints) and its When Revealed effects cannot be canceled.
11. **Ambushed at Night** (`literal`): each enemy in the staging area gets -10 engagement cost, so it engages at the engagement checks of the encounter phase; "cannot be optionally engaged" bars only the player's choice to engage it, not the checks. It cannot take damage while it is in the staging area.
12. **"Don't Leave the Path"** (`literal`): the line is the printed text of 142021:B:1 (the same mandatory travel, the engine's `mustTravel`); no progress can be placed on a location in the staging area, whatever places it.
13. **Shuffling after a search, 51107:B:1, 51122:A:2, 51125:A:2, 51141:A:1** (`recommended`; `https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/206167-encounter-deck/` (two players: "you always reshuffle the encounter deck after looking through it for a card"), `https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/253143-shuffle-the-encounter-deck-outside-of-questing-phase` (one player quotes the opposite, second hand), `https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/182893-deck-search/` (no answer); `rr.search` says nothing and no official text found settles it): the encounter deck is shuffled after each of these searches, as after every other search of it in the app (01126:B:1, 142022:A:1, 142040:A:1, 41041:A:1), though these lines print no shuffle. The discard pile is not shuffled into it. The Forest of Great Fear's search of the deck and discard pile finds an enemy of any cost and adds it to the staging area, then the deck is shuffled. Then (rr.then), only if an enemy was found, one resource goes on an enemy in play, in the staging area or engaged, with printed cost 2 or higher, the enemy just found included. Only that enemy has the resource; while this stage is in play it gets +2 Attack and +2 Defense and is immune to player card effects, and the stage cannot be defeated while it is in play. With no such enemy no resource is placed and the stage can be defeated as printed. The stage's own quest points (15) and its win on defeat are as printed.
14. **To the Elvenking's Halls, 3B** (`literal`): X (quest points) is the highest threat level of any player now. "Each player's threat cannot be reduced by more than 1 each round" holds for every reduction, not only by player card effects (the flag `threatReductionCap`, 11217:A:1). The Forced effect reveals the top card in full (rr.reveal) after a successful quest when the progress placed on this stage this phase is 10 or more.
15. **Escape from Taur-nu-Fuin, 3B** (`researched`, `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/03/06/the-woodland-realm/`: the Forced effect "will reset the encounter deck if it is empty"; the same wording of emce.guarded-x): if the encounter deck is empty while discarding, the discard pile is shuffled into it and the effect continues; with no location in the deck and the discard pile it stops. The location found goes to the staging area faceup, not revealed. This differs from The Wizard's Quest reading 11 (The Tower of Sorcery), which has no such source.
16. **Enchanted Stream** (51119:A:2, `recommended`, no source): "those heroes" are the heroes exhausted by this Travel effect; they cannot ready while Enchanted Stream is the active location (the refresh step included). While the stream is in the staging area, no progress can be placed on the main quest (as Cracked Pillar, 41006:B:2).
17. **Caught in a Web** (51123:A:1, `recommended`, mirkwood-paths precedent of the same attachment text): it attaches to a hero without a copy and exhausts it; the hero cannot ready unless its controller first spends 2 resources from the hero's pool. A hero that cannot pay stays exhausted.
18. **Ambushing Orcs, 51151:A:2** (`recommended`, no source): "the lowest engagement cost enemy in the staging area" is read on the printed cost with the staging area's current modifiers; a tie goes to the defending player's choice. The enemy engages the defending player and is dealt a shadow card.
19. **Other copied lines** (`literal`): Wasted Provisions (51153) as 18041:A:1; Dark-wood Grove, Mountains of Mirkwood, Dry Watercourse, Wild Wargs, Pack of Wargs, Beast of Taur-nu-Fuin, Old Forest Road, Mirkwood Path, Giant Web and Chieftain Ufthak as their sibling records named in the pack. Competitive and Advanced-build play: not built.
20. **A Travel effect that makes another location active** (`recommended`, no source; Dark-wood Grove's Travel reveals the top card, and Giant Web, 51131:A:1, makes itself the active location): the players cannot travel while a location is active, so the travel to the location does not take place, the location stays in the staging area and the travel opportunity is over. No FAQ or forum entry names the case (the rulebook of reading 1 was read). Alternative: the revealed location returns to the staging area and the travel goes on.
21. **How the stage 2 cap is applied** (`recommended`, a limit of the engine's trigger names; refines reading 9): the Forced line is a record that fires when the staging step begins (after characters are committed) and sets the cap to the end of the round, 10 when Mendor is committed at that moment, else 5. A later removal of Mendor from the quest does not lower it (the alternative of reading 9).

**Readings.**

1. **The opposing team in a cooperative game** (`researched`): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 1. The kit's cards use the same words, and no Moria rulebook text was found (`https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/05/22/the-mines-of-moria/` describes the opposing team choosing and searching for the players, and gives no ruling). 2. **Which sets and stages are used** (`recommended`): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 2 and The Woodland Realm, reading 2. A forum poster says a solo or cooperative game can "pick seven random encounter sets" and play the quests like any other (`https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/295134-mines-of-moria-gen-con-scenario`); the kit's 21-cost build limit is the Advanced-build rule, not built. The app deals 7 of the 14 sets from the seed; the first player chooses the stage 2 and stage 3 cards.
3. **The opposing team's choice, line by line** (`recommended`, extends The Wizard's Quest reading 3 and The Woodland Realm reading 3; no source names a measure): the engine chooses for the first player, never asks. A search for an enemy or a location takes the highest printed Threat (an enemy: then the highest Attack), then the card nearest the top (3B Drums in the Deep's enemy, 2B and Narrow Paths' locations per player, Spider Burrow, Goblin Tunnels, Goblin Warlord, Goblin Sneak, They Are Coming!). The top-5 searches of 2A and 3A take the legal card nearest the top. A hero for Shadow of Fear is the hero with the highest Willpower (Woodland reading 17). A non-unique enemy for Denizen of the Deep is the one with the highest Attack, then Threat, then the one nearest the top. An ally for Sudden Pitfall is the ally with the highest printed cost. The enemy for They Are Coming! (Shadow) is the enemy in the encounter discard pile with the highest Threat, then Attack. A card that is not found changes nothing. Alternative: the first player picks through the choice tray.
4. **Encounter cost** (`literal`, data): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 4; `overrides.cards` carries the 42 printed costs. "Cost 1 or less" reads the printed cost of any card type; "0 cost" and "the total cost of the discarded cards" (Dark and Dreadful) read printed cost, a card with no printed cost counting 0.
5. **The 2A and 3A searches** (`literal`, rr.search): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 5. The scan of 51205 (Narrow Paths) shows the shared 2A line on its side A and the guard line on its side B; the ledger had them the other way and is corrected.
6. **The 1A Setup** (`literal`): each player adds 1 resource to each of his heroes' pools (the Setup step `eachHeroResources`); The Watcher starts in the staging area (the deal).
7. **The Watcher, 1B** (`literal`; precedent Old Man Willow, 147006:B:1, and Lurker of the Depths; `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/05/22/the-mines-of-moria/` agrees and adds that removing progress "submerges" it again): the condition is read live. While the stage holds at least 5 progress, The Watcher is considered engaged with each player: it attacks each player in turn order in the combat phase with a shadow card for each attack, every player may declare attackers against it, and it adds no Threat to the staging area (rr.engaged). Below 5 progress it is in the staging area again. It cannot leave the staging area. Its +2 Threat and +4 hit points count every player. When it is defeated it is not discarded: the one two-sided card turns over and Chamber of Mazarbul enters the staging area as a new card with no tokens (the Rhosgobel flip, The Wizard's Quest reading 7). The stage, with 5 progress still on it, is then defeated and the players go on to stage 2.
8. **Chamber of Mazarbul** (`literal`; The Wizard's Quest reading 8, Rider of Mirkwood): X (Threat) is the stage number of the main quest plus 1, read live (`https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/05/22/the-mines-of-moria/` gives 4 at stage 3B before Continuing Eastward's +1 and 5 with it). "Unless they are at stage 3B" means the current quest card is stage 3 (the A side is only the reveal). The players cannot travel to it before then. It is a unique location with 5 quest points and Victory 5; exploring it puts it in the victory display, which lets stage 3B be defeated and win the game.
9. **A Presence in the Dark, 2B** (`recommended`, no source; the article above only repeats the text): at the beginning of each staging step the opposing team looks at the top 2 cards and puts the weaker one on the bottom, the other on top. The stronger card is: an enemy (higher Threat, then Attack), then a treachery, then a location (higher Threat); a tie keeps the top card on top. A deck of one card changes nothing. Alternative: the first player chooses.
10. **Narrow Paths, 2B** (`researched` for the effect, `recommended` for the choice; `https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/05/22/the-mines-of-moria/`: "the opposing team gets to select one attachment per player", returned "only to their owner's hand" when the stage is defeated): the attachment chosen is the one with the highest printed cost among the attachments a player controls (attached to any card in play), ties by the card nearest the first attached; a player with none loses none. It leaves its host (its own tokens are discarded) and lies faceup under the stage, still its owner's, out of play, text blank; while it lies there no copy of its title can be played or put into play, by any player; copies already in play stay. When the stage is defeated each returns to its owner's hand (the Carried Away precedent, The Wizard's Quest reading 9). If a guarded attachment's stage leaves play any other way, it also returns to its owner's hand.
11. **Hidden Threat** (`recommended`, no source; precedent Rescue Iârion, 09024, for a card lying facedown under a stage, and 144068:B:1 for revealing a facedown card from under a card): the opposing team takes the legal card nearest the top of the top 5 (cost at most the stage number of the main quest, counted now; any card type), places it facedown under the main quest, and the deck is shuffled (reading 14). After the next time the players quest successfully (rr.step-3-4: willpower higher than the staging threat, with or without an active location), the card is revealed in full (rr.reveal): an enemy or location enters the staging area, a treachery resolves; each card hidden so is revealed in the order placed. A stage change does not discard it. With no legal card nothing is placed.
12. **The Fate of Balin, 3B** (`literal`, the first player chooses: both options are the player's, so the choice tray asks): "reveal an additional encounter card this phase" reveals one card, in full, as the effect resolves at the beginning of the staging step; "no more than 5 progress can be placed here" caps the progress placed on this stage this phase, whatever places it, not on the active location (as The Woodland Realm reading 9).
13. **Stairs of Náin** (`span-settled` by the same sentence of The Woodland Realm, 51141 Lost in the Dark, reading 10): while it is in the staging area, the first treachery revealed each round, here or in a search, gains surge and its 'when revealed' effects cannot be canceled. Unlike Lost in the Dark it adds no Doomed.
14. **Shuffling after a search** (`recommended`, The Woodland Realm reading 13): the encounter deck is shuffled after Hidden Threat's and Goblin Sneak's top-5 searches, which print no shuffle, as after every search of it in the app. The discard pile is not shuffled in.
15. **Denizen of the Deep** (`recommended`, no source): "returns it to the staging area" is the enemy, which leaves combat; the attachment stays on it. With no non-unique enemy in play (staging or engaged) the card gains surge. The attached condition gives +1 Threat, +1 Attack, +1 Defense and immunity to player card effects, and stays until the enemy leaves play.
16. **Shadow of Fear** (`literal`, rr.blank): the attached hero's text box is treated as blank, its Traits kept; the attachment has no limit and no way to be discarded printed.
17. **Zigil Mineshaft** (`recommended`; precedent Hills of Wilderland, 18023: "Only the first player can trigger this ability"): the Quest Action may be used any number of times in the quest phase while Zigil Mineshaft is in play, each use by the first player raising every player's threat by 1 and lowering Zigil Mineshaft's Threat by 1 until the end of the phase (not below 0).
18. **Other lines** (`literal`): Foul Air's second option ends the quest phase without resolving the quest (the engine's end-the-phase verb); Savage Werewolf, Werewolf, Goblin Follower and Goblin Archer's granted Forced lines follow their printed text; Competitive and Advanced-build play: not built.
19. **Choices a line gives to no named player** (`recommended`, no source; Rules Reference, The word "Choose", `rr.the-word-choose`, gives the controller the choice): "Either no more than 4 progress can be placed on the current quest this phase, or Web-spinner gains surge" (51230:A:1) and Foul Air's two options (51232:A:1 names the first player) are the first player's, and the choice tray asks. Grabbed by Spiders (51220:A:1) removes X characters from the quest, X the Spider enemies in play (staging or engaged): the opposing team takes the committed character with the highest Willpower each time. "Assign X damage among characters in play" (Ancient Guardroom's Travel, Goblin Archer's gained Forced effect) is assigned by the first player, among any player's characters; Cave In's X is the Threat of the location it returned to the staging area, read as it returns.
20. **Timing of the Forced and Response lines** (`literal`): Goblin Warlord's Forced effect fires when it enters play, wherever it enters (the staging area or engaged); Dwarven Forge's Response answers its exploring only while it is the active location (the trigger `activeExplored`); Continuing Eastward's Forced effect hears the exploring of the active location only; Ancient Guardroom's resource goes on after any event is played, by any player; Lurking Malice, Dark and Dreadful and We Cannot Get Out add their Threat to the staging area until the end of the phase, read when the effect resolves (Lurking Malice: the resources on the heroes before they are discarded). Plundered Armoury and Darkened Stairway's Travel discard the top card of the encounter deck and resolve it only when it is the card the line names.
21. **The stages that cannot be defeated** (`literal`, `rr.quest-advancement`): a stage that holds its quest points in progress while a card holds it back advances as soon as nothing holds it, at the next step of the round: stage 1B after The Watcher's flip (the progress of the quest phase stays on it, 5 or more), the three stage 3 cards after Chamber of Mazarbul or the last enemy has left play. The progress each quest phase puts on a held stage is kept.
22. **Massing in the Deep's Shadow, duration** (`recommended`; no source: a search found no ruling and no discussion of the card, and `rr.lasting-effects` names no default duration): "Each enemy engaged with you gets +1 Attack" lasts until the end of the phase. A bonus that ended with this attack would never reach the other engaged enemies' attacks and the line would do nothing. The enemies that are engaged with the defending player when the shadow card is resolved get it. Alternative: until the end of this attack, which gives the bonus to the attacker alone.

**Readings.**

1. **The opposing team in a cooperative game** (`researched`): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 1, and the Escape from Khazad-dûm rulebook says it in its own words: "the first player should resolve that effect in the manner that will provide the greatest challenge for the players" (https://rulespal.com/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-card-game-escape-from-khazad-dum/rulebook). The engine chooses; no prompt is shown.
2. **Which sets and stages are used** (`recommended`): as The Mines of Moria, reading 2. The rulebook's Quick-build is one stage 2 and one stage 3 under stage 1 and 7 different encounter sets (https://rulespal.com/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-card-game-escape-from-khazad-dum/rulebook); the app deals 7 of the 14 sets from the seed and the first player chooses the stage 2 and stage 3 cards. The 1A Setup is the Quick-build (`BuildQuickDeck` keep 7 and two `ChooseQuestStage` steps) before the Setup search. Competitive play is not built.
3. **The opposing team's choice, line by line** (`recommended`, extends The Mines of Moria reading 3; no source names a measure): a search for an enemy or location takes the highest printed Threat (an enemy: then the highest Attack), then the card nearest the top (the Setup search, Old One Lair, Nest of Vermin, Frightful Den, Many Roads). The top-5 searches of 2A and Dark Pit's Travel take the legal card nearest the top. Vast and Intricate takes the non-unique location in the staging area with the highest Threat. Watchful Eyes takes the hero with the highest Willpower (The Woodland Realm reading 17). Goblin Dungeon takes the ally with the highest printed cost in play (one ally, not one for each player). Lost and Alone takes each player's questing character with the highest Willpower. A Foe Beyond takes the attachment with the highest printed cost (The Mines of Moria reading 10). The card chosen by Dreadful Gap, and the location Many Roads shuffles back, is the card in the encounter discard pile with the highest Threat (an enemy: then Attack), else the one most recently discarded. Black Uruks' search takes the legal card with the highest Attack if it is an enemy, else the card nearest the top. A card that is not found changes nothing, and the encounter deck is still shuffled where a line says so.
4. **Encounter cost** (`literal`, data): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 4; `overrides.cards` carries the 42 printed costs. "Cost 1 or less" reads the printed cost of any card type; "0-cost" reads a printed 0 (a card with no printed cost counts 0).
5. **The 2A and Dark Pit searches** (`literal`, rr.search): as The Wizard's Quest, reading 5, and The Woodland Realm's Spider Den Travel (51110:A:2). A Travel line that searches, sets aside and reveals a card is an effect, not a cost: the players travel to Dark Pit whatever the deck holds.
6. **The 1A Setup** (`literal`, as The Wizard's Quest, reading 6): Dark Pit starts in the staging area (the deal); the opposing team then searches the encounter deck for one different 0-cost enemy or location for each player, adds them to the staging area, and the deck is shuffled.
7. **Dark Pit and 1B** (`literal`, rr.explored; precedent Rhosgobel, The Wizard's Quest reading 7, with the one two-sided card): exploring Dark Pit (1 quest point) flips it: the card turns over and Durin's Bane is set aside out of play, not added to the staging area (3A adds it). Each player then searches the top 5 cards of his own deck for any card, adds it to his hand and shuffles his deck (a player with fewer than 5 cards searches those; none found changes nothing, the deck is still shuffled). The player chooses the card, so the choice tray asks. Dark Pit has no Victory, so it goes to the encounter discard pile as the flip leaves play. The stage cannot be defeated while Dark Pit is in play, as Rhosgobel's (51001:B:2), nor while the flip of an explored Dark Pit is still pending: its explored event (`locationExplored`, or `activeExplored` when it was the active location) waits to be heard, or the Forced effect is running (the condition `exploredPending`). A stage full at the moment Dark Pit is explored therefore advances only once the flip is done. A Dark Pit that left play in any other way with no flip pending holds nothing, so the stage is never blocked for good.
8. **Old One Lair, 2B** (`literal` for the effect; precedent The Wizard's Quest, 51003:B:1 and :B:2, with an enemy for a location): the opposing team takes an enemy with cost 2 or less (reading 3), adds it to the staging area and places a resource on it; the deck is shuffled. While it holds the resource it is immune to player card effects, and progress cannot be placed on this stage while it is in play. The block is on the stage only: the active location and a side quest still take progress (https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/, community, agrees). Quest-phase progress is lost, not kept, while it is in play. With no such enemy found the stage is not blocked.
9. **A Wrong Turn, 2B** (`literal`; the chooser is each player, named: https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/ reads it as "choose one card from their hand to keep and shuffle all others into their deck"): each player keeps one card of his hand and shuffles every other card of it into his deck, then the deck is shuffled once. A hand of one card or none changes nothing. He draws no card back. The player chooses, so the choice tray asks.
10. **A Way Up, 2B** (`span-settled` by mec109.stage-4, `mustTravel`, 142021:B:1): "Add the active location's Threat to the staging area" is read live: the Threat of the active location counts in the staging area total while it is active (11072:A:1). "The players must travel to a location, if able": the travel step offers no way to decline while a location in the staging area can be traveled to and no active location is in play; the players choose which one and still pay its costs (https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/: "forced to travel during the travel phase").
11. **Bottomless Chasm** (`span-settled`, 19130:A:2 Narrow Stair, 01088:A:1 precedent `travelOnly`): while it is in the staging area and there is no active location, the players travel to it and nowhere else, if able. With two copies in the staging area they choose between those. Its +2 Defense shadow lasts until the end of the phase.
12. **The 3A line** (`literal`): Durin's Bane (set aside by 1B) enters the staging area as a new card with no tokens; it cannot take damage until the end of the round; with more than 1 player it makes an immediate attack against the first player (a shadow card is dealt for it, faq.additional-attacks). The attack is an ordinary one: its undefended or excess damage goes on the heroes, not on 3B, because 3B's line speaks of "this attack", the Forced one (reading 14).
13. **Durin's Bane** (`literal`): "Immune to player card effects" and "Cannot be optionally engaged" as printed (Moria's Watcher, 51210). Its Forced line fires when its attack destroys a character: that character's controller raises his own threat by 2, and Durin's Bane heals 2 damage (Dagnir 19073:A:1). It fires for an undefended attack that kills a hero as for any other, and not for a character destroyed in any other way. Its Victory 5 is printed; defeating the stage wins the game whatever it holds (reading 17).
14. **The Bridge of Khazad-dûm, 3B** (`literal`, `recommended` for "excess"; https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Nearing-the-Gate-The-Bridge-of-Khazad-dûm-EfKD gives the text only, https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/ says "if 10 or more damage lands on the quest card before it clears, the players lose"; no ruling on excess damage was found): at the beginning of each quest phase Durin's Bane (wherever it is, in the staging area or engaged) makes an attack against the first player (a shadow card is dealt, rr.step-6-2). The damage of an undefended attack is placed as damage tokens on this stage and on no hero. For a defended attack the damage goes on the defender as always, and the damage beyond what is needed to destroy it (rr.damage: hit points less the damage already on it; the same count as Hill Troll's excess, 01082:A:1) is placed on this stage as well. Shadow effects that raise the damage count. The players lose when the stage holds damage equal to or greater than its quest points (10); a stage defeated wins the game. The players lose at once, as the damage is placed (the engine's `loseAtDamage`). "No more than 5 progress each round" is 142042:B:1 read for this stage (The Woodland Realm reading 9; The Mines of Moria reading 12).
15. **Last Lord of Moria, 3B** (`literal`; `recommended` for the timing of "engages"): Durin's Bane engages the first player: it engages as soon as the stage is current and whenever it is in the staging area while this stage is current (Captain Sahír, 11076:A:1, and Gollum, 146049:A:2, are the precedent), ignoring its engagement cost. "Cannot take more than X damage each round, X the number of progress tokens on this stage" is read live; with no progress it takes none; damage over the cap is not placed (the Revenant limit, 11181:A:3; `damageCap`, 11177:A:4). Its 3A line runs before the engage is heard: the card is added, then the 3A steps that follow (cannot take damage until the end of the round, the immediate attack with more than one player) pick it staged or engaged, since the Forced engage has already moved it. "This stage cannot be defeated while Durin's Bane is in play", as Moria's reading 21; defeating the stage wins the game.
16. **The Rear Guard, 3B** (`literal`; precedent The Mines of Moria reading 7, Old Man Willow 147006:B:1; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/ agrees with the threat line): Durin's Bane is considered engaged with each player and attacks each player in turn order in the combat phase with a shadow card dealt for each attack. It is read like The Watcher at 5 progress, but with no progress condition: from the moment this stage is current, every player may declare attackers against it, it adds no Threat to the staging area, and it cannot leave the staging area (the rule that holds The Watcher; Old Man Willow prints it). No player's threat can be reduced by a player card effect (18071:B:1). A shadow card dealt to Durin's Bane resolves as to any attacker.
17. **Winning, and why the Bane can stay** (`literal`): each stage 3 card wins the game when it is defeated: the players place its quest points in progress (10, 15 or 20). Last Lord also needs Durin's Bane out of play first; The Bridge and The Rear Guard do not, so Durin's Bane need not be killed there (https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/). Moria's reading 21 holds: a stage that cannot be defeated advances as soon as nothing holds it.
18. **Cave Spider and the other "either" lines with no named player** (`recommended`; as The Mines of Moria reading 19; Rules Reference rr.the-word-choose): Black Bats' When Revealed and Cave Spider's Forced line are the first player's choice (the Spider's Forced line: the defending player's; the attack is against one player), and the choice tray asks. Cave Spider's other option is a +2 Attack for this attack. Black Bats' Shadow is the defending player's.
19. **Orcish Howls and Lost and Alone** (`recommended`; as The Mines of Moria reading 19, Grabbed by Spiders): Orcish Howls exhausts X characters, X the Uruk enemies in play (staging area or engaged): the first player chooses them among the ready characters of every player, all of them if there are fewer. Lost and Alone removes, for each player, the questing character with the highest Willpower (reading 3).
20. **Undisturbed Bones** (`recommended`; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/ reads it as preventing a cancel, no ruling found; rr.cancel for what a cancel is): it attaches to the main quest card (never a side quest, `AttachToQuest main`) and stays there until the stage changes (the stage leaving play discards it). When a "when revealed" effect would be canceled, the cancel does not happen: Undisturbed Bones is discarded and the effect resolves. The ability that tried to cancel is still used and its cost still paid (rr.cancel). It answers the first cancel only, as it is gone then. Alternative: the cancel happens and Bones is discarded as well; the words "instead" rule it out.
21. **Fatigue** (`recommended`; no ruling: https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Fatigue-EfKD carries the text only): When Revealed, each player exhausts a character he controls (his choice, a ready one if any); until the end of the round player card effects cannot ready any character (`cannotReadyByPlayerCard`; framework readying in the refresh phase still happens). Shadow: the defending character leaves the attack (it stays exhausted: its cost of defending is paid), and the defending player may declare a different character as the defender, who exhausts to defend; he need not, and then the attack is undefended. The removed character cannot be declared again for this attack.
22. **Rock Adder, Cave-troll, The Great Bridge** (`recommended` for The Great Bridge, `literal` for the others): Rock Adder is not defended by any character of any player (no defender can be declared; the attack is undefended); Cave-troll's allies cannot attack or defend (19068:A:1, 143023:A:2). While The Great Bridge is in the staging area, each declaration of attackers against an enemy has one character; another character, even of the same player, may attack the same enemy in a later attack (Pelennor's 145050:A:1 reading: the limit is on one attack). No source on The Great Bridge was found (https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/The-Great-Bridge-RD is another card).
23. **Branching Paths** (`literal`; https://visionofthepalantir.com/2023/06/23/escape-from-khazad-dum/ says only "quite high threat to begin with"): it counts each location in the staging area, itself included when it is there, and gets no more than +3 Threat in all. It gets the bonus wherever it is (staging area, active location). The Threat is read live (Moria's Chamber of Mazarbul reading 8).
24. **The Mountain's Roots** (`literal`, as The Mines of Moria reading 8, Chamber of Mazarbul): its Threat X, and the X of its Travel line, is the stage number of the main quest plus 1, read live. Each player discards that many cards from the top of his deck, one player after another (an empty deck discards what it has, rr.running-out-of-cards).
25. **Deep Deep Dark, Western Hall, Unspeakable Thing, Shadow Dweller** (`literal`; precedent 18057:A:2, 142055:A:2, 19157:A:2, 09034:A:1): "character you control" is any character, ally or hero; X from a discarded card is its printed value, 0 for a card with none, and an empty deck discards nothing (X 0). The damage Shadow Dweller assigns, and Deep Deep Dark's choice, are the defending (or each) player's.
26. **Chieftain of the Pit** (`literal`; precedent 50220:A:2 for the global scope): while it is in play no shadow effect can be canceled, whoever's enemy it is on, not only its own. Nameless Thing and the Elder Nameless Thing follow 18050:A:2 (a guarded attachment under the enemy) and 09026:A:2 (a Forced line it gains while in the staging area). Nameless Thing's guarded card is discarded, to its owner's discard pile, when it leaves play (the printed line says "discard that card"; Hobgoblin returns its card to the hand, so `GuardWithDeckTop` has the field `discards` and the marker verb is `DiscardGuarded`).
27. **Black Uruks** (`literal`; precedent 10129:A:2 for the count): +1 Attack for each shadow card dealt to it for this attack, read live while it attacks, and the extra cards of an additional-shadow effect count. Its Shadow takes the top-5 card named in reading 3 and deals it to the attacking enemy as a shadow card (this does not reveal it). Orc Horn Blower, Orc Raiders and Chieftain's additional shadow cards are dealt one by one (faq.additional-attacks).
28. **Goblin Dungeon** (`recommended`; precedent Carried Away, The Wizard's Quest reading 9, with a location as the host): one ally, the one named in reading 3, lies faceup under Goblin Dungeon, its tokens discarded, still its owner's, out of play and with blank text; it returns exhausted to its owner's control when Goblin Dungeon is explored. If Goblin Dungeon leaves play any other way the ally returns exhausted at once. With no ally in play nothing happens. While it lies there no copy of its title can be played.
29. **Mithril Lode** (`literal`; precedent Amon Forn 09026:A:2 for a line gained while active): while it is the active location it has the Response; when it is explored, each player adds 1 resource to the pool of a hero he controls. The player chooses the hero, so the choice tray asks, naming the owner. A Response is optional: the player may decline.
30. **Other lines** (`literal`; cited): Swarm of Bats and Rock Adder's Shadow as printed; Twisting Passage (51249:A:1, 51152:A:1: "add it to the staging area", not "reveal"); Many Roads (51204:B:1, "different 0-cost location per player", no limit of 2); Silent Caverns (51143:A:1); Orc Raiders (143056:A:1); Chance Encounter (51014:A:1; Shadow: the additional attack comes after this one, faq.additional-attacks); Mithril Lode and Elder Nameless Thing, readings 26 and 29; New Devilry (Woodland 51114: text box blank, Traits kept, rr.blank; the same sentence without "printed"); Dark Places (09058:A:1, the Threat of the active location, surge with none; Shadow X the stage number); Forked Passage (01114:A:1: one more threat at the refresh phase, the same moment as the printed raise); Lightless Grotto's Travel is an effect, not a cost: with no enemy in the staging area nothing is engaged.
31. **Watchful Eyes** (`recommended`; "Limit 1 per hero" is part of the card's own attached text, no source): the opposing team attaches it to the hero with the highest Willpower that has no Watchful Eyes yet; with every hero holding one, none is attached.
32. **Elder Nameless Thing's gained Forced effect** (`recommended`; precedent 51007:B:1, which reads "after the players have committed characters to the quest" as the beginning of the staging step): while it is in the staging area it attacks the first player at the beginning of the staging step.
33. **Black Uruks and the Travel of Lightless Grotto** (`recommended`): +1 Attack for each shadow card is read while the stat is read and only while Black Uruks is the attacking enemy (the flag `attackPerShadow`); Lightless Grotto's Travel engages the chosen enemy with the first player, who chooses it.

## The Hunt for the Dreadnaught

The 2020 print-on-demand scenario (pack `THftD`, RingsDB's code for it; Hall of Beorn lists it under its own name), the fourteenth and last of the cycle, Normal and Easy alike. It is a fleet scenario: each player commands a ship-objective, the Corsair deck feeds Boarding, and the quest ends when the Dreadnaught is destroyed. The sea rules are the Dream-chaser ones (`dcce.ship-objectives`, `dcce.ship-enemies`, `dcce.prepare-your-fleet`, `dcce.corsair-deck`, `dcce.boarding`). The box has no rulesheet in `data/rules` and Hall of Beorn and DragnCards hold no insert; the rules are the three mode cards, the two quest cards and the box rulebook, whose text RulesPal transcribes (https://rulespal.com/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-card-game-the-hunt-for-the-dreadnaught/rulebook) and whose scenario is reviewed at https://visionofthepalantir.com/2022/01/16/the-hunt-for-the-dreadnaught/. No FAQ entry, official ruling or designer answer names the box (the FFG forum thread https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/311373-the-hunt-for-the-dreadnaught holds only speculation from before the release).

- **Data.** RingsDB's own codes are kept (no `productCodes`): `40001` Easy Mode, `40002` Standard Mode, `40003` Hard Mode, `40004` stage 1 (Searching the High Seas), `40005` stage 2 (Destroy the Dreadnaught), `40006` to `40011` the six fleet ships (Eithiliant, Golden Wing, Pride of Lebennin, Silent Mist, Swan Ship of Dol Amroth, Twilight's Call, each with an Upgraded side B), `40012` Gondorian Warship (4 copies), `40013` the Dreadnaught (Elite side A, Damaged side B), `40014` to `40019` the six unique Corsairs (Captain of the Haven, Commander of the Dreadnaught, Descendant of Castamir, Hydraken, Ophidian, Varjax), `40020` to `40025` the 13 non-unique Corsairs of the Corsair deck (Brash Corsair 1, Corsair Plunderer 3, Daring Buccaneer 3, Fierce Marauder 3, Southron Sailor 2, Umbar Captain 1), `40026` to `40032` the seven Corsair ship-enemies (11 copies), `40033` Sea Monster (2), `40034` to `40039` six locations (15), `40040` to `40046` seven treacheries (15) and `40047` the contract A Perilous Voyage (a player card, not dealt). The piles are DragnCards' deck Q99.15 with no Easy twin: its `sharedEncounterDeck` is the 43-card encounter deck (13 ship-enemies and Sea Monsters, 15 locations, 15 treacheries), `sharedEncounterDeck2` the Corsair deck (`corsairDeck`), `sharedQuestDeck` the two stages and `sharedSetAside` the three mode cards, the six ships, the four Gondorian Warships and the seven unique enemies. The product is `ledgerKeywords` and `sidedDragnRows` like the Dream-chaser boxes. Hall of Beorn files Sea Monster as a ship-enemy; its scan prints ENEMY (a Creature), so `overrides.cards` sets the type. Hall of Beorn has no stat fields for ships: the printed stats of the fleet (both sides), the Gondorian Warship, the seven Corsair ships, Sea Monster and the Dreadnaught are `overrides.cards` rows read from the scans (the ledger notes repeat them). The Dreadnaught prints no hit point number on either side, only the global value "15 per player among all groups", so its `health` stays null and a record reads it; its Damaged side has Threat 3, Attack 5, Defense 3 and Victory 99. Stage 1B prints `*` quest points ("a global quest point value of 8 per player among all groups"), so the card takes `questPointsX` and a record sets the number.
- **Mode cards.** The three mode cards are Hall of Beorn's `GenCon_Setup` cards (`campaign` type in the catalog). The scenario's `modeCard` is Standard Mode (`40002`), the cooperative game of one table (1 to 2 seats in the app): `overrides.scenarioExcluded` keeps the mode cards out of the count check, as the Siege's Standard Game Mode is kept, and it stands beside the quest deck after Setup with its back faceup. Easy Mode (`40001`: two Black Sails and a Corsair Warship set aside, Cobas Haven staged and not discarded for, 1 resource per hero, an extra draw in each resource phase, the Quest Action without the reveal) and Hard Mode (`40003`: three Tolfalas Landings staged, threat reduction capped at 1 each round, a threat raise when a character is healed or readied by a player card effect) are in the catalog and the ledger, and the app's Easy difficulty deals Easy Mode and Normal deals Standard Mode (reading 1; Hard Mode is not dealt, `scripts/checks/box-dealt.test.ts`). The Epic Multiplayer Mode (any number of groups of 1 to 4 players, one copy of the box for each, an organizer who tracks stage 1B progress and the Dreadnaught's damage, "any group" in the card text) needs several tables and is not built; "in any group" and "among all groups" read as the one table.
- **Setup (Standard Mode).** Printed, front: "Setup: • Players prepare their fleet. • Prepare the Corsair deck. • Set aside all 7 unique enemies (including the Dreadnaught). • Shuffle the encounter deck and discard cards from the top until 2 locations are discarded (3 locations instead if there are 3 or more players). Add those locations to the staging area. • Flip this card over and place it next to the quest deck." Back: "Quest Action: Deal 10 damage to a non-unique enemy engaged with a player in any group. Reveal the top card of the encounter deck. (Group limit once per game.) If a player controls no ship-objective, that player is eliminated from the game." The build reads the bullets as one paragraph (the bullets are dropped), "Players prepare their fleet" and "Prepare the Corsair deck" as the Dream-chaser steps (the ships are the box's own, so the Dream-chaser's Fleet is dealt for the Dream-chaser scenarios only), "Set aside all 7 unique enemies" as the seven set-aside enemies and the discard sentence as `discardToStaging` with a count of 2 (3 from 3 players).
- **Fleet and Corsair deck (rulebook).** Each player takes control of one of the six unique ship-objectives, Basic side faceup; the unused ships and the four Gondorian Warships stay set aside. Ship-objectives are encounter cards that act as objective allies and attack and defend against ship-enemies only; only ship-objectives defend against a ship-enemy, and an undefended ship-enemy attack's damage goes to a ship-objective the player controls. The 13 Corsair deck cards are non-unique non-Ship Corsair enemies in a separate facedown deck with its own discard pile (reshuffled when the deck runs out) that enter play only through card effects such as Boarding. Boarding X resolves when a Boarding ship-enemy engages a player from the staging area. Pillage X: after the enemy attacks a player, the player discards the top X cards of his deck. Massive: the enemy cannot leave the staging area, is engaged with every player and attacks each player in turn in the combat phase.
