# Ered Mithrin

The rulings, readings and table pieces of Ered Mithrin, scenario by scenario. Product rules, the Domain and the Core round are in `docs/brief.md`; the authority order is in `.claude/rules/authority.md`.

The Ered Mithrin campaign is nine scenarios played in order: The Wilds of Rhovanion (Journey Up the Anduin, Lost in Mirkwood, The King's Quest), the six Adventure Packs (The Withered Heath, Roam Across Rhovanion, Fire in the Night, The Ghost of Framsburg, Mount Gundabad, The Fate of Wilderland) and, for campaign mode, the Ered Mithrin Campaign Expansion (MEC115, rules doc `emce`, RingsDB `EMCE`), which adds the nine campaign cards, the boons, the burdens and the treasures (user decision, 2026-11-05: all nine scenarios; the first two deal the Revised Core sets). The campaign is registered (`data/campaigns/ered-mithrin.json`: `saga` false, no `xp`, `bookletId` `ered-mithrin`, each scenario's page of the book) and its nine scenarios are one box on the Scenarios tab, `ered-mithrin`. Which of them are playable is `src/campaign/playable.ts`; one that is not is coming soon, as in Dream-chaser. There is no XP and no Hard/Expert Mode card: the campaign runs on treasures, boon allies and encounter cards in the pool. Nightmare and the MEC115 Achievements are not built.

- **The log** (`emce.campaign-log`, MEC115 p.26 to 27). Four columns beside the Learn to Play's: the treasures discovered, the four "Unique Allies Protecting Wilderland" lines, the boxes marked beside Beldis for each player (four) and the encounter cards in the Campaign Pool with their copies. The path and the log screen show them, and nothing types a number. The campaign is won by winning the ninth scenario (12195:B, "The players win the campaign!").
- **Treasures** (`emce.treasure`). A treasure is a player card that is deck-legal only through the log (`campaignKind` treasure): at the briefing a discovered one may be in a deck, 1 copy by title, outside the 50 card minimum; an undiscovered one is refused with the rule (`validateLoadout`, `src/campaign/loadout.ts`). A treasure in the hand (The Fate of Wilderland's search adds one) is played from the hand or the discard pile like an attachment, onto the host its Attach to line names (`playsOntoHost`, `src/rules/core/printed.ts`). The saved-deck path does not admit a treasure yet: the deck editor, the import and the Cards pool hold only deck-legal cards, so no saved deck can carry one (open). The verb `AttachRandomTreasure` (the `wilderland` family, `src/catalog/families/wilderland.ts`) draws 3 undiscovered treasures with the game's seed, which advances once, so a replayed game offers the same three; the first player chooses one and it enters the staging area guarded by the card the record names (`rr.guarded`). Journey Up the Anduin claims one outright (`ClaimRandomTreasure`, 120165:A:3): the same draw of 3, the first player chooses the treasure and then the character it is attached to (any character its Attach to line allows), and the players control it from then; a guarded treasure is claimed once nothing guards it (`freeTreasures`, Rulings below). `DiscoverTreasure` runs at the Resolution: each treasure a player controls, claimed and attached, is written to the log; one still guarded in the staging area is not. A claimed treasure is not an attachment for card effects, so a pick of attachments ("discard an attachment", "an Item attachment") never takes it (`recommended`). `emce.treasure` calls it "a new type of player card", its card type is Treasure, and the game prints the exceptions where it means one ("Counts as an attachment" on the Dol Guldur objectives, 01108 to 01110); `hasAttachment.treasure` keeps it separate. No ruling found: read `emce.treasure`, `rr.attachment-cards`, Hall of Beorn's treasure pages (e.g. https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/Durin's-Dagger-EMC, no rulings) and a web search of the Ered Mithrin rules text; the FAQ has no entry.
- **Boon allies** (`emce.boon-allies`). Turayn, Melanwar, Isolde and Beldis each either join the pool or are traded for a line. `BoonAllyOrLogLine` asks the first player for the group, with no default: the ally for a player, named by one of their heroes, or the line (the offer reads "Choose which player takes Turayn, or the line for the log." in a table of several players, who see that the first player chooses, and "Take Turayn, or the line for the log." alone; each choice says what the line removes from the encounter deck at the later Setups that read it, from `ALLY_LINE_FACTS`). The ally is added to the pool for that player and shuffled into their deck; the line is recorded. The ally is fixed to the seat of the player chosen, though the campaign cards also say "any player may add [the ally] to their deck" (120165:B:1, 12168:B:1, 12170:B:1, 12187:B:1) (user ruling, 2026-11-05). Later campaign cards read a line (`RemoveFromEncounterDeckIfLogged`, and the conditions `logged` and `treasuresLogged`, "If there are 6 or more treasures recorded in the campaign log").
- **Encounter cards in the Campaign Pool** (`emce.campaign-pool-encounter`). The log tallies the copies of each card, never more than one collection holds (the catalog's quantity). `AddEncounterToPool` adds a copy of each card named, or with `inPlay` a copy for each card of the code in play that the pool did not itself deal into the game; `RemoveFromPool` takes every copy of the cards named out, or lets each player in turn remove one non-burden card of the pool, a player's pool card or an encounter card, or decline. When the checklist builds an encounter deck it shuffles in the noted copies of each card, whatever its encounter set (step 8), and a standalone game adds none. `SetAsideDiscardForPool` (A Growing Threat, 12167:A:2) sets aside the topmost non-unique, non-objective, non-burden card of the encounter discard pile and appends an `AddEncounterToPool` for it to the Resolution; "during quest resolution" is the scenario's Resolution on a win, and a loss adds nothing (user ruling, 2026-11-05).
- **Setup removals.** `RemoveFromEncounterDeckIfLogged`: when the log records the line, the first player searches the encounter deck for up to N cards of a type, non-unique when the card says so, and removes them from the game (`rr.search`: a deck with fewer gives what it has). The briefing asks one card at a time, a title with several copies named again for each; the pool's cards and the cards the campaign card shuffled in are searched too. A campaign card's Setup line that counts the treasures holds by the log (`src/campaign/wilderland.ts`, `logConditionHolds`).
- **Rulings (user ruling, 2026-11-05).** A treasure is a player card for every "player card effect" (immunity to player card effects, Lost in the Wild). Haldan (18070:A:1) is controlled by the first player while he is free of encounters, so he passes to the new first player when the token moves. "Any player gains control of Turayn" (120165:A:2): the first player, with no question. "Any player gains control of Melanwar" (12168:A:2): the first player, with no question (user ruling, 2026-11-05). When the treasure Twilight Hall guards is free of encounters, the first player picks the character it attaches to, as `ClaimRandomTreasure` does (user ruling, 2026-11-05); the engine claims any treasure in the staging area once nothing guards it (`freeTreasures`, `src/rules/families/wilderland/mirkwood.ts`), a settle sweep that opens that choice. "Cannot be discarded by player card effects" (Lost in the Wild, 18026:A:1) bars a player card's effect and a player card's discard cost, picked or random; hand-size and game-rule discards can discard it. The first player names each treachery for "search the encounter deck for 3 treacheries". Deep does nothing once its location has left play by another route, but a location its own Response added to the victory display (Forked Passage, 18055:A:2: "before resolving the deep keyword") is replaced all the same by the top card of the Caves deck, which becomes the active location (agent recommendation, the likelier reading of that sentence; no ruling found); a non-location Deep replacement other than a Dragon Sign goes to the staging area; Discover discards the other looked-at cards before the Hazard's reveal resolves; Guarded (X) with no match leaves the objective unguarded.
- **Lost in Mirkwood** (part 2, 12168). The four Objective-Locations of Searching for a Way Out (18029 to 18032) are objectives that count as locations: the players travel to them, they take progress as the active location (4 quest points, read from the scan; `data/overrides.json`) and an explored one goes to the victory display with its Victory 4 (`rr.victory`, user ruling, 2026-11-05), while they stay objectives for Guarded, the staging area and immunity (`printed.objectiveLocation`, `isLocationCard`). "Advance to the next stage A / C" is `AdvanceToLetterStage`; Haldan, dealt set aside, comes under the first player at stage 1's Setup (`TakeControlOfSetAside`); the Spiders of Mirkwood and Dol Guldur Orcs sets wait in their piles until stage 2 shuffles one in; the Resolution discards cards until X of those sets' cards are discarded and adds them to the pool (`DiscardToPoolFromSets`). X is the number of players still in the game ("one less player" for an eliminated seat, span-settled by `rr.player-elimination`), so a two-handed game that lost a seat adds 1, and the deck running dry is a different, rarer end. Progress on the quest while no Objective-Location is in play is discarded from the encounter deck and then the topmost Objective-Location of the discard pile enters play (`progressDiscards` and `PutObjectiveLocationAfterDiscard`). Stages 1 to 3 print no quest points: the quest advances only through the Objective-Locations, and "when the players advance from this stage" is the stage's `questCardDefeated` (a jump past stages names the stage it left, `stageLeft`). Stage 3A, Carried Away (18074): each player's chosen hero loses its tokens and attachments and lies facedown under the enemy discarded to (`CarryAwayHero`, the captive zone: in play, controlled by no one, `guardedHero`); a player is not eliminated while an enemy holds their only hero (`rr.player-elimination`: heroes killed); the enemies guarding a hero cannot take non-combat damage; a guard that leaves play in any way, defeated or discarded, returns the hero to its owner's control, exhausted (`ReleaseGuardedHero`, heard as `leftPlay`; user ruling, 2026-11-05), and advancing discards the heroes still held (`DiscardGuardedHeroes`). An enemy attacking a seat with no hero in play (every hero held) is undefended and deals no damage. "Guarding a card" on Unseen Danger's Shadow (18036:A:2) covers an enemy guarding an objective or a hero. Stage 3C, Truly Lost (18075): after each player has discarded to a non-objective location, each player's hand lies facedown under a different location of the staging area (an Objective-Location is one; `PlaceHandUnderLocation`, the actor chooses); a location gets +1 quest point for each facedown player card under it (`eachAttached` with `facedownUnder`), returns them to their owners' hands when explored in the staging area or as the active location; a location that leaves play unexplored leaves them facedown, out of hand, and advancing discards every facedown player card under a location (user ruling, 2026-11-05). A player may place their hand under any staging-area location with no hand under it yet (user ruling, 2026-11-05). Stage 4A, The Forest of Great Fear (18076, 8 quest points): the enemy with the most printed hit points in the set that stayed set aside enters the staging area as the nemesis, the first player choosing among a tie (`SearchNemesis`, `nemesis` until it leaves play); it gets +2 Threat, Attack and Defense, is immune to player card effects, cannot take damage with fewer than 8 progress on the stage, blocks the stage while in play, returns to the staging area and attacks the first player at the end of each round (`NemesisAttacks`), and defeating it wins the game. Melanwar's "if you quested unsuccessfully this round" (12169:A:2) is the `questFailed` condition: the seat's quest failed this round (willpower below the staging area's threat; a tie is not unsuccessful, user ruling, 2026-11-05: a quest with nothing committed against Threat above 0 is unsuccessful, against 0 Threat it is a tie and neither; `resolveQuest`), kept in `quested.failed` in a game whose cards read it. A hero whose owner was eliminated while it was held goes to that owner's discard pile when its guard leaves play instead of returning (`rr.player-elimination`); the cards returned from under a location are faceup in the hand again; only the facedown hands under a location are discarded on the advance, not a card attached facedown under another card. On the phone, each is cited to its ledger line (`docs/design-system-table.md`, Lost in Mirkwood and Choice tray): the preview of an enemy holding a hero and that seat's held dial (18074:B:2), of a location with a hand under it (18075:B:2, 18075:B:3) and of the nemesis (18076:B:1 to 18076:B:3), the Twilight Hall search offer and the claim of the freed treasure (12168:A:2), and an Objective-Location's quest-point badge and travel row (18029:A:2).
- **The King's Quest** (part 3, 12170). The campaign card's Setup: hero changes cost no threat (`WaiveThreatPenalty`), Haldan may be a hero (`HERO_BANS` leaves part 3 out), Isolde comes under the first player (the standing ruling), The Threat to Wilderland enters the staging area with X resources, X the campaign card's part number (`AddToStagingWithResources`, `amount: { part }`; a game of the third part on holds the part, earlier parts leave it unset so their state is unchanged), Dagnir's Spawn is shuffled into the encounter deck, Abandoned Ruin into the Caves deck (`ShuffleIntoPile` with `placed`), and the Turayn oath removes 3 treacheries. Its Resolution adds Abandoned Ruin to the pool and the first player chooses The Threat to Wilderland or Dagnir's Spawn for it (burdens, so `Earn` and `ChooseCards`). Caves deck (`emce.caves-deck`): "Look at the top 2 cards ... put one on top and the other on the bottom" is `ArrangeCaves`, the player who triggers the Response choosing the card that stays on top; Forked Passage (18055:A:2) does it as a Response before the Deep keyword and then goes to the victory display; Deep still resolves afterwards, so the top Caves card replaces it as the active location and nothing is discarded (the Deep reading above), and Contested Depths (18045:A:2) replaces itself with the top Caves card (`ReplaceFromCaves`). Stage 1 (18078): the first player decides between adding the discarded location to the staging area and every player raising their threat by its printed Threat; it cannot be defeated without 3 locations that print victory points in the victory display. Stage 2 (18079) sets Dragon Hoard at the bottom of the Caves deck, Fire-drake in the staging area (it cannot be engaged or take damage) and the Dragon Might set in the encounter deck; the Response (18079:B:2) is the first player's alone and removes 5 progress for the Caves look (`recommended`; https://hallofbeorn.com/LotR/Details/The-Lower-Depths). Stage 3 (18080): the first player chooses a Dragon treachery from the deck or discard pile for Fire-drake (`AttachSearched`); "declares X attackers" is the attack as a whole, once for each declared attack (`enemyAttacked`, X the characters that attacked), and the player removes X progress or the attack deals no damage (`CancelStrike`; the attackers stay exhausted); the removal is offered only with at least X progress on the quest (user ruling, 2026-11-05). Dragon Hoard (18043:A:3): the chosen player's Artifact attachment from hand lies in the staging area guarded by a location found as Guarded (location) does, and once nothing guards it the first player claims it for a character, as for a treasure (user ruling, 2026-11-05: "Staging, guarded; when freed the first player claims it for a character"); the attachment is not attached to Dragon Hoard first (user ruling, 2026-11-05). Hobgoblin (18050): the first player's top deck card lies faceup under it (held) and returns to its owner's hand once the enemy is out of play; "that card's cost" is its printed cost, 0 for an X cost, and X reads whatever card Hobgoblin guards, an objective it guards or a player card it took (`guardedCost`; user ruling, 2026-11-05). Stone-troll (18049:A:2): the excess is the damage dealt beyond the character's remaining hit points at the time, its current hit points with their modifiers less the damage already on it (user ruling, 2026-11-05; `combatDamage` notes the character's hit points and the event carries the damage left beyond them as X, `excessOf`, read when the attack ends, so damage a Response canceled does not count), so a +1 hit point attachment makes one point less. Dragon Scales (18059:A:2) cancels the damage as it would be placed and is then discarded. Lightless Grotto (18063:A:1) acts as the staging step begins, which is when the commit step ends. The Threat to Wilderland (12172:A:1, 12170:A:2) enters with X = 3 resources (user ruling, 2026-11-05), and Dagnir's Spawn (12173:A:2), when a player distributes the damage, is shuffled into the encounter deck only: it does not also enter the staging area (user ruling, 2026-11-05). The Threat to Wilderland removes a resource at the beginning of the quest phase and, only when one was removed (`rr.then`), the first player chooses the threat or the immunity of every encounter card for the phase. A location that prints X quest points (Underground Lake 18065, Hills of Wilderland 18023: the number of characters controlled by the player with the most characters) carries `keywords.questPointsX` in the data (`questPoints` stays null) and its record sets the value, so it takes progress as the active location and is explored when it holds X (`hasQuestPoints`; user decision, 2026-11-05); the pinned King's Quest wins still keep the Lake in the Caves deck (the stage 2 look sends it to the bottom). On the phone, each is cited to its ledger line (`docs/design-system-table.md`, The King's Quest and Choice tray): the preview of Hobgoblin with the card it holds (18050:A:2), of an Artifact guarded in the staging area (18043:A:3) and of The Threat to Wilderland with its resources (12172:A:2), the shield Dragon Scales gives its enemy (18059:A:2, a flag badge), and the choices that name the number, the side and the player ("Player 2: choose a card from your discard pile to return to your hand.", the cards Stone-troll's excess discards, the Caves look, the threat of Contested Depths, of the discarded location and the attackers' progress).
- **The Withered Heath** (part 4, 12175). The campaign card's Setup sets the four Accolade boons aside (12176 to 12179); each player in turn, the first player first, may search their deck for a unique boon ally (a pool ally the checklist is about to shuffle into it is in the deck) and add it to their hand, and the offer ends with the first who takes one (`SearchBoonAllyToHand`, `src/campaign/checklist.ts`); Setup follows the opening hand and the mulligan (`rr.setup` step 7), so the ally joins the hand after the mulligan. With Melanwar's line the first player removes 2 non-unique locations from the encounter deck and 1 location from the Caves deck the scenario deals (`RemoveFromCavesIfLogged`, the Lost Caves set: a pooled Abandoned Ruin is shuffled into the Caves deck only after this Setup), with Isolde's 3 non-unique enemies. The back of the card stays in the campaign zone all game: after a main quest stage is defeated each player in turn order who controls no Accolade boon may attach that stage's boon to one of their heroes (Limit 1 per hero; only one copy of each exists, so once taken it is not offered again), and for stage 4 the defeat of Cold-Drake is the stage's defeat: the boon is offered first and the win comes at the end of that phase, because the engine closes a won game before it announces the events behind it. The Resolution adds each Accolade boon in play to the pool on its hero (`EarnAttached`), lets each player choose one Song boon for the pool (each Song once), and with 4 Dragon Signs in the victory display the first player chooses 1 of 3 random undiscovered treasures (`ChooseRandomTreasure`: drawn once with the game's seed; the scenario is over, so the character it is attached to is not asked, and `DiscoverTreasure` records it). Reading (put to the user): the three leave out every treasure dealt in this game, claimed or not, as well as those the log holds, so a treasure still guarded in the staging area is never among them (`drawTreasures`, `src/rules/families/wilderland/treasure.ts`).
- **Dragon Signs and Cold-Drake** (`emce.dragon-signs`, 19011 to 19025). A Dragon Sign is a Guarded (enemy) objective: the enemy found is attached to it as its guard, immune to player card effects; once nothing guards it a settle sweep adds it to the victory display (`VictoryWhenFree`; with no enemy left to guard it, it is free at once). Creature Den searches the Caves deck for one and guards it itself (`AttachGuardedFromPile`); a Dragon Sign that replaces the active location through Deep is added to the staging area and resolves Guarded. A Travel effect is a cost of traveling, resolved before the location becomes the active location (`rr.travel`), so High Falls is still in the staging area and counts itself in X; the first player assigns its damage among all characters in play. Stages 1 and 3 shuffle 2 set-aside Dragon Signs into the Caves deck; the look at the top 3 cards of the encounter deck reveals 1 and discards the rest first (as Discover does), the first player choosing. Stage 2 makes Cold-Drake behave as the Watcher in the Water does (indestructible, engaged with each player from the staging area, no Threat), takes at most 6 damage a round and never has more than 18 (`damageCeiling`); stage 3 sets it aside keeping its damage (`SetAsideInPlay` with `keepDamage`; the set-aside card loses 3 damage each round end) and stage 4 brings it back with that damage, attacking each player in turn order, at most as much damage a round as there are Dragon Signs in the victory display, returning to the staging area to attack again at the beginning of each quest phase; stage 4 prints 0 quest points, so it holds until Cold-Drake lies in the victory display. Mountain Pass keeps the Weather treacheries' When Revealed effects from being canceled while it is in the staging area (`UncancelableWhenRevealed`). Heavy Snow's first player chooses the character it exhausts; Deadly Cold asks each player, for each ready character they control, whether it is exhausted or takes 1 damage (`ExhaustOrDamageEachReady`).
- **The Withered Heath rulings (user ruling, 2026-11-05).** (1) High Falls (19019:A:3): the first player, the Travel's actor, assigns all X damage among any players' characters, one point at a time (`rulings.test.ts`). (2) Heavy Snow (19020:A:2): the first player chooses the one ready character in play that is exhausted, any player's; until the end of the round no player card effect readies a character, and that holds for a character that enters play later that round (`cannotReadyByPlayerCard` on the class of characters, `rr.lasting-effects`; a card of the encounter deck or the refresh phase still readies). (3) Dragon-slayer (12175:B:5): when Cold-Drake dies (stage 4 defeated) each player with no Accolade boon is offered Dragon-slayer in turn order, the first player first, and the game is won at the end of that phase. (4) The look at the top 3 cards (19022:B:1, 19024:B:1): the other looked cards are discarded first, then the chosen card is revealed. (5) Creature Den (19018:A:1): the Dragon Sign it brings is guarded by Creature Den only, its own Guarded (enemy) is not resolved. (6) The Melanwar line (12175:A:2): the Caves removal reads the scenario's own Caves cards (the Lost Caves set); a pooled Abandoned Ruin is shuffled in after and cannot be removed (`campaign.test.ts`, `golden.test.ts`). (7) A Cornered Dragon (19025): the stage prints 0 quest points, and it cannot be defeated until Cold-Drake lies in the victory display. A Cold-Drake at 18 damage is not destroyed when stage 2's protection lapses as Hunting the Beast is revealed: it is set aside with its damage (`heldBySettingAside`, `src/rules/families/wilderland/heath.ts`: the stage's When Revealed record names the enemy it sets aside with its damage, so no other scenario is touched).

- **The Accolade and Song boons** (12176 to 12183). Master of the Hunt's player chooses the player whose top 3 cards are looked at; that player keeps 1 on top and chooses the order of the rest at the bottom (`ArrangeLookedDeck`). The Canny Huntsman asks which of the top 5 encounter cards go to the bottom, in the order chosen, and the rest are put back on top in any order (the first choice is the cards for the bottom, so the complement is the cards for the top). Unbroken is any player's, once each round for each player. Dragon-slayer's bearer engages the enemy and is attacked (`ImmediateAttackBy`). - **Roam Across Rhovanion** (part 5, 12184). The campaign card's Setup: a player may search their deck for a unique boon ally, with Melanwar's line the first player removes 3 non-unique enemies other than Grey Mountain Giant from the encounter deck (`RemoveFromEncounterDeckIfLogged` with `except`), and once the quest card's Setup has put Urdug under the first player (`TakeControlOfSetAside`, `afterSetup`) the first player puts their trust in Urdug (`PutTrust`: a resource on him, `PlaceResourceTokens`) or a copy of Urdug's Cunning is shuffled into the encounter deck. The back of the card stays in the campaign zone: the same question after the first player claims an objective, Urdug gets +1 Willpower, Threat, Attack and Defense for each resource on him (either side, `resourcesOn`), and Deep is ignored. A claim is any of the three ways the players gain an objective: Tiny and Urdug gain control (`GainControlWhenFree` with `claim`, `rr.objective-cards`: "If a player claims an objective, then that player gains control of that objective"), Durin's Key is attached to a hero the first player chooses and Urdug's Horn to Urdug once nothing guards them (`ClaimWhenFree`, a settle sweep); each is announced as `claimed`. The Resolution: with 3 or more resources on Urdug each player earns Misplaced Trust, shuffled into their deck after the starting hand (`Earn` with `afterHand`), else Urdug's Cunning; then each player in turn, the first player first, chooses a location in the staging area, a different title from the players before them if able, and a copy of it joins the pool's encounter cards (`ChooseStagedLocationForPool`; a burden location is not offered, Abandoned Ruin joins as a burden). The stages: stage 1 sets Urdug under the first player with the other objectives set aside by the piles, and its active location explored with the first player controlling him advances the quest to a random stage 2; the three stages 2 are played in a random order, each once, and a defeated one leaves the game (`AdvanceToRandomStage`, copied onto the stage as `randomNext`, the game's seed; the quest then goes to stage 3). Each searches the encounter deck and discard pile for its guard (Grey Moorland, Grey Mountain Giant, Deep Ravine), adds it to the staging area and attaches the set-aside objective (Tiny, Urdug's Horn, Durin's Key) to it as a guarded objective; a search that finds no copy still adds the objective, unguarded, to be claimed at once. The players cannot travel to the Moorland guarding Tiny or the Ravine guarding the Key without 5 progress on the stage (`Restrict` travel with `guarding`), the Giant guarding the Horn takes no damage without it, and no stage 2 is defeated unless the players control what its line names. Stage 3 flips Urdug and Tiny to their enemy sides in the staging area, removing damage and keeping resources and attachments (`FlipToEnemySide`); quest progress cannot be placed from questing; a failed quest places the amount the threat would have risen by on the stage instead of raising threat (the Forced effect hears the failed quest, whose event carries the amount as X, waives the threat and `AddStageProgress`), and the players lose at 5 progress; the stage is never defeated by progress, and the players win when Urdug has as much damage as hit points (Indestructible keeps him in play). While Urdug is engaged he attacks at once at the beginning of the quest phase, and if that attack damages a character (`damagedByAttack`, `characterDestroyedByAttack`, whose events name the attacking enemy) Urdug and Tiny return to the staging area; while Tiny is in play Urdug cannot take damage. Deep Ravine's cap is on all the locations in the staging area together (`progressCapShared`). ## Encounter card readings

- **Lost in Mirkwood encounter cards.** Unseen Danger (18036:A:1) discards the character it removed from the quest when the printed Threat of the card it discarded is greater than that character's Willpower, not the discarded encounter card. While Accursed Forest (18038:A:1) is in the staging area only 'when revealed' effects cannot be canceled (`rr.cancel`): a Shadow effect can still be canceled.
- **Lost in Mirkwood stages 2A and 2C** (18072:A:1, 18073:A:1). The stage shuffles the encounter deck even when its encounter set has no card left to add, and that shuffle counts as resolved, so the players still reveal 1 encounter card per player (`rr.then`; `recommended`, agent recommendation, the same reading as Hazardous Terrain in `docs/scenarios/shadows-of-mirkwood.md`; Caleb Grace, ALeP 3.114 and 3.119, https://alongextendedparty.com/semi-official-errata-faq-1-10/, speak of effects that target nothing, not of this shuffle).
- **Gondolin Shield and Helm of Heroes** (12201:A:3, 12202:A:3). After Gondolin Shield cancels a shadow effect, the new shadow card is flipped and resolved in the same attack, and its 1 resource comes from any resource pool its controller controls, of any sphere (Caleb Grace, ALeP 3.174 and 3.359). Helm of Heroes triggers only on damage dealt by the enemy's attack strength, not on damage from a shadow effect (Caleb Grace, ALeP 3.287). `researched`, https://alongextendedparty.com/semi-official-errata-faq-1-10/.
- **Wormsbane** (19143:A:1). The Setup's attach follows `rr.restricted` like any attach: a hero that already holds 2 Restricted attachments discards one of the three at once, the choice of the hero's controller (`docs/brief.md`, Keywords implemented once).
