# Design system — Table (landscape)

The table screen's design. The rest of the design system (`docs/design-system.md`: tokens, Standards, Performance, Components, States) applies here too; this file only adds what is the table's own.

## Overview

The table is played in landscape only (`orientation` on its stack screen), with the status bar hidden (as on every screen: `docs/design-system.md`, Navigation) and the stack's edge swipe off (a drag from the hand's edge must not leave the table; Leave the table in the menu does). Scene `table`: the canvas is transparent over its fog, with no felt and no zone boxes. Under the top bar, the canvas at the left and the command column at the right (30% of the width, 210 to 280 pt), both inside the side safe-area insets. The web build draws it with CanvasKit once that has loaded (the reader table until then, and if it never loads), and the desktop app is that build.

## Top bar

Code: `src/screens/table/top-bar.tsx`, `src/play/chrome/top-bar-layout.ts`.

52 pt (`TOP_BAR`: the 46 pt dial with 3 pt above and below), under the top safe-area inset and inside both side ones, starting at least 8 pt under the screen's top edge (`BAR_TOP`) so a dial's pop stays on the screen. Where a status bar stays (an iPad), the bar starts right under its inset, counted once: the table's `Screen` bleeds and the table insets itself. Three zones and nothing else: the threat dials at the left, the phase rail centred on the screen, one menu button at the right.

- **The threat dial** (`ThreatRing` compact, `table-chrome.tsx`): 46 pt round, a ring of threat red (`ArcRing`) filling toward 50, the elimination level (`rr.threat-elimination-level`), or toward 99 while the main quest has Dire (`mec113.dire`; The Black Gate Opens: `seatDialLimit` in `threat-level.ts`, `limits` on the bar by seat, the warming at its fractions, the threat panel "of 99", and the words "a player is eliminated at 99"; a seat holding The One Ring reads 45 the same way), on a faint red track, around a dark disc holding the number alone in Marcellus SC 20, with a red glow. A tick on the ring at each staging enemy's engagement cost (not one that cannot be engaged), bone where the threat has reached it and faint where not; once stage 2B has split the table each dial reads its own seat's staging area (`dialTicksSelector`), and its name counts that seat's enemies. A level the threat is kept above (`notch` on `ThreatRing`, `HandMark.notch`; The Passing of the Grey Company's starting level on stage 2B) is a notch: a short bone bar drawn across the arc at that value, longer than the engagement dots and a bar where they are dots, with its words in the spoken label. It warms near elimination (`src/play/readouts/threat-level.ts`): from 40 the ring turns danger; from 45 the disc fills red (at the same fractions of 99 under Dire) and a screen reader hears "near elimination". A seat that left the game shows the word "Out" on its dimmed disc in place of the number (the spoken label says "eliminated"). The dial is one control whose name says how many engage at this threat and, while it shows, the change. When the number changes the dial pops (`dialPopRise` to 1.22, then `dialPopSettle`) and a badge floating over the disc's foot (position absolute, no touch, above the dial, its foot ending inside the bar's foot (`badgeFoot`) so the bar never clips it; it takes no room, so the bar's height never changes) says the change for three seconds, "−6" on an accent wash when it fell, "+2" on a threat-red wash when it rose, read as "Threat lowered by 6, now 24"; reduced motion keeps the dial still and shows the badge. Dials stand 10 pt apart, each with its seat's label (P1). Life, then the Burden deck (labelled "Burden", read as "Burden deck"), are the same dial with no meter and no levels, when declared. The pursuit dial of The Uruk-hai (labelled "Pursuit", testID `pursuit`, read as "Pursuit value 27, the players lose at 30"; `widgets.pursuitTracker`, `usePursuitReadout`) follows Life: the threat dial's ring filling toward the value at which the players lose, 30, warming from 24 (the ring turns danger) and solid red from 27 ("near the limit"), with the change badge and no ticks (`threatLevel` and `threatMeter` take the limit). The Defense dial of Helm's Deep (labelled "Tokens", testID `defense-tokens`, read as "Resource tokens on The Defense of Helm's Deep 6, 2 more and the players advance to stage 5"; `widgets.defenseTracker`, `useDefenseTokens`, `readouts/fortress.ts`) follows Pursuit while the objective is in play: the tokens on it against the number the record ends it at, 8 (143033:A:3), and the stage its record advances to, both read from the record. Reaching 8 is the players' way out, so the dial never warms: its ring fills toward the limit in the accent (`goal` on `ThreatRing`), the disc and glow in the accent too, and a screen reader never hears "near the limit"; it has the change badge, no ticks and no held dial. The Road to Isengard's dials (`useScenarioDials`, `readouts/scenario-dials.ts`: the three scenarios' dials, `readouts/isengard.ts`, testID `isengard-<id>`) follow Defense; a dial with a `limit` always draws its ring, filling toward it (`elimination` only adds the levels and the ticks). "Reveals" while stage 1B is current (its resource tokens, read as the encounter cards revealed on advancing), "Damage" while stage 2B is (its damage against the 5 that advance the players, a goal dial like Defense), "Ents" while an Ent of Fangorn in the staging area holds a resource (one dial for the Ent closest to control: the most resources against the 3 that give a player control, a goal dial; its spoken words list each Ent's count) and "Gate · HP" while the Staff is on Saruman (a goal dial: the accent ring fills with the damage toward his hit points as the engine reads them, the disc keeps the hit points left, read as "Saruman's hit points left 4, the players cannot win while any are left", and "the gate is open, so defeating the stage wins" at 0). On stage 3B each seat's cards in hand are on that seat's own threat dial, not a dial of their own (`useSeatMarks`, `HandMark`; the bar's width is spent on scenario dials already): the label reads "P1 · 3 cards" (solo, "3 cards"), "P1 · last card" at 1 and "P1 · out" at 0, and at 1 or 0 the disc wears a danger edge (`warn` on `ThreatRing`, the worse of its level and the threat's own) and a screen reader hears "playing or discarding it eliminates this player" or "this player is eliminated" after the label. The limits come from the records (143079:B:2, 143046:A:1) and no dial types a number. On Nightmare the Eye of Sauron follows them, in threat red with the word Nightmare beside it (always: the peeked seat's eye mark is another eye), with the dials' pressed fill; a tap opens the setup card's preview (`nightmare-card`). The Island Map chip (The Island Map) follows the seat dials, and the Heading dial (below) the chip.
- **The Heading dial** (`HeadingDial`, `table-chrome.tsx`; `src/play/dream-chaser/heading.ts`): in the top bar after the seat dials, while the Heading card is in play (`heading`; `dcce.heading`). It is the Life dial's frame, 46 pt, with no number and the label "Heading", in a label column as wide as the widest of "Heading", "Toward Sun" and "Toward Storm" (`HEADING_LABEL_W`), so the dial's width, and with it the rail, does not move when the badge comes and goes. While the dials would scroll, or would take the rail below `RAIL_WIDE`, the dial is compact (`headingCompact`): the disc alone, "Heading" left to the spoken label, and the badge laid over the disc's foot; otherwise the badge floats under the label and takes no room. The disc holds the current step's icon (Sun, Cloud, Rain, Storm: the MEC111 rulebook's own icons, cut from its embedded font by `pnpm data sync icons`), and the ring is four quarter arcs lit clockwise from the top: Sun four, Cloud three, Rain two, Storm one. Sun is the accent, Cloud and Rain bone, Storm threat red with the disc filled; the icon's shape and the arcs carry the step without the colour. `heading-value` is clear text with the step's word ("Rain"), as `rail-current` is. It is heard as "Heading, Rain, off-course, two steps from on-course" with the hint "Shows the Heading card". A shift pops the dial as the threat dial pops and a badge (`heading-delta`) says the direction of the shift, "Toward Sun" on an accent wash or "Toward Storm" on a threat-red wash, for three seconds (a Storm-to-Rain shift is still off-course, so the badge words the direction and not the state), heard as "Heading shifted off-course, now Rain"; reduced motion keeps the dial still and shows the badge. A tap opens the Heading card's preview, titled "Heading · Rain (off-course)", listing the four steps with the current one marked and drawing the card turned a quarter turn per step; a peek never changes the dial, and a peek at the other seat leaves it where it is. There is no stepper.
- **The Battle of Five Armies' stage dials** (`readouts/the-hobbit/armies.ts`; `docs/scenarios/the-hobbit/the-battle-of-five-armies.md`; test ids `armies-stage-2`, `armies-stage-3`, `armies-stage-4`; ledger lines 132076:B:2, 132077:B:3, 132078:B:3, 132079:B:2): one goal dial after the seat dials for each of the three stages in play together, labelled "Stage 2", "Stage 3" and "Stage 4", with its progress against its 8 quest points. The label drops with the other scenario dials' words; the note says "Current stage", the Battle or Siege it prints and how many points are to go. A stage with no progress warms to near, and its warning says the Forced effect of the end of the round. A tap shows the stage card. No dial shows on stage 5.
- **Two seats**: the labels are P1 and P2, read number first as "Threat 28, P1, acting" and "Threat 33, P2, viewing" (a solo dial: "Threat 20"; `dialSpeech`, `peek.ts`). The seat dials are drawn at Large's size at most (`dialScale`, `top-bar-layout.ts`), since at Extra large two seats' full-size dials scroll on a 667 pt phone and at Large's size they fit. The acting seat's label is in the accent. A tap on the dial of a seat that is not shown peeks at it (hint "Shows Player 2's cards", "Shows Player 2's area" once stage 2B has split the table; Peeking); a tap on the shown seat's dial opens its threat panel (Reading panels). A seat peeked at that is not acting wears a bone ring just outside its dial, a small eye mark at its top-right (`threat-<seat>-viewing`, `threat-<seat>-eye`, drawn as views) and a bone label; neither touches the Ring mark in the label row, which keeps its place at 667 × 375 with two seats. Only the acting seat's dial pulses (Seat handoff); a peek never does.
- **Held marks**: while the Ring-bearer is facedown under a card, the seat that gets it back (always the first player) wears the printed One Ring, 12 pt, in the accent beside its label, and the dial's name says "The Ring-bearer is facedown under Watcher in the Water" (`threat-<seat>-ring`). The seat whose hero is the prisoner of Escape from Dol Guldur wears the printed hero icon in bone in the same slot, and its dial says "Aragorn is the prisoner, face down" (`heldDial`, `src/play/readouts/widgets.ts`). The seat guarding Gollum in Return to Mirkwood (02126:A:1, `sotm.guarding-gollum`) wears the hero mark too, and its dial says "Player 2 is guarding Gollum" and, while a card says the guard cannot change (The Spiders' Ring, Isolation), "and the guard cannot change" (`guardianHeld`, `src/play/readouts/widgets.ts`); the Gollum card itself stands in that seat's play-area row like any ally.
- **The phase rail** (`PhaseRail`, `src/play/phases/phase-rail.tsx`): no pill and no edge. The phase in Marcellus SC 17 tracked 4 with a silver glow, and the round's seven phases as ticks under it (22 × 3, 5 apart: past accentLine, current accentHigh and glowing, to come dark). Outside the seven the phase reads Beginning or End; Setup and the mulligan have no phase, so their step stands in its place. The round and the step are not in the rail at all (the carousel names the round); the rail's label says them ("Planning, round 1": the phase as drawn, the round, the step only when it is not the phase, then the stop note), and `rail-current` is the headline only when the step stands in the phase's place. When the rail is wider than `RAIL_WIDE`, the previous and next phase names stand inline at the phase's sides in muted label, in equal slots so the phase stays on the rail's middle; they change in place. An accent S follows the phase while Stops are on and one of the player's stops lies ahead in this phase (`rail-stop-ahead`); its room is kept on both sides. A phase change brightens the glow (`railFlash`, 300 ms), which snaps under reduced motion, and what is on the table when it opens appears without fading.
- **Rail layout**: the rail is centred on the screen, as wide as it can be without reaching the last dial or the menu button, and never under `RAIL_FLOOR`, computed from its own words (the widest phase, and Setup and Mulligan, whose step stands in the phase's place) and ticks; a test checks every one against it. When the dials need more room than that leaves them (two seats with Life and the Burden deck on a phone), the dials scroll sideways behind a seven-band fade at the left zone's right edge, rather than the rail leaving the middle, and the row scrolls to keep the Heading dial and its badge whole inside the zone, clear of the fade (`headingOffset`; at Large on a 667 pt phone with two seats the first seat's dial is partly scrolled out, and the row scrolls back to it). The dials then keep their names in sight by moving each from beside its ring to a caption under it (`captioned`, `dialZones`), the seat dials' "P1" and "P2" included (accent while the seat is the one asked): a `text-micro` pill in night at 88% that hangs from the dial row's foot by `badgeFoot` as the change badge does, so it ends inside the bar's foot, over the disc's lower part under the number and the ring's foot, and never reaches the cards below the bar. It is never wider than the dial and the gap to its neighbour less 2 pt (`CAPTION_WORDS`, 9 letters), so two captions never touch. The caption takes no room in the row and no tap, and it gives way to the change badge for the three seconds the badge stands in its place. Its words are the label when they fit, else the dial's `short` name (`TableDial.short`: "Aside" for Out of play, "Battle" for Battlefield, "Round" for This round, "Losing" for Losing Time), else the label up to its " · " (`captionOf`). The row then holds the rings alone (five 54 pt dials and four gaps, 310 pt, fit the 844 pt Crown row), the zones are those of the row with the names beside the rings, so the rail keeps its size, and only a row too wide even so scrolls (a 667 pt phone): a caption always stands under its own ring, a fade at the right edge says a dial lies out of view there, and a fade at the left edge says so once the row is scrolled. Where the names fit beside the rings (a solo table, a tablet) they stay beside them; only when the menu side cannot hold half the floor does the rail move. Until the bar and the dials are measured the rail is held invisible, so it does not jump when the table opens.
- A tap on the rail opens the phase carousel and a second tap closes it; the rail is heard as a button that expands. The rail and a dial show a faint bone fill while pressed or open.
- **The menu button** is the round glass button (`GlassIconButton`, `glassSurface`), 44 pt, with three bone dots drawn as views (accent while the menu is open), label "Menu" (`table-menu`).

## Spacing and the play area's rows

Code: `layout.ts`; shares and caps in `table-spacing.ts`, all pure and tested.

The rows are laid out at their minimum gaps first (the 8 pt gutter, 6 pt gaps, 10 pt peeks, Medium's sizes times the UI scale). The room left between the play area's one row and a gap above the hand, less a 12 pt slack (times the scale) that is never shared, goes to four gaps by shares, each capped at a share of the card's height: above staging 0.1 (cap 0.15), staging to the engagement area 0.2 (cap 0.3), the engagement area to the play area 0.35 (cap 0.4), the play area to the hand 0.35 (cap 0.4). A capped gap's overflow goes to the others; what no gap can take stays as ground above the hand. The play area's extra rows take their room from the bottom up: the ground, the gap above the hand, the gap above the play area, then the upper gaps, so a card exhausting into a second row moves nothing above the play area while there is ground. The card's cap is 120 pt (Medium's, times the scale). Ground still left past every cap raises the hand, up to its whole card above the bottom edge, so a tablet player reads the hand without the scrub; the hand comes back down when the play area's extra rows need that ground. Across, only when the card is at its cap, the width past a table of capped cards (7.5 cards, three gutters and five gaps) goes to the left edge 0.25 (cap 0.25 of a card's width), the gap beside the left column 0.5 (cap 0.4) and the right edge 0.25 (cap 0.25); the rest widens the rows. The left column follows the rows' y values and the left edge. A phone has no slack past the 12 pt, so its table is unchanged by this, which a test pins. The marks' room, the peeks and the 44 pt floors do not change.

The play area wraps: a row takes cards left to right a gap apart (an exhausted card by its sideways footprint) and a card that would not fit starts the next row; attachments peek under their host, a committed character stands a half peek higher, and each row is as deep as its card and its deepest attachments. When the extra rows do not fit and one row would squeeze each card under its neighbour by more than a fifth of a card (or under a 44 pt step), the play area's cards shrink, all to one size, to the largest that lets their rows fit, down to 0.7 of the table's card and never under a 44 pt target (`PLAY_FLOOR`); a slighter squeeze keeps the table's card, so the play area does not change size each time a card exhausts. Under that floor the cards overlap, keeping 44 pt targets where they can, by whichever has the larger card: one row at the floor's size while every step is still a target, or two stacked rows (`play-area.ts`, `stacked`): the front row whole and the back row showing a 44 pt band of its top (the title, where a tap lands), both of the largest card that leaves the front row's foot at most 10 pt (`HAND_TUCK`, times the scale) under the top of the hand's strip, which the hand is drawn over. A back-row card's attachments peek under the front row (the preview lists them), its tokens and effect pills are drawn inside the band that shows (`coveredFrom`, `shownBounds`), so a hero's resources are never hidden under the front row, and a committed front-row card stands its half peek over the back row's band. When neither keeps a target, the play area is one row of the table's card, squeezed. `zones.play`, the drop target for playing a card, covers every row; the deck and the discard pile stay level with the first. A card that changes rows slides (`useCardMotion`).

## Marks of the open moment

Code: `cardFrame`, `halo`, `src/play/canvas/frame.ts`; `card-marks.native.tsx`.

A card a tap does something with glows: an accent halo at 90% drawn under the card (a Gaussian blur, sigma 3 pt, so the picture covers its middle; faded to about 2% by the 6 pt gap to the next card) and a solid 2.5 pt accent edge inside it. The hand card being played rises whole. While the banner's current beat names a card, it has a 2 pt bone ring. The halo, the edges, the veil and the bone ring are drawn in the card's turned group, so they follow a fanned card's tilt and an exhausted card's quarter turn; the check ring, the pills and the other marks stay upright. On a veiled card its tokens, effect pills and marks fade to 55% with it, while the option pill stays bright. A tap on a fanned card is turned back by its tilt before it is tested, so a tilted card's corner hits that card.

## Motion

The table moves like a game, not a form, and never holds the player up: every token is at most 500 ms, apart from the title sweep, the spotlight's count, its hold and its whole run, and the splash ring's breath. A sequence of tokens runs longer (a played card 760 ms, a revealed one about 790, a destroyed one about 570, a hand of six 870), and the player never waits on it. A rule is already resolved when its motion plays. Every length, curve and spring is a token in `src/ui/motion-tokens.ts`; `motionScale(speed, reduced)` multiplies each: 1 for Normal, 0.6 for Quick, 1.5 for Slow, 0 under reduced motion, which snaps everything and plays no spotlight. **Reduce motion** (Follow system, Always; `reduceMotion`) is a setting beside the phone's: Follow system reads the phone's setting live, Always snaps whatever the phone says (`useReduceMotion`, `src/ui/motion-run.ts`). **Animation speed** (Normal, Quick, Slow; `animationSpeed`, `table-settings.ts`, stored in `user.db`). Slow also lengthens the banner's beats and hold by 1.5 (`paceScale`, `readouts/banner-time.ts`); the two-tap bar keeps its own time. Pure code works out what each card does (`motion.ts`: `flip`, `delay`, `arc`, `land`, and the cards that left) and its pose at a moment (`motion/pose.ts`, `motion/curves.ts`); the UI thread draws one clock per state (`card-motion.native.tsx`), so the canvas draws again only when what it shows changes and no timer runs while nothing moves.

- **Move.** An arrival eases out past its slot a little and settles (`cardArrive`); a card that leaves eases in (`cardDepart`). A card that was elsewhere on the table moves from there.
- **Draw.** A card leaves the deck on a shallow arc (a 24 pt rise at mid-path), showing its printed back, and turns to its face at the midpoint. Cards drawn together leave 90 ms apart.
- **Reveal.** An encounter card comes off the encounter deck showing the Eye, turns over, then pops (1.12, a 120 ms hold, a spring back to 1). A dealt shadow card does not flip: it stays face down. A flip shows only the printed back the canvas already has, never a face the player may not see.

- **The look strip.** The cards a Sailing test looks at come off the encounter deck face up, the Eye, the turn, the pop, 90 ms apart, and glide to the encounter discard pile when the test is answered (`cardDepart`, no shake).
- **The Discover look strip** (`emce.discover`; `words/scenarios/wilderland.ts`, `store/outcome.ts`). Discover X resolves in one engine step (`discover`: the cards looked at, the Loot objective attached, the Hazard revealed), so the strip belongs to the banner's beats and not to a moment the player answers. - **Play.** A card from the hand lifts to 1.12 (`lift`), glides to its slot (`glide`) and lands squashed to 0.96, springing back to 1 (`land`). The landing ticks once (`tickLand`).
- **Exhaust and ready.** The quarter turn is a spring with a light overshoot (`exhaust`).
- **Leave.** A card destroyed or discarded from play shakes (three steps of 4 pt, 50 ms each), then shrinks toward its pile (`cardLeave`) while it fades out on an ease-out curve: 570 ms in all. A count that fell to 0 has no token to pop, so only its number floats. Progress on a quest card or a location pops its strip the same way.
- **Floating number.** A number rises 20 pt from the token and fades over 700 ms: "+2" in the accent for a gain, "−1" in danger for damage or a loss, Marcellus SC 13 pt with a night outline. The numbers are the reducer's.
- **Phase title sweep** (`phase-sweep.tsx`). When the round moves into a new phase, its name in Marcellus SC at 48 pt, upper case, crosses the screen's centre left to right (fade in, slide 40 pt, fade out, 900 ms). Taps pass through it. It is skipped under Quick, under reduced motion (it plays at Slow, 1350 ms), when the phase went backward (Undo), when the new state moves cards or changes a count (what opens the phase entered does not count: a shadow card dealt as Combat opens, beside the enemies; the draw, the resources gained and the Refresh phase's readying as Resource opens), while a combat spotlight plays, and when Stops passed the whole phase (a second phase change within 150 ms takes the first one's place).
- **Seat handoff** (two-handed play). When the acting seat changes, its dial pulses once in an accent ring (1 to 1.12 and back, `seatPulse`) and the table cuts to that seat at rest, as a peek does: no card moves or fades, so the two seats' cards are never drawn over each other, and nothing is dealt.
- **Peek.** A peek and its way back have no motion: the table is redrawn from the other seat at once, every card at rest. Only the seat's own zones change (the hand, the play area, the engaged enemies with their cards, the deck and discard pile, and, once stage 2B has split the table, the staging row, the quest card and the active location); before the split staging, the quest, the locations, the encounter piles, the second slot of piles and the Heading dial stay put. The canvas making room for the pill under a split table's peek snaps too. No hand rises, no dial pulses, no token pops, no card lands and no haptic plays (`kindMoves` in `motion/kind.ts`). A peek that ends because the player confirmed something on the dock is a new state, so its own motions play, diffed from the acting seat's layout; when the engine turns to the seat the table already shows, that hand does not slide up again.
- **Hurry.** A touch on the canvas finishes the running card motions at once (the pending landing tick is dropped, so no haptic follows a snapped card), skips a combat spotlight that is playing, and does nothing else (it picks, previews and drags nothing); any other touch is handled as usual. Token pops and floating numbers keep running. It never delays or swallows a tap, a drag or a long press outside a spotlight; it is the first touch of a hold on the hand too, so a scrub that starts mid-draw finds the hand at rest; and it touches neither the tray's outside-tap fold nor the More popover's outside-tap close. Nothing animates while the carousel scrolls, the rail does not animate when the table opens, and no motion moves the dock's pinned buttons.

## Spotlight

When an attack deals or blocks damage the table plays it large, full screen: an enemy against a defender, an enemy against an undefended hero, or the players' attackers against an enemy. Archery, damage from a card effect, a canceled attack and one that placed no damage step get none (the banner covers them). An attack outside the Combat phase (an immediate attack) plays as one in it: the store opens a gate for the moment's attacks with the two states they were resolved between, and the moments the table answers after it in the same tap (the windows it passes) leave that gate be until it ends (`nextGate`, `spotlight/gate.ts`). `clashesOf(prev, next, steps)` (`clash.ts`) reads the engine's `strike`, `damage` and `destroy` steps, and every number on screen is theirs (two attacks on one target count up in turn from the state's own count). It is drawn by its own Skia canvas over the whole table, the top bar and the dock (`spotlight/layer.native.tsx`, always mounted; its canvas, which ignores touches, mounts only while a spotlight plays on the phone, so there is no idle loop. ## Gestures

Code: `src/play/canvas/hand-gestures.native.ts`; pure geometry in `hand-scrub.ts`.

A tap picks for the open moment, or, on a card the moment does not ask about, shows it large. A long press (350 ms) on a card on the table shows it large, with the tokens on it now (`card-preview-tokens`: "2 damage · 1 progress · 3 resources · Exhausted · Committed", accent `text-body`, joined by " · ", at most two lines at the phone's default text size, as many as its words take at a larger one): in the right column under the name and role on a wide screen (the column, name included, scrolls inside the card's height, on an opaque night backing so the dock never shows through its rows, and its rows start a step under the name), centred under the card upright, where the card gives up 52 pt for it, more at a larger text size (`previewStrip`); a discard pile's opens its list. A card that can be played, committed, traveled to or engaged can be dragged (a move of 6 pt starts it at once; nothing waits for a hold); the drop picks its target. While the table peeks at the other seat, a tap on any card only opens its preview, nothing is picked or sent, and nothing can be dragged; a pile still opens its list, the viewed seat's. ## Hold, slide and release

On the hand. A press on a hand card, or on the hand strip between cards, held still for 150 ms (`SCRUB_HOLD_MS`; a move of 6 pt, `SCRUB_SLOP`, before that is a drag) starts a scrub, with one selection tick (`tickArmed`). These are gesture thresholds, outside the motion scale. The scrub starts on the card pressed (between cards, the nearest), and the nearest centre takes over from the first move. The hand rises to show whole cards (`scrubRise`), at 1.5 times the table's card whatever the hand's length (smaller only on a canvas narrower than one such card), and fans out like a held hand across the canvas width (`scrubSpread`), a gap apart when the cards fit and overlapping evenly when they do not; a long hand gets a thinner slice of each card, never smaller cards (the nearest centre still picks). The cards sit on an arc about three card heights in radius, each turned 0.07 rad from its neighbour, the fan at most 0.45 rad, so the middle card stands highest and the ends turn and drop, every turned corner on the canvas. The card nearest under the finger straightens and lifts above the others (`scrubLift`), 1.55 larger, grown from its foot, its foot a quarter of its height higher, so it is drawn above the finger. Its neighbours keep the spread's size. The card the finger left settles back over `scrubLift`. Every grown card stays inside the canvas less its 8 pt edge: an end card is moved in, its turned corners counted, and the lifted card grows no taller than the room above its foot (`spread.room.lift`) and rises less where the top edge would cut it. A selection tick plays each time the card under the finger changes, including coming back onto a card after sliding off an end. The canvas dims under night at 35%, a layer of its own; the top bar and the command column stay bright. Letting go on a card opens its preview (`CardPreview`). Pushing forward without letting go, more up than sideways, by 0.3 of the 44 pt reach (`SCRUB_PUSH`, 13 pt at Medium) from wherever the finger turned forward, on any part of the card and with no pause first, picks the lifted card up (user instruction, 2026-11-01): the hand settles back and the card becomes a drag, centred under the finger and following it, that picks only when dropped on one of its targets (the play area, or an attachment's host), exactly as a drag straight from the hand; dropped anywhere else it goes back and nothing is asked. A card that cannot be played is picked up too, with no targets; let go, it plays the reject haptic (`tickRefused`) and shakes where it was dropped (four `dragRefuse` swings of 6 pt) and goes home, saying the move is not legal (under reduced motion only the haptic plays). While the push is under way the pushed card stays lifted; a move sideways or back down starts the push again from there. Letting go more than 44 pt above the lifted card's top (`spread.ceiling`), or off the ends of the hand, cancels: the hand settles back (`scrubFall`) and no preview opens. A scrub sends no intent and picks nothing; Android Back cancels an open one. Under reduced motion the hand shows risen and spread at once, the lift is instant and it settles at once. The scrub runs on the hand of the seat peeked at too, as a preview; a peek that changes the shown seat while a scrub is open cancels the scrub first. On the hand the scrub replaces the 350 ms press. With a screen reader on there is no scrub: the hand cards stay reachable through the list path, and the 350 ms press previews them. The touch that skips a spotlight never starts a scrub. The finger is read on the UI thread (`hand-scrub.native.tsx`, no idle loop); only a change of the card under it crosses to JavaScript, for the tick, the release and the pick-up.

## Pointer and keys

The web and the desktop build only: the phone has no pointer, and nothing here changes a touch. Code: `src/play/canvas/hand-gestures.canvaskit.ts` (`metro.config.js` serves it in place of the phone's file on the web, and it imports the phone's), pure geometry in `hand-hover.ts`; the keys in `table-keys.ts`.

- **Hover.** A mouse or pen resting over a hand card, or over the strip between the cards, raises and spreads the hand as the scrub does and lifts the card the risen spread has under the pointer's x (`scrubRise`, `scrubSpread`, `scrubLift`, the same pose), with no tick and no night. The hand stays risen only while the pointer is inside it as drawn: the lifted card's rectangle and the spread cards' rectangles, with the gaps between them and the strip below (`risenCardAt`); it settles over `scrubFall` as soon as the pointer is above the spread's top or past its ends, or at once under reduced motion, so it never covers the rows the player reaches next (a payment target, an engaged enemy). The lifted card keeps the lift while the pointer is on its rectangle, so a few pixels do not pass it to a neighbour; the card under the pointer is lifted once the pointer leaves it. After a left press the hand does not rise again until the pointer has left the hand's strip once. A touch never opens it, and neither does a playing spotlight. It follows the pointer only while the table's canvas is what the pointer is over, so the command column, a sheet or the preview over it end it. Hovering sends no intent and picks nothing.
- **Click and drag on the risen hand.** A press on the risen hand is a press on the card drawn under the pointer, not on the resting hand beneath it: the phone's gesture never sees it. A click taps that card (what a tap on it sends, here or in a pick moment), and the hand settles. A move past the slop (`SCRUB_SLOP`) hands the press to the phone's drag from that card's resting place, so it is picked up under the pointer and plays only when dropped on a target, as a drag from the hand. A press outside the risen hand closes it and goes to the phone's gesture as it is.
- **Wheel.** Over the open hand the wheel steps the lifted card, never past either end. A mouse notch (a delta of 60 px or more, or a line or page delta) is exactly one card and clears what was left; only a trackpad's smaller deltas add up to a step (`wheelAdvance`). A wheel with Ctrl (a zoom) is left alone.
- **Keyword chips.** A chip in the preview opens its bubble on a click, or on Enter or Space once Tab has focused it; there is no hover. Escape closes the bubble before the preview (the same path as Back).
- **Right click.** Opens the preview the 350 ms press opens (a face-up, held or dead card on the table; a pile opens its list), or, with the pointer on the risen hand, the card drawn under it; outside the risen hand it goes to what is under the pointer on the table. The browser's own menu does not open. Not while a spotlight plays.
- **Keys.** Escape and Z, Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z work wherever the focus is, as under Screen reader, Keyboard: `table-keys.ts` sends a key the table maps, pressed outside the table's root, to the root as the same key, so one handler runs it. A key typed in a text field is left alone. Enter and Space press the focused control as the browser does. The hand has no arrow keys: the drawn canvas takes no keyboard focus, and a keyboard player plays through Table as a list.
- **Volume rows.** The track of Effects volume and Music volume is a tab stop with the focus ring and the slider's value for a screen reader ("80%"); the arrows move it 5%, Page Up and Page Down 10%, Home and End to 0% and 100%, each written at once, so Effects volume plays its confirm sound at each step (`volume-slider.canvaskit.tsx`, `src/ui/web/volume-keys.ts`). A click on the track sets the volume there and a drag moves it; the wheel scrolls the page.
- **Chrome.** A control is a `Pressable`, which react-native-web gives `cursor: pointer`. The dock's "i" (`dock-more`) keeps the phone's slop there, where `hitSlop` is ignored, by a pseudo-element: 44 pt tall and 6 pt of slop to its left (its right slop is cut by the readout's clip, as on Android) (`src/ui/web/page-styles.ts`). Instructions say Click for the phone's Tap (Standards 9 in `docs/design-system.md`). The focus ring is the phone's (Screen reader, Focus ring), drawn for the keyboard's focus and not a click's (`src/ui/web/focus-ring.canvaskit.ts`).

## Why a seat is out

The engine's reason for the loss of a game whose last seat went out is "No seats remain" (the engine's own text, kept in saves): it names neither the seat nor the cause, and the Defeat note, the outcome screen and the resolution screen say it as "Every player is eliminated". A loss with any other reason (a Mûmak left play, a card that says the players lose) shows that reason as it is. The table tells the cause of each elimination in one sentence per seat: "Player 1's threat reached 50." when the seat's threat is at its elimination level, and "Player 1's last hero was destroyed." when destroy steps took every hero it had in play. An elimination with neither cause (the deck ran out, a Mûmak left play) gets no cause line. The sentence is under the "Player 1 is eliminated" beat, which reads the steps of its own intent. The Defeat note and the outcome screen add the threat cause to "Every player is eliminated"; the outcome screen adds the heroes cause too, from the heroes a destroy step named over the whole game. The outcome screen tells a hero of an eliminated player that no destroy step named "Defeated with the player"; a hero a destroy step named is "Destroyed". The threat dial of an eliminated seat shows "Out" and no threat change (`src/play/chrome/table-chrome.tsx`).

## Two taps

A button that sends an intent with nothing picked first (Pass, End planning, Keep, Mulligan, Leave undefended, Do not travel, Do not engage, No attack, Continue, Done, Commit no one) arms on the first tap: the armed line says what the second tap does ("Tap again: no one quests", "Tap again: planning ends") in the accent, with a 2 pt accent bar running down under it for the Confirm time setting: Standard is three seconds (ten while a screen reader is on), Long is ten, and No limit never runs out, the bar drawn full and still until the second tap, another button, a new pick or a new moment (`armMs`, `choice/dock/armed-room.ts`; the bar shows the timer on a linear curve, so Quick leaves it as long, and reduced motion leaves it still); the second tap sends it; the bar running out, another button, a new pick or a new moment puts it back. The wait under Standard follows the real screen reader, not Table as a list. Arming changes no button's size or place, so the second tap lands where the first did. The same line shows what a disabled primary waits for. A primary that confirms a pick sends on one tap, because the pick was the first. Each armed button carries the hint "Tap twice to confirm", and a screen reader hears the armed line when it arms. A confirm that costs more than it shows arms the same way, though something is picked: a defender or a hero hit whose fall loses the game ("Tap again: Frodo Baggins falls and the game is lost"; the readout says why), an attachment whose text the app does not apply ("Tap again: pay 2 for no effect"), and a travel, or Do not travel, that a rule charges for ("Tap again: 2 resources go on a Raider enemy (Streets of Umbar)").

An answer that reaches past one window is gold, so the silver Pass a player taps window after window never looks like it (user request, 2026-11-17): in planning the window's pass is End planning, a gold filled primary (`phaseSurface`, its word in `onPhase`), and Pass to stop is a secondary with its word and a dashed 1.5 pt edge in `phase` (the edge comes out of the side padding, so the capsule keeps its size). Armed, either one's line and bar are gold too. The button carries its reach (`DockReach`: "phase" or "run", `table-choice.ts`). End planning also ignores every tap, arming included, for 500 ms after its moment appears (`PHASE_PAUSE_MS`, `choice/dock/phase-pause.ts`), so the tail of a quick "Pass, Pass" from the window before cannot end planning.

## One answer, one tap

When the engine allows one intent and the sheet shows one answer for it (no character can defend, so Leave undefended is the answer, and with one hero it hits, the dock names that hero, says whether the hero is destroyed, and draws the hero beside the enemy; with several heroes Leave undefended names none, and once the shadow effect has resolved the hero pick is the whole sheet, each hero with the damage it would take, its primary "Take the damage" and no alternative (`rr.step-6-4-3`, `choice/combat-sheets.ts`); nothing to travel to; a window with only Pass; a Look's Done; a lone option of a choice; an archery total one character must take), the answer is picked already: its card shows the picked mark, the primary is ready and not armed, the line above it says "The only choice now: one tap sends it" in muted (a screen reader hears "Sends at once"), and one tap on the primary or on the card sends it (`onlyAnswer`, `src/play/choice/table-choice.ts`). A sheet opened from the table never counts: the window behind it has other answers. For 350 ms after a new moment appears no tap sends anything, so the second tap of a double tap meant for the moment before does not land on this one.

## Stops and Always use

With Stops on (the carousel's switch), the dock's spoken step starts with "Stops on ·", and an action window that is not one of the player's stops passes by itself unless a card usable there is new since the player was last asked. While stops are on, an action window offers the answer Pass to stop, in gold (Two taps) and armed like Pass, its line naming where the run ends at the latest ("Tap again: pass until before the quest resolves", or "until planning", which always asks); a choice on the way ends it sooner. A Response with no cost and nothing to choose offers Always use, armed ("Tap again: use it now and every time"); the table settings list those Responses, and a tap there asks again. The answers that follow a send go in the same tap, so a window the table passes is never drawn: in two-handed play the table does not flash to the other seat and back (`followAnswers`, `stops.ts`). Undo takes back an answer the table gave, and the table then asks at that window.

## Guide

While the first-game guide is on (the default), the dock's folded line is "Guide: …" in muted, the first two times each step's own decision comes up (the step's decision from the turn reference, or what an action window is for). It is not said in a Response or a pick inside a step, nor when the note already says it, and it is the one line the dock shows (the note waits behind the "i" pill), so on a short phone it never pushes the buttons away. It is turned off in the settings.

## Choice tray

Code: `src/play/choice/tray.tsx`; its tiles and the reading panels' frame in `tray-logic.ts`.

## Haptics

A tick when a button arms, a confirm when an intent is sent or taken back, a reject when the reducer refuses one, a play the heroes cannot pay for is tried, or a card that cannot be played is let go out of the hand scrub, a light tick when a card played from the hand lands (`tickLand`, at most one per new state), a medium impact at a spotlight's hit stop (`tickImpact`, at most one per strike), the lightest tick as each of its damage tokens lands in the count (`tickToken`, at most 9 an attack, played on the spotlight's clock in `spotlight.native.tsx`, so a skip plays none; user decision, 2026-11-19), and the scrub's selection tick (`tickArmed`; no scrub runs during a spotlight). The confirm plays the moment the reducer accepts the intent, before the table redraws and the save is written (a refused intent gets only the reject); the landing tick plays as the card's motion clock passes the landing, so a touch that snaps the motion plays none. One of each in a moment. A Nazgûl's roar, a treachery's sting and the hand's deal sound (`combat.roar`, `dread.treachery`, `card.deal`) are not played: they would have to ride the reveal and draw cues of the motion clock, which know no printed card or phase.
- **Dials and rail:** a seat's threat dial rising or falling (`dread.threat-up`, `dread.threat-down`; `threatSound`), or crossing into critical (`dread.danger`); a new round, as the rail turns to the Resource phase (`dread.phase`; the other phases are quiet); the quest advance screen (`dread.stage`); the outcome title, Victory or Defeat (`dread.win`, `dread.loss`), once as it first shows (an end plays none).
- **Screens:** every button tap plays a click (`ui.confirm`; user decision, 2026-11-21): the shared buttons play it themselves (`PrimaryButton`, `SecondaryButton`, `TextButton`, `GlassIconButton`, `IconButton`, `PillButton`, `ListRow`, `PopoverRow`; `withSound`), so a new screen gets it too; so do the tiles that are buttons of their own: a campaign or a scenario in its tab (`CatalogTile`, `RunRow`), a stop on the campaign's path, a deck in the Decks list, and the card pool's sphere buttons and filter rows (user request, 2026-11-21). With a screen reader on, the interface sounds (`ui.*`) do not play: the reader sounds each control itself. The table's sounds do.

A table sound asked for again within 60 ms plays once; an interface sound plays for every tap (a second call within 25 ms is the same tap). A slot keeps up to four players, so quick taps each get one that is not still sounding. All the slots together keep at most `PLAYERS_MAX` players loaded (`src/ui/sound/sound.ts`), and a new one takes the place of the player used longest ago. Each loaded player holds one of the phone's audio decoders, and Android gives an app only a few dozen, so the music always finds one for its loops. A sound starts with no wait when its player is loaded and stands at its start, so both are done before the moment (`primeSounds`): the screens' sounds are loaded at launch and when the table closes, the table's most played while it shows (`SCREEN_SOUNDS`, `TABLE_SOUNDS`, `src/ui/sound/slots.ts`), a state's motion sounds as the state is drawn and a spotlight's strikes as it starts; a player that has sounded is put back to its start once the player itself says the sound has ended (never by the clock alone: on a busy phone a sound starts late). On Android the sounds use none of these players: each is a sample of the phone's `SoundPool` (`modules/sound-pool`, `src/ui/sound/pool-samples.ts`; user decision, 2026-11-25), decoded once into memory, loaded at the same moments and kept, and started with no seek. A paused player with a short file goes dead there, its next play silent while the player says it plays, and a seek before each play is heard as a late sound. A sample follows the media volume, takes no audio focus of its own and pauses while the app is not in front. An Android build without the module (a development client built before it) plays through the players, every play a seek to the start with the play sent right behind it (`STARTS_BY_SEEK`, `src/ui/sound/starts-by-seek.native.ts`). Each file starts at its sound: the silence its source holds before it is cut off (`soundStart`, `scripts/build/pack-sounds.ts`), with the quiet lead-in of a pick that names an `onset` (`scripts/build/sound-picks.ts`), and `pnpm assets sounds --check` fails on a packed file that starts with silence. On iOS the silent switch turns them off; on Android the media volume sets them, and a ringer on silent or vibrate does not (`src/ui/sound/session.ts`); the web and the desktop play them through the computer's own output. The other app decides whether it obeys, and it does not start again when this app closes. With the setting off, the sounds mix with the player's own music. The web and the desktop cannot stop another program's audio and do not show the setting. Under reduced motion the card and token sounds, which ride the motion clocks, do not play, and with the Combat spotlight off (or under reduced motion) the strike sounds do not either. The threat dial and the rail do not wait for a spotlight: their sounds play as the numbers change, while it is still playing. They play at the **Effects volume** (`soundVolume`, 80% by default), and the **Sound effects** setting (`sounds`, on by default) turns all of them off; both are in the Settings category Sound and the table's settings panel, which shows only in a bundle that carries the files (`docs/design-system.md`, Settings). The files are cut from the player's own Darkest Dungeon II files, and the card landing from the player's own Hearthstone sounds (`scripts/build/sound-picks.ts`; user decisions, 2026-11-21), pinned in `data/sources.json` and kept in the asset mirror, never in git; every release's phone builds and desktop installers carry them (user decision, 2026-11-21), and a build packed with no mirror and no cache plays none. The web and the desktop read the same files (`src/ui/sound/bundled.canvaskit.ts`) through expo-audio's web players (`audio-player.canvaskit.ts`), and their own files keep the phone's sounds: the spotlight's copy and the volume row's copy are held to the phone's files (`scripts/checks/web-copies.test.ts`), the compact rail presses its tabs and Settings through the phone's `tabItem` and `pressSettings` (`src/screens/nav/tab-bar.tsx`), and the Sound category shows when the export carries the files (`settings-platform.canvaskit.ts`). The two open owner decisions about sound (visual effects, the Hobbit score) are in `docs/plans/sound-and-music.md`.

**Music.** One loop plays at a time (`src/ui/sound/music.ts`): the menu's (`menu`, from The Prophecy) on every screen but the table and the screens read from it, and on the table its scenario's calm loop, or its combat loop while a fight is on (`tableMusic`, `src/ui/sound/music-map.ts`: the film's own scene for the saga scenarios, a mood's pair for the boxes with none). A new loop crossfades in over `MUSIC_LEAD_SECONDS`, equal-power; a loop left mid-piece is paused where it was and goes on from there when it comes back (the calm loop across a fight). A loop repeats with no seam: its file holds `MUSIC_LEAD_SECONDS` of the music before the loop's start, the next copy is loaded a few seconds early, and as one copy reaches its last `MUSIC_LEAD_SECONDS` the next starts at the matching point and the two crossfade, landing on the loop's start. ## Screen reader

It reads the zone and pile selectors the canvas reads and draws with the app's tokens and `Text`: 44 pt rows, a glowing card outlined and a picked one filled. A heading (`accessibilityRole="header"`, `zoneSpeech`) opens each zone, in this order: the quest stage, the active location (and the second one), staging, engaged, the seat's heroes and play area, the hand, then the piles (deck, discard, the second slot's piles, the victory display once it holds a card). Staging, engaged, the active location, play and the hand keep their heading when empty ("Your hand, no cards"). A card is followed by what is attached to it and by the characters paired with it in a fight. The sea lines (the Heading, the ships, the Sailing test's look) and the Discover look stand as text above the zones. Each card is a button with its `cardSpeech` label and hint, selected while picked; while a pick is open, a card it does not ask about is drawn faint and its hint says "Not part of this choice. Double tap to show the card." (it is not disabled: TalkBack may then block its click and its actions), and a double tap on it opens the card large, as a tap on the canvas does. A **double tap** is the canvas tap (`onTap(cardId)`, `onTap("pile:<id>")` for a pile): it plays, picks or opens the preview through `tapAction`, and nothing else. The custom action **Show card** is the long press (`onPreview`); a facedown card has none. A pile whose tap opens a list has the action **Open the list**.
- **The reader note.** A sheet may carry a `readerNote` beside its `note`; the dock shows and speaks it in place of the note while a reader is on. The window's says what the buttons say instead of naming a glow or a drag: "Cards that say 'can be played' can be played now." and "Cards that say 'can use' can be used now.", with the discard pile sentence kept. The visible `note` is unchanged for everyone else.
- **A new moment.** When the open sheet's key changes, focus moves to the dock's title after the layout settles (`src/ui/use-reader-focus.ts`, about 350 ms) and once the announcer is quiet, so the banner's title and lines are heard first, then the new title by focus, then its note, which is the title's hint. The table's first moment takes focus once when the table opens. The won and lost sheets are told the same way. Focus does not move while the reader is on a card the new moment still asks about: the last card or row it activated is kept through an accepted send (an Undo, a peek or leaving the table forget it), and if the new sheet asks about it the title and note are announced and focus stays (`choice/use-reader-choice.ts`, `choice/reader-moment.ts`, `holdsFocus`, `momentSpeech`, `touchedAfter`; `ui/reader-focus.ts`, `focusPlan`).
- **Refusals.** A refusal is announced as it is made, with its reason: the reducer's own ("That card is not in hand"), and the table's, a cost the pools cannot pay ("It costs 3. Your Spirit heroes have 1 resource."). The dock's waiting sentence names the list, not a glow ("Choose a card that can be picked.", `readerDock`).
- **The tray's rows.** A pick row is a radio, a multi row a checkbox, a trigger row a button; a share row is adjustable. See Choice tray.
- **Combat.** The banner is announced as its title and then each line (Banner), so an attack is told in order though no spotlight plays.
- **Panels.** The reading panels, the card preview, the phase list and the quest advance screen are modal too; escape closes each reading panel, the preview and the phase list, and moves the quest advance screen on when it asks nothing. A reading panel takes focus at its header when it opens (`chrome/use-panel-focus.ts`); the Log keeps its order, oldest first, and its focus lands on the newest line. On Android the reader table is hidden from TalkBack while a panel is open (`importantForAccessibility`, `choice/reader-panel.ts`), since only iOS has a modal view. The preview's scrim is a button.
- **Keyboard** (user instruction, 2026-11-07). Play with a hardware keyboard goes through the list table (Table as a list on Always, or a reader on): its card and pile rows, the tray's rows and the dock's buttons are focusable (iPad Full Keyboard Access and Android keyboard navigation move focus; Enter or Space presses the focused control as the platform does, so two taps stay two presses), and the one focused wears the focus ring. Escape closes the banner, else the topmost layer exactly as Android Back does (Android Back), and never leaves the table; Z, Ctrl+Z or Cmd+Z runs the dock's Undo button, only while that button is on the dock and nothing is open over the table: no spotlight playing, no hand scrub, no panel, preview, menu or popover (`src/ui/key-shortcuts.ts`, `screens/table/use-table-back.ts`). React Native 0.88 gives a View key events on Android only, so the shortcuts work there and not on iOS, where Tab and the platform's own keys remain; the web and the desktop bind a window listener (Pointer and keys). The drawn canvas takes no keyboard focus: a keyboard player plays through Table as a list.
- **Focus ring.** An outline 2 pt wide and 2 pt off the control, scaled with the UI scale and following the control's corner radius: accentHigh, or ink on paper and parchment (the sheet's rows and buttons), where accentHigh would be about 1:1 (a test holds 3:1 on the night, raised, dock and paper grounds in both themes). It is on every focusable control of the table: the reader rows, the tray's rows, the dock's buttons and its "i", the More popover's and the table menu's rows, the threat and Heading dials, the reading panels' card rows, the card preview's related rows and chips, and the Segmented options of the settings (`src/ui/focus-ring.ts`, drawn by `PrimaryButton`, `SecondaryButton`, `TextButton`, `GlassIconButton`, `Tile`, `PopoverRow`, `Segmented`, `RelatedRow`, `RelatedChip` and the reader table's rows). It draws only while the control holds focus, which a touch never gives these Pressables, so a touch player sees no change; the web and the desktop draw the same ring for a keyboard's focus and none for a click (`src/ui/web/focus-ring.canvaskit.ts`). A control that does not draw it itself (the rail's items, History, the phase rail, the booklet's page region) gets the same ring from one stylesheet for every `button`, `link`, `tab` and tab stop (`src/ui/web/page-styles.ts`, `:focus-visible`), which a control wearing its own ring puts out inline, so no control wears two. A disabled or dimmed control fades its fill and word, never its ring, which is on a view of its own. The ring is never covered or clipped: the tray's and the quest advance screen's scroll views pad their content by the ring's room on the sides and top and take it back in their margins (`ringScroll`), the list table's padding leaves it room and, while the banner is up, its content starts under the banner's height (`src/play/readouts/banner-height.ts`), and on a dense dock a focused primary moves the armed bar up by the ring's room. A row's ring at the very foot of a scroll view can lose its outer pixels.

## Android Back

First skips a playing spotlight, then cancels an open scrub, then ends a peek, then closes the table menu or the dock's More or "i" popover, then the preview, then the reading layer (the carousel, a panel, the log, a pile's list), then puts back a sheet opened from the table, then folds the choice tray, and only then leaves the table.
