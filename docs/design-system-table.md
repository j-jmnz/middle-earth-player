# Design system — Table (landscape)

The table screen's design. The rest of the design system (`docs/design-system.md`: tokens, Standards, Performance, Components, States) applies here too; this file only adds what is the table's own.

## Overview

The table is played in landscape only (`orientation` on its stack screen), with the status bar hidden and the stack's edge swipe off (a drag from the hand's edge must not leave the table; Leave the table in the menu does). Scene `table`: the canvas is transparent over its fog, with no felt and no zone boxes. Under the top bar, the canvas at the left and the command column at the right (30% of the width, 210 to 280 pt), both inside the side safe-area insets. Phone checks of the canvas are open (`docs/phases/STATUS.md`): the web build lists the zones instead of drawing them.

## Top bar

Code: `src/screens/table-top-bar.tsx`, `src/play/top-bar-layout.ts`.

52 pt (`TOP_BAR`: the 46 pt dial with 3 pt above and below), under the top safe-area inset and inside both side ones, starting at least 8 pt under the screen's top edge (`BAR_TOP`) so a dial's pop stays on the screen. Where a status bar stays (an iPad), the bar starts right under its inset, counted once: the table's `Screen` bleeds and the table insets itself. Three zones and nothing else: the threat dials at the left, the phase rail centred on the screen, one menu button at the right.

- **The threat dial** (`ThreatRing` compact, `table-chrome.tsx`): 46 pt round, a ring of threat red (`ArcRing`) filling toward 50, the elimination level (`rr.threat-elimination-level`), on a faint red track, around a dark disc holding the number alone in Marcellus SC 20, with a red glow; its label still says "threat". A tick on the ring at each staging enemy's engagement cost (not one that cannot be engaged), bone where the threat has reached it and faint where not. It warms near elimination (`src/play/threat-level.ts`): from 40 the ring turns danger; from 45 the disc fills red and a screen reader hears "near elimination". The dial is one control whose name says how many engage at this threat and, while it shows, the change. When the number changes the dial pops (`dialPopRise` to 1.22, then `dialPopSettle`) and a badge under it says the change for three seconds, "−6" on an accent wash when it fell, "+2" on a threat-red wash when it rose, with no edge, read as "Threat lowered by 6, now 24"; reduced motion keeps the dial still and shows the badge. Dials stand 10 pt apart, each with its seat's label (P1) beside it. Life, then the Burden deck (labelled "Burden", read as "Burden deck"), are the same dial with no meter and no levels, when declared. On Nightmare the Eye of Sauron follows them, in threat red with the word Nightmare beside it (always: the peeked seat's eye mark is another eye), with the dials' pressed fill; a tap opens the setup card's preview (`nightmare-card`).
- **Two seats**: the labels are P1 and P2, read as "Player 1 threat, acting, 28" and "Player 2 threat, viewing, 33". The acting seat's label is in the accent. A tap on the dial of a seat that is not shown peeks at it (hint "Shows Player 2's cards"; Peeking); a tap on the shown seat's dial opens its threat panel (Reading panels). A seat peeked at that is not acting wears a bone ring just outside its dial, a small eye mark at its top-right (`threat-<seat>-viewing`, `threat-<seat>-eye`, drawn as views) and a bone label; neither touches the Ring mark in the label row, which keeps its place at 667 × 375 with two seats. Only the acting seat's dial pulses (Seat handoff); a peek never does.
- **Held marks**: while the Ring-bearer is facedown under a card, the seat that gets it back (always the first player) wears the printed One Ring, 12 pt, in the accent beside its label, and the dial's name says "The Ring-bearer is facedown under Watcher in the Water" (`threat-<seat>-ring`). The seat whose hero is the prisoner of Escape from Dol Guldur wears the printed hero icon in bone in the same slot, and its dial says "Aragorn is the prisoner, face down" (`heldDial`, `src/play/widgets.ts`).
- **The phase rail** (`PhaseRail`, `src/play/phase-rail.tsx`): no pill and no edge. The phase in Marcellus SC 17 tracked 4 with a silver glow, and the round's seven phases as ticks under it (22 × 3, 5 apart: past accentLine, current accentHigh and glowing, to come dark). Outside the seven the phase reads Beginning or End; Setup and the mulligan have no phase, so their step stands in its place. The round and the step are not drawn (the carousel names the round): `rail-round` and `rail-current` stay in the rail as clear text a flow reads, and the rail's label says them. When the rail is wider than `RAIL_WIDE`, the previous and next phase names stand inline at the phase's sides in muted label, in equal slots so the phase stays on the rail's middle; they change in place. An accent S follows the phase while Stops are on and one of the player's stops lies ahead in this phase (`rail-stop-ahead`); its room is kept on both sides. A phase change brightens the glow (`railFlash`, 300 ms), which snaps under reduced motion, and what is on the table when it opens appears without fading.
- **Rail layout**: the rail is centred on the screen, as wide as it can be without reaching the last dial or the menu button, and never under `RAIL_FLOOR`, computed from its own words (the widest phase, and Setup and Mulligan, whose step stands in the phase's place) and ticks; a test checks every one against it. When the dials need more room than that leaves them (two seats with Life and the Burden deck on a phone), the dials scroll sideways behind a seven-band fade at the left zone's right edge, none cut, rather than the rail leaving the middle; only when the menu side cannot hold half the floor does the rail move. Until the bar and the dials are measured the rail is held invisible, so it does not jump when the table opens.
- A tap on the rail opens the phase carousel and a second tap closes it; the rail is heard as a button that expands. The rail and a dial show a faint bone fill while pressed or open.
- **The menu button** is the round glass button (`GlassIconButton`, `glassSurface`), 44 pt, with three bone dots drawn as views (accent while the menu is open), label "Menu" (`table-menu`).

## Table menu

A popover under the menu button, 240 pt wide, over the table and any open panel and never on the canvas. Its frame is shared with the dock's More and "i" popovers (`popover-frame.tsx`): an opaque lit night card (`floatingSurface`, no drawn edge, so the dock's words never read through it) with 20 pt corners and a deep shadow, its rows 48 pt with 12 pt corners, 6 pt inside its edge, that fill when pressed. Four rows of bone text that open something over the table, with no chevron: Log (`log-open`), Rules (`rules-open`), Booklet (`booklet-open`) and Table settings (`settings-open`); then, apart at the foot and set off by 8 pt of space with no rule, Leave the table (`table-back`) in danger, after a door mark drawn as views. A screen reader hears where it goes ("Back to the path") and the hint "The game is saved". Rules opens the glossary (`glossary.tsx`, the Rules tab's list without the books, `NavBar` Back to the table; it and a rule turn with the phone). A row closes the menu and then acts; a tap anywhere outside closes it and sends nothing (a full-screen Pressable under the popover, not a Modal, which would take over the orientation lock and the status bar). It enters in `menuEnter` (scale 0.96 to 1 from its corner with a fade), leaves in `menuLeave` from where it stood, and snaps under reduced motion.

## Quest scale

Code: `quest-scale.ts`, `quest-scale-view.tsx`.

Shown only in the Quest phase (and while the dock's quest readout is up); outside it the column is not drawn and the canvas takes its room, so the table lays out again (its cards slide) as the Quest phase starts and ends. It stands at the table's left edge, 86 pt wide (110 on a tablet, with 70 pt dials), on a soft dark radial with no panel: the staging area's threat on a red dial with "staging" under it (one small word per dial; the player's own threat dial in the top bar has none), the difference between the dials in the middle ("+N" in green, "−N" in red, or Even), and the willpower committed on a silver dial with "willpower" under it. The dials keep their rings. While the players commit it reads the dock's quest readout, which adds each character picked; otherwise the engine's `stagingThreat` and `committedWillpower`. A screen reader hears the threat in staging, the willpower committed and how far ahead or short it is.

## Canvas

The left column is one and a half cards wide: the quest card, sideways, level with staging, with a strip at its foot showing progress / quest points; the active location level with the engagement area (two side by side, smaller, when the scenario opens a second slot); the player's deck and discard pile level with the play area. To its right, top to bottom: staging, with the encounter deck and its discard pile at its right end; the engagement area, told apart by a red haze toward its left, where the enemies stand; the play area; the hand, peeking up from the bottom edge above the bottom safe-area inset: 56% of a card and never less than 52 pt, so each hand card keeps a 44 pt target past the fan's drop. The card size fits three rows, the hand's strip and seven and a half cards across: about 51 × 71 pt on an 844 × 390 phone. An empty zone shows one faint word (Staging, No enemy engaged, No location). In the Quest phase the canvas lays out in the room right of the quest scale.

## Spacing and the play area's rows

Code: `layout.ts`; shares and caps in `table-spacing.ts`, all pure and tested.

The rows are laid out at their minimum gaps first (the 8 pt gutter, 6 pt gaps, 10 pt peeks, Medium's sizes times the UI scale). The room left between the play area's one row and a gap above the hand, less a 12 pt slack (times the scale) that is never shared, goes to four gaps by shares, each capped at a share of the card's height: above staging 0.1 (cap 0.15), staging to the engagement area 0.2 (cap 0.3), the engagement area to the play area 0.35 (cap 0.4), the play area to the hand 0.35 (cap 0.4). A capped gap's overflow goes to the others; what no gap can take stays as ground above the hand. The play area's extra rows take their room from the bottom up: the ground, the gap above the hand, the gap above the play area, then the upper gaps, so a card exhausting into a second row moves nothing above the play area while there is ground. The card's cap is 120 pt (Medium's, times the scale). Ground still left past every cap raises the hand, up to its whole card above the bottom edge, so a tablet player reads the hand without the scrub; the hand comes back down when the play area's extra rows need that ground. Across, only when the card is at its cap, the width past a table of capped cards (7.5 cards, three gutters and five gaps) goes to the left edge 0.25 (cap 0.25 of a card's width), the gap beside the left column 0.5 (cap 0.4) and the right edge 0.25 (cap 0.25); the rest widens the rows. The left column follows the rows' y values and the left edge. A phone has no slack past the 12 pt, so its table is unchanged by this, which a test pins. The marks' room, the peeks and the 44 pt floors do not change.

The play area wraps: a row takes cards left to right a gap apart (an exhausted card by its sideways footprint) and a card that would not fit starts the next row; attachments peek under their host, a committed character stands a half peek higher, and each row is as deep as its card and its deepest attachments. When the extra rows do not fit and one row would squeeze each card under its neighbour by more than a fifth of a card (or under a 44 pt step), the play area's cards shrink, all to one size, to the largest that lets their rows fit, down to 0.7 of the table's card and never under a 44 pt target (`PLAY_FLOOR`); a slighter squeeze keeps the table's card, so the play area does not change size each time a card exhausts. Under that floor the cards overlap, keeping 44 pt targets where they can, by whichever has the larger card: one row at the floor's size while every step is still a target, or two stacked rows (`play-area.ts`, `stacked`): the front row whole and the back row showing a 44 pt band of its top (the title, where a tap lands), both of the largest card that leaves the front row's foot at most 10 pt (`HAND_TUCK`, times the scale) under the top of the hand's strip, which the hand is drawn over. A back-row card's attachments peek under the front row (the preview lists them), and a committed front-row card stands its half peek over the back row's band. When neither keeps a target, the play area is one row of the table's card, squeezed. `zones.play`, the drop target for playing a card, covers every row; the deck and the discard pile stay level with the first. A card that changes rows slides (`useCardMotion`).

## Pictures on the table

Code: `table-pictures.ts`, `table-images.native.ts`.

Each code is decoded once at the largest box the canvas draws it in: a hand card at the scrub's lifted size (its hand size times `scrubScale` times `scrubLiftScale`, about 2.3 times), any other card at its slot's (a sideways quest card at its sideways box), at the screen's pixel ratio. The file is brought down in halving steps with linear filtering, then drawn to the size, so a 1200 px scan does not alias, and the full decode is released. A copy is decoded again only when it is too small for where its card now is or more than twice the pixels it needs, and the old copy is drawn until the new one is ready; a code that leaves the table is released. The canvas draws every picture with linear mipmap sampling. A test keeps a crowded table (8 cards in hand and 32 other codes) under 16 MB of copies on a 3× phone and 36 MB on a 2× 12.9" tablet at Large. Judge sharpness on a release build: a dev build without bundled pictures draws the smaller RingsDB files.

## Cards

Pictures with a drop shadow; a card whose file is missing shows its name wrapped in Barlow, its sphere icon in the corner. A face-down card shows its printed back: the Eye for a shadow card or an encounter card, the One Ring for a player card. Exhausted cards lie sideways and take a sideways footprint in their row; their tokens and effects stay upright. A committed character stands half a step higher with an accent willpower pill at its foot. Attachments and face-down shadow cards peek out under their host, the most recent lowest; an attachment is drawn under its host only. A face-up shadow card stands beside its enemy at 80%. Cards facedown under a location (Weather Hills) are out of play and not drawn.

- **Not applied**: a card with a printed line the app does not apply carries a small mark at its right edge, just under the damage token's corner, upright: a faint ring struck through on a night disc, 13 pt. Never a band over the picture, never a word on the canvas, not accent and not danger. The preview lists the lines under Not applied (`docs/plans/done/inactive-text.md`).
- **Combat**: the enemy whose attack is in progress has a 3 pt threat-red edge and its attack icon at its side from the moment it attacks. A defender, the hero an undefended attack hits, or the attackers stand beside the enemy, out of the play area, with a mark between them (an accent defense icon, a red attack icon when undefended, an accent attack icon for the players' attack): from the moment the player picks them, fainter until confirmed, and until the attack ends.
- **Escape from Dol Guldur.** An encounter card that guards an objective lies over it, shifted right by 72% of its width, so the objective's name and left side stay readable, and the pair keeps a wider gap from the next card in staging; its preview says "Guarded by Hill Troll", or "Free of encounters: you can claim it". The prisoner is its player's hero, facedown in that seat's play area with the card held under it (Mendor) peeking out below; its back wears a small bone hero mark on a night disc; its preview says "Aragorn is the prisoner, face down" (the cards held with it stay hidden), and the web list reads "Prisoner: Aragorn, face down". An encounter card attached to a deck (Iron Shackles) peeks out above the deck by a step, and the deck wears a small bone chain on its count strip; a tap on the deck opens the card, "Iron Shackles: your next draw is discarded" (the web list says "· Iron Shackles attached").
- **Quest card**: its preview is titled by its stage and progress ("Flight to the Ford · 2 / 8"). A stage that prints 0 quest points and is won another way ("Don't Leave the Path!", 01121) shows no "25 / 0" strip, and its preview says "To win: …". The preview names what holds a full stage back in the rule's own sentence ("Players cannot defeat this stage while any Hill Troll cards are in play."), so a strip past its quest points (13 / 8) reads as the rule. When a branch chose the stage (01120:B:1), the banner says "The path was chosen at random" and the stage's printed lines but its When Revealed.
- **Player side quest**: it stands first in its player's play-area row with its progress / quest points strip; in the commit window, once characters are picked, a plate "To <side quest>" beside Commit sends them to it instead of the current quest, and the window's note says so.

## Tokens and effects

The token art at 18 pt with its count in Marcellus SC, bone with a night outline: resources bottom left, damage top right, progress bottom right. Down a card's left edge, each number an effect changed is a 15 pt pill with its icon and the change in 11 pt bold: accent with onAccent "+1" when raised, night with danger "-1" when lowered; each "cannot" is its icon in danger, struck through. A card shows at most two of these pills, then one night pill "+n" for the rest (`badgeSplit` in `card-effects.ts`; fewer when the card is too short, never past the resource token); the preview lists every effect. A change that lasts only until a timing point has a dashed edge, and the preview says until when. The numbers are the engine's (`card-effects.ts`). A card a delayed effect will act on (Sneak Attack's return) has a small accent clock at its right edge, under the not-applied mark; the preview says what will happen and when. An enemy in staging shows its engagement cost at its foot: the engagement token art and the number on a pill, threat red when the shown seat's threat engages it at the checks, night when it stays. Strong outlines (a setting) draws the pills and the token counts with stronger outlines, and a resource token carries a ring in its sphere's colour.

## Piles

A stack of the printed backs (the player deck's One Ring, the encounter deck's Eye; while the file decodes, a raised fill with an accent ring for the player's, a threat ring for the encounter deck's), or the top card of a discard pile (its name on raised night while its file is missing, so a discard never looks like a deck), with the count in Marcellus SC on a night strip and the word under it (Deck, Discard, Encounter). An empty pile is a dashed outline. Only a discard pile does something on a tap or a long press: its list opens; its touch area is at least 44 pt. The top of the deck shows face up when the rules let the player play it (Gandalf).

## Marks of the open moment

Code: `cardFrame`, `halo`, `src/play/frame.ts`; `card-marks.native.tsx`.

A card a tap does something with glows: an accent halo at 90% drawn under the card (a Gaussian blur, sigma 3 pt, so the picture covers its middle; faded to about 2% by the 6 pt gap to the next card) and a solid 2.5 pt accent edge inside it. A picked card has a wider halo (sigma 4.5 pt, pulled in 1.5 pt so it leaves about 5% on the next card), a 3 pt accent edge and an accent ring with a check at its centre, away from the token corners; a payment shows "-1" in the ring on each hero paying, archery "+2" in a threat-red ring. While a pick is open (a defender, a location, a payment; not the action window), the cards it does not ask about are veiled in night at 45%; in the planning window, so are the hand cards that cannot be played now, and the preview says why. A card the pick asks about may carry its number on a pill just above its token row: the damage a defender would take, on night, or on threat red when it destroys the card. The hand card being played rises whole. While the banner's current beat names a card, it has a 2 pt bone ring. The halo, the edges, the veil and the bone ring are drawn in the card's turned group, so they follow a fanned card's tilt and an exhausted card's quarter turn; the check ring, the pills and the other marks stay upright. On a veiled card its tokens, effect pills and marks fade to 55% with it, while the option pill stays bright. A tap on a fanned card is turned back by its tilt before it is tested, so a tilted card's corner hits that card.

## Motion

The table moves like a game, not a form, and never holds the player up: every token is at most 500 ms, apart from the title sweep, the spotlight's hold and its whole run. A sequence of tokens runs longer (a played card 760 ms, a revealed one about 790, a destroyed one about 570, a hand of six 870), and the player never waits on it. A rule is already resolved when its motion plays. Every length, curve and spring is a token in `src/play/motion-tokens.ts`; `motionScale(speed, reduced)` multiplies each: 1 for Normal, 0.6 for Quick, 0 under reduced motion, which snaps everything and plays no spotlight. **Reduce motion** (Follow system, Always; `reduceMotion`) is a setting beside the phone's: Follow system reads the phone's setting live, Always snaps whatever the phone says (`useReduceMotion`, `motion-run.ts`). **Animation speed** (Normal, Quick; `animationSpeed`, `table-settings.ts`, stored in `user.db`). Pure code works out what each card does (`motion.ts`: `flip`, `delay`, `arc`, `land`, and the cards that left) and its pose at a moment (`motion-pose.ts`, `motion-curves.ts`); the UI thread draws one clock per state (`card-motion.native.tsx`), so the canvas draws again only when what it shows changes and no timer runs while nothing moves.

The scrub's shapes are in `SHAPE`: `scrubScale` 1.5, `scrubLiftScale` 1.55, `scrubLiftRise` 0.25 of the card's height (its foot's rise), `scrubNight` 0.35, `peekVeil` 0.3, and the fan's `fanStep` 0.07 rad, `fanMax` 0.45 rad and `fanRadius` 3 card heights. A spring is named by its damping ratio and the time it takes to settle; the canvas evaluates the same damped oscillator Reanimated integrates, so a state has one clock. Timers that are not motion stay outside the scale: the top bar's 400 ms measure fallback, the dock's second-tap window, the banner's 650 ms beats and 4.5 s hold, the 350 ms settle after a new moment (it starts again when a spotlight on it ends or is skipped), and the 150 ms the sweep waits to see whether the table passes the phase.

- **Move.** An arrival eases out past its slot a little and settles (`cardArrive`); a card that leaves eases in (`cardDepart`). A card that was elsewhere on the table moves from there.
- **Draw.** A card leaves the deck on a shallow arc (a 24 pt rise at mid-path), showing its printed back, and turns to its face at the midpoint. Cards drawn together leave 90 ms apart.
- **Reveal.** An encounter card comes off the encounter deck showing the Eye, turns over, then pops (1.12, a 120 ms hold, a spring back to 1). A dealt shadow card does not flip: it stays face down. A flip shows only the printed back the canvas already has, never a face the player may not see.
- **Play.** A card from the hand lifts to 1.12 (`lift`), glides to its slot (`glide`) and lands squashed to 0.96, springing back to 1 (`land`). The landing ticks once (`tickLand`).
- **Exhaust and ready.** The quarter turn is a spring with a light overshoot (`exhaust`).
- **Leave.** A card destroyed or discarded from play shakes (three steps of 4 pt, 50 ms each), then shrinks toward its pile (`cardLeave`) while it fades out on an ease-out curve: 570 ms in all. One discarded from the hand, an event played, or one returned to a deck glides to the pile in `cardDepart`; a card set aside fades where it stood.
- **Token pop.** When a damage, resource or progress count changes (`tokenDeltas`, `token-deltas.ts`), its token pops, 1 to 1.4 and back on a spring (about 300 ms), once its card has arrived. A count that fell to 0 has no token to pop, so only its number floats. Progress on a quest card or a location pops its strip the same way.
- **Floating number.** A number rises 20 pt from the token and fades over 700 ms: "+2" in the accent for a gain, "−1" in danger for damage or a loss, Marcellus SC 13 pt with a night outline. The numbers are the reducer's.
- **Phase title sweep** (`phase-sweep.tsx`). When the round moves into a new phase, its name in Marcellus SC display crosses the canvas centre (fade in, slide 40 pt, fade out, 900 ms). Taps pass through it. It is skipped under Quick, under reduced motion, when the phase went backward (Undo), when the new state moves cards or changes a count (so a title never runs under a spotlight), and when Stops passed the whole phase (a second phase change within 150 ms takes the first one's place).
- **Seat handoff** (two-handed play). When the acting seat changes, its dial pulses once in an accent ring (1 to 1.12 and back, `seatPulse`) and its hand slides up into place (`seatHand`) while the rest of its table fades in, so the two seats cross-fade; nothing is dealt.
- **Peek.** A peek and its way back have no motion: the table is redrawn from the other seat at once, every card at rest. Only the seat's own zones change (the hand, the play area, the engaged enemies with their cards, the deck and discard pile); staging, the quest, the locations and the encounter piles stay put. No hand rises, no dial pulses, no token pops, no card lands and no haptic plays (`kindMoves` in `motion-kind.ts`). A peek that ends because the player confirmed something on the dock is a new state, so its own motions play, diffed from the acting seat's layout; when the engine turns to the seat the table already shows, that hand does not slide up again.
- **Hurry.** A touch on the canvas finishes the running card motions at once (the pending landing tick is dropped, so no haptic follows a snapped card), skips a combat spotlight that is playing, and does nothing else (it picks, previews and drags nothing); any other touch is handled as usual. Token pops and floating numbers keep running. It never delays or swallows a tap, a drag or a long press outside a spotlight; it is the first touch of a hold on the hand too, so a scrub that starts mid-draw finds the hand at rest; and it touches neither the tray's outside-tap fold nor the More popover's outside-tap close. Nothing animates while the carousel scrolls, the rail does not animate when the table opens, and no motion moves the dock's pinned buttons.

## Spotlight

Plan: `docs/plans/done/combat-spotlight-redesign.md`.

When an attack deals or blocks damage the table plays it large, full screen: an enemy against a defender, an enemy against an undefended hero, or the players' attackers against an enemy. Archery, damage from a card effect, a canceled attack and one that placed no damage step get none (the banner covers them). `clashesOf(prev, next, steps)` (`clash.ts`) reads the engine's `strike`, `damage` and `destroy` steps, and every number on screen is theirs (two attacks on one target count up in turn from the state's own count). It is drawn by its own Skia canvas over the whole table, the top bar and the dock (`spotlight-layer.native.tsx`, always mounted; its canvas, which ignores touches, mounts only while a spotlight plays, so there is no idle loop; a web stub returns null). The table canvas publishes the plan and the clock to it through `spotlight-handoff.ts`.

- **Stage.** Always dark: the layer draws with the Dark tokens on the Light theme too. The dark is one radial gradient over the screen, 78% of the night at the middle and the full night at the edge, at most 88% (`SPOT.dark`), with a pool of light under the cards (the glow at 20%, screen blend). The attacker is at the left (the enemy, or the players' attackers as a fan of up to three, stepped 0.32 of a card's width and turned 6 degrees apart, then a small night chip "+n"), the target at the right, at least 28 pt and 0.22 of a card's width apart. The cards stand at 60% of the screen's height, at most 340 pt, their centres at 47% of the screen's height; the stage shrinks to keep the group, the shadow card included, within the screen's width less 2 times the larger side inset less 32 pt, and to keep the medallions clear of the note chip (`spotlight-stage.ts`; shapes in `SPOT`, `spotlight-shape.ts`). Under each card straddling its foot, a 26 pt medallion in Marcellus SC: the attack with the attack icon (threat red for an enemy, accent for the players) and the defense with the defense icon (accent; none under an undefended hero). The camera pushes in to 1.06 over the charge, holds, and lets go over the last return; the medallions, the number and the stamps take the same push but not the shake, and the note chip stands still.

- **Flash**: warm white over the target only (a radius of 0.55 of a card's height), 70% at its peak (60% of that when blocked), up in 12% of its 160 ms and falling to 0: one bump per attack, so even at Quick it stays under 2 a second, inside WCAG 2.3.1's 3, and it is never saturated red.
- **Shake**: Eiserloh's trauma model on the stage only: trauma starts at 0.55 plus 0.12 per damage (at most 1), 1 on a kill and 0.35 when blocked, falls by 1 over 420 ms, and the offset is 9 pt and 1.2 degrees times trauma squared times a smooth seeded wobble (`shakeAt`; two sines of 5 to 8 Hz and 9 to 13 Hz, so the fastest is under 25 Hz even at Quick).
- **Particles**: closed-form (`spotlight-particles.ts`: a sprite's place comes from its index, the plan's seed and the time, with no state kept between frames), drawn as additive Skia `Atlas` sprites, every glow a gradient and no blur filter: embers 24, converging embers 16, sparks 28, deflection 18 and ash 36, at most 90 alive at once, in three `Atlas` draws of 104 slots (`spotlight-atlas.ts`).
- **Burn**: a destroyed target runs through a noise-threshold dissolve with an ember rim (a runtime shader: `spotlight-burn.ts` for the field and the SkSL, `spotlight-burn.native.tsx`; the shader works in the layer's local coordinates, so it is given the target's stage rectangle in points), the char darkens what is behind the rim, ash rises where the front passes, and "Destroyed" is stamped in threat red. On the last attack it burns over `spotlightBurn` inside the return; in an earlier attack it burns over the 200 ms the next attack takes to swap in. If the shader does not compile (`RuntimeEffect.Make` returns null) the target fades, drops 12 pt and darkens under a night veil (`Veil`).
- **Several attacks** play in turn under one dark: the first enters and does not go back (2700 ms), each later one swaps in over 200 ms with no flight from the slots (a read of 100 ms, a charge of 200 ms, then the same hold, 2200 ms in all, `SPOTLIGHT_AGAIN`) while the one before fades out, and the last goes back (500 ms more). A lone attack runs 3200 ms at Normal and 1920 at Quick; three take 7600 and 4560. A note chip above the bottom safe inset reads "2 of 3 · Tap to skip" ("Tap to skip" for a lone attack).
- **Never gates.** The state is already resolved: the spotlight never gates an intent, and the dock's next moment is ready under it. While it plays, the state's own card motions, the pops of the damage it placed, the banner and the 350 ms settle wait, and all start when it ends. A tap anywhere skips every attack of the moment and lands the table on the new state at once: on the canvas it rides the hurry; over the top bar and the dock a transparent skip catcher takes a press and reaches nothing under it (`spotlight-scrim.tsx`; gone the moment the spotlight ends); Android Back skips first. A reading layer closes when a spotlight begins. Under reduced motion, with a screen reader on, and with the setting Combat spotlight off there is none and the banner carries the attack; the web build has none; Undo plays none.
- The enlarged cards (and the shadow card's face) are decoded again at the stage's size times the 1.06 push-in while it plays, and released when it ends; a card whose file is missing shows its name. Its fonts (the number Marcellus SC 64, the stamps 34, the note chip Barlow Bold 15) load once with the layer (`useSpotFonts`). Nothing else on the table borrows its tokens.

## Gestures

Code: `src/play/hand-gestures.native.ts`; pure geometry in `hand-scrub.ts`.

A tap picks for the open moment, or, on a card the moment does not ask about, shows it large. A long press (350 ms) on a card on the table shows it large; a discard pile's opens its list. A card that can be played, committed, traveled to or engaged can be dragged (a move of 6 pt starts it at once; nothing waits for a hold); the drop picks its target. No drag or drop sends an intent, and a tap sends one only in a moment with one answer (One answer, one tap). While the table peeks at the other seat, a tap on any card only opens its preview, nothing is picked or sent, and nothing can be dragged; a pile still opens its list, the viewed seat's. There is no swipe between seats: the seat is changed by its dial, so the drag-to-play path is never delayed.

## Hold, slide and release

On the hand. A press on a hand card, or on the hand strip between cards, held still for 150 ms (`SCRUB_HOLD_MS`; a move of 6 pt, `SCRUB_SLOP`, before that is a drag) starts a scrub, with one selection tick (`tickArmed`). These are gesture thresholds, outside the motion scale. The scrub starts on the card pressed (between cards, the nearest), and the nearest centre takes over from the first move. The hand rises to show whole cards (`scrubRise`), grows to 1.5 times its size, and fans out like a held hand across the canvas width (`scrubSpread`), overlapping evenly and never leaving less than a 44 pt slice of any card (the scale holds at 1.5 up to about 12 cards; a longer hand gets a smaller scale; a hand too long to keep a 44 pt slice even at its own size spreads at its own size with a thinner slice). The cards sit on an arc about three card heights in radius, each turned 0.07 rad from its neighbour, the fan at most 0.45 rad, so the middle card stands highest and the ends turn and drop, every turned corner on the canvas. The card nearest under the finger straightens and lifts above the others (`scrubLift`), 1.55 larger, grown from its foot, its foot a quarter of its height higher, so it is drawn above the finger. Its neighbours keep the spread's size. The card the finger left settles back over `scrubLift`. Every grown card stays inside the canvas less its 8 pt edge: an end card is moved in, its turned corners counted, and the lifted card grows no taller than the room above its foot (`spread.room.lift`) and rises less where the top edge would cut it. A selection tick plays each time the card under the finger changes, including coming back onto a card after sliding off an end. The canvas dims under night at 35%, a layer of its own; the top bar and the command column stay bright. Letting go on a card opens its preview (`CardPreview`). Sliding more than 44 pt above the top of the risen hand, or letting go off the ends of the hand, cancels: the hand settles back (`scrubFall`) and no preview opens. A scrub sends no intent and picks nothing; Android Back cancels an open one. Under reduced motion the hand shows risen and spread at once, the lift is instant and it settles at once. The scrub runs on the hand of the seat peeked at too, as a preview; a peek that changes the shown seat while a scrub is open cancels the scrub first. On the hand the scrub replaces the 350 ms press. With a screen reader on there is no scrub: the hand cards stay reachable through the list path, and the 350 ms press previews them. The touch that skips a spotlight never starts a scrub. The finger is read on the UI thread (`hand-scrub.native.tsx`, no idle loop); only a change of the card under it crosses to JavaScript, for the tick.

## Peeking at the other seat

Code: two-handed play; `src/play/peek.ts`, `use-peek.ts`, `peek-banner.tsx`.

A tap on the dial of the seat that is not shown shows that seat's whole area: hand, play area, engaged enemies, deck and discard pile (what the canvas, the web list and the pile's list draw is `shownSeat(game, viewedSeat)`; everything that picks, pays or sends reads `actingSeat(game)`). `viewedSeat` belongs to the table store only: not engine state and not in the save. It is null when the table opens and returns to null with every new state (a new sheet key or a change of the acting seat as well), when a sheet opens, on Android Back and on a tap on the acting seat's dial. Who acts never changes by a peek: the dock stays the acting seat's (its title already starts "Player 1 · …"). While it lasts a raised night pill (`peek-banner`) hangs 2 pt under the top bar, centred over the canvas, 36 pt tall with one line of words (at most 1.3 times the system font scale, so it never grows past the 42 pt the table keeps for it): "Viewing Player 2 · Player 1 is acting" ("Player 2 is out of the game" for an eliminated seat, its zones empty) and, at its right, an accent text button "Back to Player 1" (`peek-back`, 36 pt with 4 pt of slop above and below). Only the button takes a touch: the rest of the pill lets taps through to the cards under it. The table's banner moves under the pill while it shows. It fades in and out over `bannerIn` and `bannerOut` and snaps under reduced motion. The viewed hand has the 30% night veil (`peekVeil`), and nothing on the table glows or shows a pick. No spotlight plays during a peek, because the peek has ended by the time a moment arrives. A tap on a dial with the choice tray open folds the tray and also peeks or opens the panel (the tray's outside-tap layer over the bar stops where the dials end, `bar-edge.ts`). A tap that peeks or ends a peek closes the reading layer. True solo has one dial and no peek.

## Command column

The dock alone, at the bottom in thumb reach, with nothing above it. The quest's progress is on the quest card's strip and its preview; the active locations are drawn on the table.

- **The dock** (`table-dock.tsx`) has no panel and no corners: it stands on a fade over the whole command column, from transparent at the canvas side and at the top to the night at the right edge and the foot (one radial gradient, so no straight edge shows). Its words are flush left in two tiers: the moment in Marcellus SC at the heading size, led by the seat in two-handed play ("Player 1 · Choose a defender") and, for a Response or an Action, by its timing ("Response · Pathless Country", `headerOf` `lead`), then one folded line in muted label: the first-game guide when it is on, else the moment's note, else the first thing the rules refuse, in faint (`src/play/dock-lines.ts`). There is no step overline, because the rail names the phase; the step, with the timing of a Response or an Action and "Stops on" while Stops are on, is in the title's spoken label. The folded line is one line under 400 pt of screen height and two above. When more lines wait, or the one is longer than the line holds, a 26 pt round "i" button (`dock-more`, a bone wash, a 44 pt target) beside the title or the line opens a popover; a screen reader hears every line from the folded one.

Ready after this: 2 · Enemies to face: …"; `dockReadout`, `readout.ts`), the reducer's refusal in danger, the armed line, and the buttons. Tap twice to confirm").

It is the confirm of what is picked, its verb alone (Commit 2, Defend, Travel, Attach, Use, Play), or, with nothing picked, the moment's own Pass (Pass, Commit no one, Continue, Done, Keep), or the alternative when there is nothing to pick (Leave undefended with no ready defender). While it waits for a pick it is the same capsule at 35%, with the sentence that says why above it.

The 2 pt bar has 4 pt of its own under the text.

Its rows keep their ids (`sheet-inspect`, `sheet-list`, `table-actions`, `sheet-expand`); a row closes it and then acts; a tap anywhere else closes it and does nothing more; Android Back closes it; it closes by itself when the moment or the pick changes.

## Two taps

A button that sends an intent with nothing picked first (Pass, Keep, Mulligan, Leave undefended, Do not travel, Do not engage, No attack, Continue, Done, Commit no one) arms on the first tap: the armed line says what the second tap does ("Tap again: no one quests", "Tap again: planning ends") in the accent, with a 2 pt accent bar running down under it for three seconds (ten while a screen reader is on; the bar shows the timer on a linear curve, so Quick leaves it as long, and reduced motion leaves it still); the second tap sends it; the bar running out, another button, a new pick or a new moment puts it back. Arming changes no button's size or place, so the second tap lands where the first did. The same line shows what a disabled primary waits for. A primary that confirms a pick sends on one tap, because the pick was the first. Each armed button carries the hint "Tap twice to confirm", and a screen reader hears the armed line when it arms. A confirm that costs more than it shows arms the same way, though something is picked: a defender or a hero hit whose fall loses the game ("Tap again: Frodo Baggins falls and the game is lost"; the readout says why), and an attachment whose text the app does not apply ("Tap again: pay 2 for no effect").

## One answer, one tap

When the engine allows one intent and the sheet shows one answer for it (no character can defend, so Leave undefended is the answer, and with one hero it hits, the dock names that hero, says whether the hero is destroyed, and draws the hero beside the enemy; nothing to travel to; a window with only Pass; a Look's Done; a lone option of a choice; an archery total one character must take), the answer is picked already: its card shows the picked mark, the primary is ready and not armed, the line above it says "The only choice now: one tap sends it" in muted (a screen reader hears "Sends at once"), and one tap on the primary or on the card sends it (`onlyAnswer`, `src/play/table-choice.ts`). A sheet opened from the table never counts: the window behind it has other answers. For 350 ms after a new moment appears no tap sends anything, so the second tap of a double tap meant for the moment before does not land on this one.

## Stops and Always use

With Stops on (the carousel's switch), the dock's spoken step starts with "Stops on ·", and an action window that is not one of the player's stops passes by itself unless a card usable there is new since the player was last asked. While stops are on, an action window offers the answer Pass to stop, armed like Pass, its line naming where the run ends at the latest ("Tap again: pass until before the quest resolves", or "until planning", which always asks); a choice on the way ends it sooner. A Response with no cost and nothing to choose offers Always use, armed ("Tap again: use it now and every time"); the table settings list those Responses, and a tap there asks again. Undo takes back an answer the table gave, and the table then asks at that window.

## Guide

While the first-game guide is on (the default), the dock's folded line is "Guide: …" in muted, the first two times each step's own decision comes up (the step's decision from the turn reference, or what an action window is for). It is not said in a Response or a pick inside a step, nor when the note already says it, and it is the one line the dock shows (the note waits behind the "i" pill), so on a short phone it never pushes the buttons away. It is turned off in the settings.

## Choice tray

## Phase carousel

Code: `src/play/phase-carousel.tsx`, columns in `carousel-columns.tsx`, data `phaseCarousel` in `src/play/carousel.ts`, every word from the turn reference through `data/rules`.

A tap on the rail opens one panel of the round. It is for reading, so it is a lit night panel (`floatingSurface`, opaque, 20 pt corners, a deep shadow), not paper, floating 8 pt inside the canvas and 4 pt under the top bar, over the canvas only, so the dock stays usable and the panel and the choice never cover each other. A tap outside closes it (`carousel-scrim`) and does nothing more; the rail again or Android Back closes it too; it sends no intent. It drops in `carouselEnter` (12 pt down with a fade) and leaves in `carouselLeave`; it snaps under reduced motion, and nothing animates while it scrolls. It is the room under the bar less 8 pt (or the foot's safe inset, if larger), at most 340 pt (`carouselHeight`, `carousel-size.ts`).

- **Phases** (left column, 132 to 168 pt): "Round 1" (or "Before the round") in a muted Marcellus SC label on one line at the top, 20 pt in from the panel's edge so it clears the corner, then the round's seven phases in `ROUND` order (Resource, Planning, Quest, Travel, Encounter, Combat, Refresh) as 38 pt rows with a 3 pt hit slop each side for the 44 pt target (`carousel-phase-<id>`; the label and all seven rows fit the panel at 667 × 375 and 844 × 390 with no scrolling, `carousel-size.test.ts`), each a dot and the name in Marcellus SC caps: the dot is accent now, accentDeep when done, an outline to come; the name accent when current, bone to come, muted past. The phase being read is washed in the accent with 12 pt corners; a tap on a row shows that phase's steps. The game's own phase is shown on open (the first before the Resource phase, the last after Refresh) and the column opens scrolled to it. A screen reader hears "Combat phase, now" or "done this round".
- **Steps** (right column) of the phase being read: the master Stops switch (`carousel-stops`, the word Stops with the setting's sentence as its hint) at the top right, with the accent S and "A stop is ahead" (`carousel-stop-ahead`) beside it while a stop is ahead.
- Each step (`carousel-step-<id>`) stands on a plain dot timeline: a dot on a line to the next step (accent with a soft ring now, accentDeep when done, an outline to come), its rule titles in bone label (accentHigh now, muted when done), the player's decision in muted, and the step's action windows as Stop switches (`carousel-stop-<id>`), each a whole row at least 44 pt tall. No plate, card, border, number disc, Now chip or arrow draws; the column opens on the current step and shows its scroll indicator.

## Quest advance

Code: `quest-advance.ts`, `use-quest-advance.ts`, `quest-advance-view.tsx`.

When an intent the player sent advances the quest, and when the table opens on a game that is not over (so a new game shows its first quest card), a screen like the card preview opens over the table, after any spotlight: the stage it reached, large on the left on its B side (`picture-key.ts`; side A where the build has none), and a column at its right, one stack: "Stage 2 of 3" as an accent overline, the stage's name in the title face ("A Fork in the Road"), and a muted line with its quest points, the printed line that wins it, or the rule that holds it back in its own sentence, with no lead. No Details button; a tap on the dimmed screen does nothing. Under the words, one primary (`quest-advance-progress`) goes back to the table: Begin on the opening of a new game, Continue on the opening of a resumed one and on a stage held back, Progress on a stage that can progress (`advanceButton`); Android Back does the same. When the stage's own When Revealed asks the player something (41004:B:1, 41005:A:1), the question stands there in place of Progress: the dock's step and title, its options as tiles (`quest-advance-option-<id>`) and the dock's buttons with their two taps (`DockButtons`); once answered the screen goes back to the table by itself. While it is up the table answers nothing for the player (Stops, Always use); it answers the moment once the screen closes. The engine has advanced already: the screen holds the moment so the new stage is read before the round goes on.

## Undo

Undo is the first of the dock's quiet pills. It takes back the last choice while it showed nothing hidden (`docs/brief.md`, The engine plays the game); each tap goes back one more, and it leaves the dock once a card has been revealed since. While a pick is being made, its first tap puts back the pick only ("Put back this pick"); while a sheet opened from the table is up, Cancel is there instead. The cards move back the way they came. The banner says "Taken back", the choice in words ("Frodo Baggins committed to the quest") and what it had done.

## Haptics

A tick when a button arms, a confirm when an intent is sent or taken back, a reject when the reducer refuses one, a light tick when a card played from the hand lands (`tickLand`, at most one per new state), a medium impact at a spotlight's hit stop (`tickImpact`, at most one per strike), and the scrub's selection tick (`tickArmed`; no scrub runs during a spotlight). The confirm plays the moment the reducer accepts the intent, before the table redraws and the save is written (a refused intent gets only the reject); the landing tick plays as the card's motion clock passes the landing, so a touch that snaps the motion plays none. On iOS the selection, light and medium impact and warning feedback; on Android the system's Segment tick, Confirm, Reject and Long press constants (`performAndroidHapticsAsync`), which follow the phone's touch-feedback setting, never the vibrator. They add to the screen and never replace it. The **Haptics** setting (`haptics`, on by default) turns all of them off.

## Banner

Code: `moment-banner.tsx`, `banner-span.ts`.

After each intent that changed the table, a panel centred on the screen at its top edge, 4 pt under the safe area, over the top bar, the table and any open panel, never over the command column and its tray, says what happened. It has 20 pt corners and no outline. On Dark it is night at 90% with no lit edge; the combat banner, for a failure or an attack, is a dark red under a 3 pt threat-red band along its top, the only line it draws. Light keeps its pale `raisedTop` at 90%. The corners are a style on the filled view (a class on a reanimated view does not apply). The title in Marcellus SC and up to five centred lines with their marks, 260 to 440 pt wide, narrower when the screen's middle leaves less room, never wider than the screen less 16 pt a side: "Quest failed · Willpower 3 against threat 5 · Threat +2", "Black Rider attacks · Attack 3 against Sam Gamgee's defense 2 · Shadow, Hasty Departure: … · 1 damage on Sam Gamgee", "Traveled to Bag End".

- A tap closes it; a swipe sideways (56 pt) or up (34 pt), or a flick, slides it off and closes it (a shorter drag springs back; its round summary, when one follows, then shows); a screen reader's escape gesture or its Dismiss action closes it too. It is announced on both platforms (`announceForAccessibility`). A new moment enters afresh even while the last is up.
- The banner and each line fade in (`bannerIn`) and the banner fades out (`bannerOut`). Its lines resolve in beats: the first with the title, then one more every 650 ms (a timer, not a token), each ringing the cards it names on the table (reduced motion shows every line at once and rings none). It closes 4.5 seconds after its last line shows; the Log keeps every step.
- The moment that ends a round (not setup) is followed by the round's summary in the same banner, "Round 3 ends": the threat before and after when it moved, progress placed, damage taken and dealt, encounter cards defeated or explored, cards drawn. When an attack lost its defender before it struck, a line says "With Sam Gamgee gone, the attack is undefended".
- When the moment resolved an attack that plays as a spotlight, the banner enters, and its beats and hold begin, when the spotlight ends or is skipped. Where there is no spotlight it shows at once.

## Android Back

First skips a playing spotlight, then cancels an open scrub, then ends a peek, then closes the table menu or the dock's More or "i" popover, then the preview, then the reading layer (the carousel, a panel, the log, a pile's list), then puts back a sheet opened from the table, then folds the choice tray, and only then leaves the table.
