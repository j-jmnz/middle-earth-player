# Design system — Table (landscape)

The table screen's design. The rest of the design system (`docs/design-system.md`: tokens, Standards, Performance, Components, States) applies here too; this file only adds what is the table's own.

## Overview

The table is played in landscape only (`orientation` on its stack screen), with the status bar hidden and the stack's edge swipe off (a drag from the hand's edge must not leave the table; Leave the table in the menu does). Scene `table`: the canvas is transparent over its fog, with no felt and no zone boxes. Under the top bar, the canvas at the left and the command column at the right (30% of the width, 210 to 280 pt), both inside the side safe-area insets. Phone checks of the canvas are open (`docs/phases/STATUS.md`): the web build lists the zones instead of drawing them.

## Top bar

Code: `src/screens/table/top-bar.tsx`, `src/play/chrome/top-bar-layout.ts`.

52 pt (`TOP_BAR`: the 46 pt dial with 3 pt above and below), under the top safe-area inset and inside both side ones, starting at least 8 pt under the screen's top edge (`BAR_TOP`) so a dial's pop stays on the screen. Where a status bar stays (an iPad), the bar starts right under its inset, counted once: the table's `Screen` bleeds and the table insets itself. Three zones and nothing else: the threat dials at the left, the phase rail centred on the screen, one menu button at the right.

- **The threat dial** (`ThreatRing` compact, `table-chrome.tsx`): 46 pt round, a ring of threat red (`ArcRing`) filling toward 50, the elimination level (`rr.threat-elimination-level`), on a faint red track, around a dark disc holding the number alone in Marcellus SC 20, with a red glow; its label still says "threat". A tick on the ring at each staging enemy's engagement cost (not one that cannot be engaged), bone where the threat has reached it and faint where not; once stage 2B has split the table each dial reads its own seat's staging area (`dialTicksSelector`), and its name counts that seat's enemies. It warms near elimination (`src/play/readouts/threat-level.ts`): from 40 the ring turns danger; from 45 the disc fills red and a screen reader hears "near elimination". The dial is one control whose name says how many engage at this threat and, while it shows, the change. When the number changes the dial pops (`dialPopRise` to 1.22, then `dialPopSettle`) and a badge floating over the disc's foot (position absolute, no touch, above the dial, its foot ending inside the bar's foot (`badgeFoot`) so the bar never clips it; it takes no room, so the bar's height never changes) says the change for three seconds, "−6" on an accent wash when it fell, "+2" on a threat-red wash when it rose, with no edge, read as "Threat lowered by 6, now 24"; reduced motion keeps the dial still and shows the badge. Dials stand 10 pt apart, each with its seat's label (P1) beside it. Life, then the Burden deck (labelled "Burden", read as "Burden deck"), are the same dial with no meter and no levels, when declared. On Nightmare the Eye of Sauron follows them, in threat red with the word Nightmare beside it (always: the peeked seat's eye mark is another eye), with the dials' pressed fill; a tap opens the setup card's preview (`nightmare-card`). The Island Map chip (The Island Map) follows the seat dials, and the Heading dial (below) the chip.
- **The Heading dial** (`HeadingDial`, `table-chrome.tsx`; `src/play/dream-chaser/heading.ts`): in the top bar after the seat dials, while the Heading card is in play (`heading`; `dcce.heading`). It is the Life dial's frame, 46 pt, with no number and the label "Heading", in a label column as wide as the widest of "Heading", "Toward Sun" and "Toward Storm" (`HEADING_LABEL_W`), so the dial's width, and with it the rail, does not move when the badge comes and goes. While the dials would scroll, or would take the rail below `RAIL_WIDE`, the dial is compact (`headingCompact`): the disc alone, "Heading" left to the spoken label, and the badge laid over the disc's foot; otherwise the badge floats under the label and takes no room. The disc holds the current step's icon (Sun, Cloud, Rain, Storm: the MEC111 rulebook's own icons, cut from its embedded font by `pnpm data:sync icons`), and the ring is four quarter arcs lit clockwise from the top: Sun four, Cloud three, Rain two, Storm one. Sun is the accent, Cloud and Rain bone, Storm threat red with the disc filled; the icon's shape and the arcs carry the step without the colour. `heading-value` is clear text with the step's word ("Rain"), as `rail-current` is. It is heard as "Heading, Rain, off-course, two steps from on-course" with the hint "Shows the Heading card". A shift pops the dial as the threat dial pops and a badge (`heading-delta`) says the direction of the shift, "Toward Sun" on an accent wash or "Toward Storm" on a threat-red wash, for three seconds (a Storm-to-Rain shift is still off-course, so the badge words the direction and not the state), heard as "Heading shifted off-course, now Rain"; reduced motion keeps the dial still and shows the badge. A tap opens the Heading card's preview, titled "Heading · Rain (off-course)", listing the four steps with the current one marked and drawing the card turned a quarter turn per step; a peek never changes the dial, and a peek at the other seat leaves it where it is. There is no stepper.
- **Two seats**: the labels are P1 and P2, read as "Player 1 threat, acting, 28" and "Player 2 threat, viewing, 33". The acting seat's label is in the accent. A tap on the dial of a seat that is not shown peeks at it (hint "Shows Player 2's cards", "Shows Player 2's area" once stage 2B has split the table; Peeking); a tap on the shown seat's dial opens its threat panel (Reading panels). A seat peeked at that is not acting wears a bone ring just outside its dial, a small eye mark at its top-right (`threat-<seat>-viewing`, `threat-<seat>-eye`, drawn as views) and a bone label; neither touches the Ring mark in the label row, which keeps its place at 667 × 375 with two seats. Only the acting seat's dial pulses (Seat handoff); a peek never does.
- **Held marks**: while the Ring-bearer is facedown under a card, the seat that gets it back (always the first player) wears the printed One Ring, 12 pt, in the accent beside its label, and the dial's name says "The Ring-bearer is facedown under Watcher in the Water" (`threat-<seat>-ring`). The seat whose hero is the prisoner of Escape from Dol Guldur wears the printed hero icon in bone in the same slot, and its dial says "Aragorn is the prisoner, face down" (`heldDial`, `src/play/readouts/widgets.ts`).
- **The phase rail** (`PhaseRail`, `src/play/phases/phase-rail.tsx`): no pill and no edge. The phase in Marcellus SC 17 tracked 4 with a silver glow, and the round's seven phases as ticks under it (22 × 3, 5 apart: past accentLine, current accentHigh and glowing, to come dark). Outside the seven the phase reads Beginning or End; Setup and the mulligan have no phase, so their step stands in its place. The round and the step are not in the rail at all (the carousel names the round); the rail's label says them, and `rail-current` is the headline only when the step stands in the phase's place. When the rail is wider than `RAIL_WIDE`, the previous and next phase names stand inline at the phase's sides in muted label, in equal slots so the phase stays on the rail's middle; they change in place. An accent S follows the phase while Stops are on and one of the player's stops lies ahead in this phase (`rail-stop-ahead`); its room is kept on both sides. A phase change brightens the glow (`railFlash`, 300 ms), which snaps under reduced motion, and what is on the table when it opens appears without fading.
- **Rail layout**: the rail is centred on the screen, as wide as it can be without reaching the last dial or the menu button, and never under `RAIL_FLOOR`, computed from its own words (the widest phase, and Setup and Mulligan, whose step stands in the phase's place) and ticks; a test checks every one against it. When the dials need more room than that leaves them (two seats with Life and the Burden deck on a phone), the dials scroll sideways behind a seven-band fade at the left zone's right edge, rather than the rail leaving the middle, and the row scrolls to keep the Heading dial and its badge whole inside the zone, clear of the fade (`headingOffset`; at Large on a 667 pt phone with two seats the first seat's dial is partly scrolled out, and the row scrolls back to it); only when the menu side cannot hold half the floor does the rail move. Until the bar and the dials are measured the rail is held invisible, so it does not jump when the table opens.
- A tap on the rail opens the phase carousel and a second tap closes it; the rail is heard as a button that expands. The rail and a dial show a faint bone fill while pressed or open.
- **The menu button** is the round glass button (`GlassIconButton`, `glassSurface`), 44 pt, with three bone dots drawn as views (accent while the menu is open), label "Menu" (`table-menu`).

## Table menu

A popover under the menu button, 240 pt wide, over the table and any open panel and never on the canvas. Its frame is shared with the dock's More and "i" popovers (`popover-frame.tsx`): an opaque lit night card (`floatingSurface`, no drawn edge, so the dock's words never read through it) with 20 pt corners and a deep shadow, its rows 48 pt with 12 pt corners, 6 pt inside its edge, that fill when pressed. Six rows with no chevron, five of bone text that open something over the table: Log (`log-open`), Rules (`rules-open`), Booklet (`booklet-open`), Setup card (`setup-card-open`: the scenario's campaign card on its Setup side in the card preview, with the Nightmare setup card beside it on Nightmare; Back closes it as it closes any preview) Table settings (`settings-open`) and Resign (`resign-open`, danger: it asks "Resign this scenario?" and on Resign sends the `Resign` intent, which loses the game, `lossReason` "The players resigned", so the outcome opens like any loss); then, apart at the foot and set off by 8 pt of space with no rule, Leave the table (`table-back`) in danger, after a door mark drawn as views. A screen reader hears where it goes ("Back to the path") and the hint "The game is saved". Rules opens the glossary (`glossary.tsx`, the Rules tab's list without the books, `NavBar` Back to the table; it and a rule turn with the phone). A row closes the menu and then acts; a tap anywhere outside closes it and sends nothing (a full-screen Pressable under the popover, not a Modal, which would take over the orientation lock and the status bar). It enters in `menuEnter` (scale 0.96 to 1 from its corner with a fade), leaves in `menuLeave` from where it stood, and snaps under reduced motion.

## Quest scale

Code: `quest-scale.ts`, `quest-meter-view.tsx`, `command-head.ts`, `threat-sources.ts`.

The quest meter stands in the command column's head above the dock (Command column), in the Quest phase only (outside it the head is empty, no dimmed bars): the staging area's threat and the willpower committed, as two vertical bars from one baseline, their heights in proportion to the larger of the two (at least 1). Threat is on the left on threat red with its number above and "staging" under it (one small word per bar; the player's own threat dial in the top bar has none; once stage 2B has split the table between players it names the seat whose area it counts, "P1 staging": it is the acting seat's whichever seat a peek draws), willpower on the right on silver with its number above and "willpower" under it. Between them the difference ("+N" in green, "−N" in red, or Even), and a line across the willpower bar at the threat's height, so the gap reads at a glance. Every bar rises or falls to each new value (`meterFill`); reduced motion snaps it. The meter mounts on entering the Quest phase with its bars already at their values and leaves with it (a Planning "−3 Short" before anyone could commit would be a false alarm). While the players commit it reads the dock's quest readout, which adds each character picked; otherwise the engine's `stagingThreat` and `committedWillpower` (the acting seat's own staging area once stages are separate). A screen reader hears one line (`scaleLabel`): the threat in staging, the willpower committed and how far ahead or short it is. The meter is one button (hint "Shows the cards that make up the threat"; it dims while pressed): a tap opens the staging threat list (Reading panels). While the choice tray is open, a tap there folds the tray first, as any tap on the column above the dock does.

With 44 to 100 pt of room the meter is compact: two short horizontal bars with their numbers and the marker beside the willpower.

## Canvas

The left column is one and a half cards wide: the quest card, sideways, level with staging, with a strip at its foot showing progress / quest points; the active location level with the engagement area (two side by side, smaller, when the scenario opens a second slot); the player's deck and discard pile level with the play area. To its right, top to bottom: staging, with the encounter deck and its discard pile at its right end; the engagement area, told apart by a red haze toward its left, where the enemies stand, with the second slot of piles at its right end when the scenario has a second deck (Piles); the play area; the hand, peeking up from the bottom edge above the bottom safe-area inset: 56% of a card and never less than 52 pt, so each hand card keeps a 44 pt target past the fan's drop. The hand's cards are the table's card, overlapping evenly when the hand is long, each covering at most half of the card under it; past that they narrow to the largest card that keeps that half, never under 36 pt (`handCardSize`). The card size fits three rows, the hand's strip and seven and a half cards across: about 51 × 71 pt on an 844 × 390 phone. An empty zone shows one faint word (Staging, No enemy engaged, No location). Once Breaking of the Fellowship's stage 2B has split the table the staging row, the quest card and the active location are the shown seat's own (no second active location). Between two players the canvas says whose area it draws (`seatTag`, `stagingWord`, `words/area.ts`): the empty staging row's word is "P2 staging" (the web list's heading too), a small caps tag in the same words stands upward in the gutter at the row's left end (`StagingTag`, clear of the cards), and the quest card's progress strip reads "P2 · 2 / 8". True solo keeps "Staging" and a bare "2 / 8". An almost invisible 1 pt element (`table-area`, `area-summary.tsx`; its alpha is 0.01, not 0, because VoiceOver skips a view that is clear) holds one accessible line about the area: "Player 2's staging area · Stage Orc Hunting · 3 cards · Active location Sarn Gebir", and a screen reader announces the new words when they change while no peek is open. The canvas takes the whole width left of the command column.

## Spacing and the play area's rows

Code: `layout.ts`; shares and caps in `table-spacing.ts`, all pure and tested.

The rows are laid out at their minimum gaps first (the 8 pt gutter, 6 pt gaps, 10 pt peeks, Medium's sizes times the UI scale). The room left between the play area's one row and a gap above the hand, less a 12 pt slack (times the scale) that is never shared, goes to four gaps by shares, each capped at a share of the card's height: above staging 0.1 (cap 0.15), staging to the engagement area 0.2 (cap 0.3), the engagement area to the play area 0.35 (cap 0.4), the play area to the hand 0.35 (cap 0.4). A capped gap's overflow goes to the others; what no gap can take stays as ground above the hand. The play area's extra rows take their room from the bottom up: the ground, the gap above the hand, the gap above the play area, then the upper gaps, so a card exhausting into a second row moves nothing above the play area while there is ground. The card's cap is 120 pt (Medium's, times the scale). Ground still left past every cap raises the hand, up to its whole card above the bottom edge, so a tablet player reads the hand without the scrub; the hand comes back down when the play area's extra rows need that ground. Across, only when the card is at its cap, the width past a table of capped cards (7.5 cards, three gutters and five gaps) goes to the left edge 0.25 (cap 0.25 of a card's width), the gap beside the left column 0.5 (cap 0.4) and the right edge 0.25 (cap 0.25); the rest widens the rows. The left column follows the rows' y values and the left edge. A phone has no slack past the 12 pt, so its table is unchanged by this, which a test pins. The marks' room, the peeks and the 44 pt floors do not change.

The play area wraps: a row takes cards left to right a gap apart (an exhausted card by its sideways footprint) and a card that would not fit starts the next row; attachments peek under their host, a committed character stands a half peek higher, and each row is as deep as its card and its deepest attachments. When the extra rows do not fit and one row would squeeze each card under its neighbour by more than a fifth of a card (or under a 44 pt step), the play area's cards shrink, all to one size, to the largest that lets their rows fit, down to 0.7 of the table's card and never under a 44 pt target (`PLAY_FLOOR`); a slighter squeeze keeps the table's card, so the play area does not change size each time a card exhausts. Under that floor the cards overlap, keeping 44 pt targets where they can, by whichever has the larger card: one row at the floor's size while every step is still a target, or two stacked rows (`play-area.ts`, `stacked`): the front row whole and the back row showing a 44 pt band of its top (the title, where a tap lands), both of the largest card that leaves the front row's foot at most 10 pt (`HAND_TUCK`, times the scale) under the top of the hand's strip, which the hand is drawn over. A back-row card's attachments peek under the front row (the preview lists them), its tokens and effect pills are drawn inside the band that shows (`coveredFrom`, `shownBounds`), so a hero's resources are never hidden under the front row, and a committed front-row card stands its half peek over the back row's band. When neither keeps a target, the play area is one row of the table's card, squeezed. `zones.play`, the drop target for playing a card, covers every row; the deck and the discard pile stay level with the first. A card that changes rows slides (`useCardMotion`).

- **Ships first.** A ship-objective is an objective ally and a character for all purposes (`dcce.ship-objectives`), so it stands in its controller's play-area row at the table's card size. A seat's row is its player side quest, then its ships (the Dream-chaser before any other), then its heroes and the rest, with one more gap (`SHIP_GAP`, one `GAP`) after the last ship so the ships read as a group without a box. In two stacked rows the ships go to the front row's left end, so their attachments still peek and their damage stays in reach. The Modification boon and the Heading card peek out under their ship as any attachment does, the most recent lowest; the printed stats are on the picture, a Modification's change is an effect pill, and damage is the damage token. The other seat's ship is seen by peeking at that seat.
- **Squeeze, then fold** (`row-fold.ts`, pure, the table's crowding budget as `dock/budget.ts` is the dock's): it lays out staging and the engagement area for every scenario. Groups (a card with its stack, a guarded pair, an engaged enemy with its shadows and fighters) stand a gap apart; when they do not fit they overlap evenly while every step between them is at least a 44 pt target (`touch`), exactly as `rowXs` squeezes a row; when even 44 pt steps do not fit, groups leave the row one at a time, in fold order, into a fold stack at the row's left end until the rest fits at 44 pt steps beside it. A card the open moment asks about, picks, or the banner's beat names never folds; neither does an enemy that is attacking or has a fighter beside it. The order in staging: the Stormcaller's area's cards (never its stage) while they are not at the players' stage (see Cards), then cards the players control (marked `claimed` by the engine; a freed tower is one, built by its scenario phase), then guarded pairs, then locations, then enemies, and last of all a location with enemies grappled under it (its count and the Threat they add are on it; the fold list reads them), the leftmost first within each; in the engagement area the enemies that are not attacking and not in the open moment, oldest first. The quest meter, the threat dial and the threat panel still count every folded card. A folded card is not on the table (`onTableFor`), so a pick that reaches one opens as the choice tray, as a card out of play does.
- **Staging stacks.** In a sea game (`sea` in the state), a staging card with more than one card under it (a grappled arm, Black Key on the Raider Flagship) shows one peek with a "×2" count on it; a card with one card under it shows that peek, as before. The engagement row starts below the peek (it hangs one peek below the card, deeper than the gap), so the count never sits under an enemy. Every other game peeks each card under a staging card as it always did, and a row with nothing under any card is unchanged. A crowded Fellowship or Mirkwood table, whose rows keep 44 pt steps, is laid out slot for slot as it was, which a test pins against the old layout.

## Pictures on the table

Code: `table-pictures.ts`, `table-images.native.ts`.

Each code is decoded once at the largest box the canvas draws it in: a hand card at the scrub's lifted size (the table's card times `scrubScale` times `scrubLiftScale`, about 2.3 times), any other card at its slot's (a sideways quest card at its sideways box), at the screen's pixel ratio (half of it on Low card pictures, never below 1; the spotlight keeps the screen's). The file is brought down in halving steps with linear filtering, then drawn to the size, so a 1200 px scan does not alias, and the full decode is released. A copy is decoded again only when it is too small for where its card now is or more than twice the pixels it needs, and the old copy is drawn until the new one is ready; a code that leaves the table is released, except the `PARKED` codes it stopped drawing last (`table-images.native.ts`), so the seat a two-handed table turns back to shows its pictures at once. The canvas draws every picture with linear mipmap sampling. A test keeps a crowded table (8 cards in hand and 32 other codes) under 16 MB of copies on a 3× phone and 36 MB on a 2× 12.9" tablet at Large. Judge sharpness on a release build: a dev build without bundled pictures draws the smaller RingsDB files.

## Cards

Pictures with a drop shadow; a card whose file is missing shows its name wrapped in Barlow, its sphere icon in the corner. A face-down card shows its printed back: the Eye for a shadow card or an encounter card, the One Ring for a player card. Exhausted cards lie sideways and take a sideways footprint in their row; their tokens and effects stay upright. A committed character stands half a step higher with an accent willpower pill at its foot. In the enemy attacks step each engaged enemy wears its attack on a threat-red pill and each character its defense on an accent pill, in the same place (`CombatPill`); the values count every effect, and the effect pills down the card's left edge stay. Attachments and face-down shadow cards peek out under their host, the most recent lowest; an attachment is drawn under its host only. A face-up shadow card stands beside its enemy at 80%. Cards facedown under a location (Weather Hills) are out of play and not drawn.

- **Not applied**: a card with a printed line the app does not apply carries a small mark at its right edge, just under the damage token's corner, upright: a faint ring struck through on a night disc, 13 pt. Never a band over the picture, never a word on the canvas, not accent and not danger. The preview lists the lines under Not applied (`docs/plans/done/inactive-text.md`).
- **Combat**: the enemy whose attack is in progress has a 3 pt threat-red edge and its attack icon at its side from the moment it attacks. A defender, the hero an undefended attack hits, or the attackers stand beside the enemy, out of the play area, with a mark between them (an accent defense icon, a red attack icon when undefended, an accent attack icon for the players' attack): from the moment the player picks them, fainter until confirmed, and until the attack ends.
- **Escape from Dol Guldur.** An encounter card that guards an objective lies over it, shifted right by 72% of its width, so the objective's name and left side stay readable, and the pair keeps a wider gap from the next card in staging; its preview says "Guarded by Hill Troll", or "Free of encounters: you can claim it". The prisoner is its player's hero, facedown in that seat's play area with the card held under it (Mendor) peeking out below; its back wears a small bone hero mark on a night disc; its preview says "Aragorn is the prisoner, face down" (the cards held with it stay hidden), and the web list reads "Prisoner: Aragorn, face down". An encounter card attached to a deck (Iron Shackles) peeks out above the deck by a step, and the deck wears a small bone chain on its count strip; a tap on the deck opens the card, "Iron Shackles: your next draw is discarded" (the web list says "· Iron Shackles attached").
- **Quest card**: its preview is titled by its stage and progress ("Flight to the Ford · 2 / 8"). A stage that prints 0 quest points and is won another way ("Don't Leave the Path!", 01121) shows no "25 / 0" strip, and its preview says "To win: …". The preview names what holds a full stage back in the rule's own sentence ("Players cannot defeat this stage while any Hill Troll cards are in play."), so a strip past its quest points (13 / 8) reads as the rule. When a branch chose the stage (01120:B:1), the banner says "The path was chosen at random" and the stage's printed lines but its When Revealed.
- **Ships in a pick.** A ship-enemy is an enemy for all purposes (`dcce.ship-enemies`): in staging it shows its engagement cost pill, and its cost puts a tick on the threat dial. The defender pick for a ship-enemy's attack glows the ships and veils every other character; the veiled card's preview, headed "Cannot be chosen now", gives the engine's own refusal ("Only a ship-objective can defend against a ship-enemy's attack."), and attacking a non-ship enemy veils the ships the same way ("A ship-objective can only attack a ship-enemy."). The table adds no words of its own, and outside a sea game a veiled card says nothing. An undefended hit goes to a ship; with one ship, One answer, one tap names it and draws it beside the enemy, with two the pick asks which one. The spotlight has nothing new: a ship defending against a ship-enemy, an undefended hit on a ship, and ships with heroes attacking a ship-enemy are clashes like any other, the ship with its own defense or attack on the medallion.
- **Cards under The Havens Burn and Aflame** (`dcce.aflame`). A destroyed Aflame location lies facedown under The Havens Burn, out of play, and is not drawn. The Burn wears a night pill at its foot, as the engagement cost pill, with the short count of cards under it against the number that loses: "2/5" (5 with two players, 4 with one; `UnderMarkView`), narrow enough to stay inside the card at every size; the pill turns threat red when one more card loses. Only the count is ever shown, never which cards lie under the Burn. The preview of the Burn says it in words ("2 cards are facedown underneath it, out of play. The players lose at 5."), and with a screen reader on the Burn has a 44 pt button over it (`reader-<id>`) that says "The Havens Burn, 2 under, lose at 5" and opens the preview. Each Aflame location in play wears the same kind of pill with its damage against its value, "3/5" (`FireMarkView`: the damage token art and the count, the art left out when the card is too narrow for it), threat red when one more damage destroys it; the preview of an Aflame location says "Aflame 5: 2 of 5 damage, 3 more damage place it underneath The Havens Burn". The damage on a location in staging is also the damage token with its count, as on any card. No control types a count.
- **The Stormcaller's area** (`dcce.stormcallers-area`, `dcce.stormcallers-area-stages`; code: `stormcaller-table.ts`, `words/stormcaller.ts`).
  - **Place.** The area is the first group of the staging row, right beside the players' quest card, only in a game with a Stormcaller's area. It holds the Stormcaller's current stage (2D to 4D), drawn sideways at a staging card's sideways footprint, with the quest strip's progress / quest points set close ("12/18") in the part of the stage its cards leave uncovered. The area's cards (the Stormcaller first, then Sahír's Escort, Vast Coastland) stand over the stage's right part, stepped as a guarded pair (`PAIR_STEP`, and never less than a 44 pt target, the last card's strip counted against the next group; `stormcaller-layout.ts`). The stage keeps its tap target where the cards leave it, and a pill on its uncovered corner names its number ("2", "3", threat red "4/4" at the last stage, where filling it loses the game). The stage never folds: it carries the Stormcaller's progress, which stays visible whatever folds. The stage and the Stormcaller show the side they lie on (`12016.B`, `110042.B`).
  - **Not at the players' stage.** The area's cards carry the night veil at 30% (`SHAPE.peekVeil`). Their previews say "In the Stormcaller's area: not in the staging area, immune to player card effects". The stage wears an "Apart" plate, which the veil on the cards does not say. The area's cards are the first to fold (the stage stays with its strip), as a stack that wears the Stormcaller's face, the word "Area" and the count "+n" of the cards folded, the stage not among them; when only they are in it, its list is titled "Stormcaller's area" and a screen reader hears "3 more cards in the Stormcaller's area". A mixed stack (the area's cards and others) wears the first other folded card's face and the word "More". A card the open moment asks about never folds. The stage's preview reads "The Stormcaller's stage 3 of 4 · 12/18 · the players are at stage 2: its cards are out of reach", and at the last stage that filling it loses.
  - **At the players' stage.** The veil lifts and the cards count as staging, in place, so nothing jumps; the group then folds as a guarded pair does. The stage's preview says "the players are at its stage: its cards are in the staging area".
  - **Progress and advance.** The Stormcaller's progress pops the strip as the quest card's does. A stage advance grows the new stage in where the last one stood and lands it with a pop, as a revealed card does; the last stage fades out. The advance to stage 3 is a beat of the banner that reads the C side's When Revealed; the advance to stage 4 opens the quest advance screen on the C side ("The Stormcaller is at stage 4 of 4"). Hidden Cove's travel row states "Stormcaller 10 → 12 of 12: it advances" (or "the players lose") and arms the confirm.

## Motion

The table moves like a game, not a form, and never holds the player up: every token is at most 500 ms, apart from the title sweep, the spotlight's hold and its whole run. A sequence of tokens runs longer (a played card 760 ms, a revealed one about 790, a destroyed one about 570, a hand of six 870), and the player never waits on it. A rule is already resolved when its motion plays. Every length, curve and spring is a token in `src/ui/motion-tokens.ts`; `motionScale(speed, reduced)` multiplies each: 1 for Normal, 0.6 for Quick, 0 under reduced motion, which snaps everything and plays no spotlight. **Reduce motion** (Follow system, Always; `reduceMotion`) is a setting beside the phone's: Follow system reads the phone's setting live, Always snaps whatever the phone says (`useReduceMotion`, `src/ui/motion-run.ts`). **Animation speed** (Normal, Quick; `animationSpeed`, `table-settings.ts`, stored in `user.db`). - **Exhaust and ready.** The quarter turn is a spring with a light overshoot (`exhaust`).
- **Leave.** A card destroyed or discarded from play shakes (three steps of 4 pt, 50 ms each), then shrinks toward its pile (`cardLeave`) while it fades out on an ease-out curve: 570 ms in all. One discarded from the hand, an event played, or one returned to a deck glides to the pile in `cardDepart`; a card set aside fades where it stood.
- **Token pop.** When a damage, resource or progress count changes (`tokenDeltas`, `token-deltas.ts`), its token pops, 1 to 1.4 and back on a spring (about 300 ms), once its card has arrived. A count that fell to 0 has no token to pop, so only its number floats. Progress on a quest card or a location pops its strip the same way.
- **Floating number.** A number rises 20 pt from the token and fades over 700 ms: "+2" in the accent for a gain, "−1" in danger for damage or a loss, Marcellus SC 13 pt with a night outline. The numbers are the reducer's.
- **Phase title sweep** (`phase-sweep.tsx`). When the round moves into a new phase, its name in Marcellus SC at 48 pt, upper case, crosses the screen's centre left to right (fade in, slide 40 pt, fade out, 900 ms). Taps pass through it. It is skipped under Quick, under reduced motion, when the phase went backward (Undo), when the new state moves cards or changes a count (what opens the phase entered does not count: a shadow card dealt as Combat opens, beside the enemies; the draw, the resources gained and the Refresh phase's readying as Resource opens), while a combat spotlight plays, and when Stops passed the whole phase (a second phase change within 150 ms takes the first one's place).
- **Seat handoff** (two-handed play). When the acting seat changes, its dial pulses once in an accent ring (1 to 1.12 and back, `seatPulse`) and the table cuts to that seat at rest, as a peek does: no card moves or fades, so the two seats' cards are never drawn over each other, and nothing is dealt.
- **Peek.** A peek and its way back have no motion: the table is redrawn from the other seat at once, every card at rest. Only the seat's own zones change (the hand, the play area, the engaged enemies with their cards, the deck and discard pile, and, once stage 2B has split the table, the staging row, the quest card and the active location); before the split staging, the quest, the locations, the encounter piles, the second slot of piles and the Heading dial stay put. The canvas making room for the pill under a split table's peek snaps too. No hand rises, no dial pulses, no token pops, no card lands and no haptic plays (`kindMoves` in `motion/kind.ts`). A peek that ends because the player confirmed something on the dock is a new state, so its own motions play, diffed from the acting seat's layout; when the engine turns to the seat the table already shows, that hand does not slide up again.
- **Hurry.** A touch on the canvas finishes the running card motions at once (the pending landing tick is dropped, so no haptic follows a snapped card), skips a combat spotlight that is playing, and does nothing else (it picks, previews and drags nothing); any other touch is handled as usual. Token pops and floating numbers keep running. It never delays or swallows a tap, a drag or a long press outside a spotlight; it is the first touch of a hold on the hand too, so a scrub that starts mid-draw finds the hand at rest; and it touches neither the tray's outside-tap fold nor the More popover's outside-tap close. Nothing animates while the carousel scrolls, the rail does not animate when the table opens, and no motion moves the dock's pinned buttons.

## Spotlight

Plan: `docs/plans/done/combat-spotlight-redesign.md`.

Archery, damage from a card effect, a canceled attack and one that placed no damage step get none (the banner covers them). ## Gestures

Code: `src/play/canvas/hand-gestures.native.ts`; pure geometry in `hand-scrub.ts`.

A tap picks for the open moment, or, on a card the moment does not ask about, shows it large. A long press (350 ms) on a card on the table shows it large, with the tokens on it now (`card-preview-tokens`: "2 damage · 1 progress · 3 resources · Exhausted · Committed", accent `text-body`, joined by " · ", at most two lines): in the right column under the name and role on a wide screen, centred under the card upright, where the card gives up 52 pt for it; a discard pile's opens its list. A card that can be played, committed, traveled to or engaged can be dragged (a move of 6 pt starts it at once; nothing waits for a hold); the drop picks its target. No payment sheet opens for a cost the pools cannot meet, whatever asked for it (a drop, a tap, an attachment's host, an Action or Response's Use): when the pools that can pay, with what lowers the cost as it is paid (characters exhausted for it, a card that pays for it), hold less than the cost, the reject haptic plays and the dock shows the cost against the pools as a refusal; an attachment's host pick is refused so when no host can be paid for (`cannotPay` in `why.ts`; user instruction, 2026-11-01). No drag or drop sends an intent, and a tap sends one only in a moment with one answer (One answer, one tap). While the table peeks at the other seat, a tap on any card only opens its preview, nothing is picked or sent, and nothing can be dragged; a pile still opens its list, the viewed seat's. There is no swipe between seats: the seat is changed by its dial, so the drag-to-play path is never delayed.

## Hold, slide and release

On the hand. A press on a hand card, or on the hand strip between cards, held still for 150 ms (`SCRUB_HOLD_MS`; a move of 6 pt, `SCRUB_SLOP`, before that is a drag) starts a scrub, with one selection tick (`tickArmed`). These are gesture thresholds, outside the motion scale. The scrub starts on the card pressed (between cards, the nearest), and the nearest centre takes over from the first move. The hand rises to show whole cards (`scrubRise`), at 1.5 times the table's card whatever the hand's length (smaller only on a canvas narrower than one such card), and fans out like a held hand across the canvas width (`scrubSpread`), a gap apart when the cards fit and overlapping evenly when they do not; a long hand gets a thinner slice of each card, never smaller cards (the nearest centre still picks). The cards sit on an arc about three card heights in radius, each turned 0.07 rad from its neighbour, the fan at most 0.45 rad, so the middle card stands highest and the ends turn and drop, every turned corner on the canvas. The card nearest under the finger straightens and lifts above the others (`scrubLift`), 1.55 larger, grown from its foot, its foot a quarter of its height higher, so it is drawn above the finger. Its neighbours keep the spread's size. The card the finger left settles back over `scrubLift`. Every grown card stays inside the canvas less its 8 pt edge: an end card is moved in, its turned corners counted, and the lifted card grows no taller than the room above its foot (`spread.room.lift`) and rises less where the top edge would cut it. A selection tick plays each time the card under the finger changes, including coming back onto a card after sliding off an end. The canvas dims under night at 35%, a layer of its own; the top bar and the command column stay bright. Letting go on a card opens its preview (`CardPreview`). Pushing forward without letting go, more up than sideways, by 0.3 of the 44 pt reach (`SCRUB_PUSH`, 13 pt at Medium) from wherever the finger turned forward, on any part of the card and with no pause first, picks the lifted card up (user instruction, 2026-11-01): the hand settles back and the card becomes a drag, centred under the finger and following it, that picks only when dropped on one of its targets (the play area, or an attachment's host), exactly as a drag straight from the hand; dropped anywhere else it goes back and nothing is asked. A card that cannot be played is picked up too, with no targets; let go, it plays the reject haptic (`tickRefused`) and shakes where it was dropped (four `dragRefuse` swings of 6 pt) and goes home, saying the move is not legal (under reduced motion only the haptic plays). While the push is under way the pushed card stays lifted; a move sideways or back down starts the push again from there. Letting go more than 44 pt above the lifted card's top (`spread.ceiling`), or off the ends of the hand, cancels: the hand settles back (`scrubFall`) and no preview opens. A scrub sends no intent and picks nothing; Android Back cancels an open one. Under reduced motion the hand shows risen and spread at once, the lift is instant and it settles at once. The scrub runs on the hand of the seat peeked at too, as a preview; a peek that changes the shown seat while a scrub is open cancels the scrub first. On the hand the scrub replaces the 350 ms press. With a screen reader on there is no scrub: the hand cards stay reachable through the list path, and the 350 ms press previews them. The touch that skips a spotlight never starts a scrub. The finger is read on the UI thread (`hand-scrub.native.tsx`, no idle loop); only a change of the card under it crosses to JavaScript, for the tick, the release and the pick-up.

## Peeking at the other seat

Code: two-handed play; `src/play/store/peek.ts`, `use-peek.ts`, `peek-banner.tsx`.

A tap on the dial of the seat that is not shown shows that seat's whole area: hand, play area, engaged enemies, deck and discard pile (the Heading dial and the second slot of piles are shared, and stay), and, once Breaking of the Fellowship's stage 2B has split the table, its staging area, quest stage and active location (the canvas draws one staging row, the shown seat's; what the canvas, the web list and the pile's list draw is `shownSeat(game, viewedSeat)`; everything that picks, pays or sends reads `actingSeat(game)`). `viewedSeat` belongs to the table store only: not engine state and not in the save. It is null when the table opens and returns to null with every new state (a new sheet key or a change of the acting seat as well), when a sheet opens, on Android Back and on a tap on the acting seat's dial. Who acts never changes by a peek: the dock stays the acting seat's (its title already starts "Player 1 · …"). While it lasts a raised night pill (`peek-banner`) hangs 2 pt under the top bar, centred over the canvas, 36 pt tall with one line of words (at most 1.3 times the system font scale, so it never grows past the 42 pt the table keeps for it): "Viewing Player 2 · Player 1 is acting" ("Player 2 is out of the game" for an eliminated seat, its zones empty) and, at its right, an accent text button "Back to Player 1" (`peek-back`, 36 pt with 4 pt of slop above and below). Only the button takes a touch: the rest of the pill lets taps through to the cards under it. The table's banner moves under the pill while it shows. A peek at a split table draws a staging row the pill would cover, so the canvas leaves the pill its room: it starts the pill's height and gap lower while the peek lasts, at once and without motion. It fades in and out over `bannerIn` and `bannerOut` and snaps under reduced motion. The viewed hand has the 30% night veil (`peekVeil`), and nothing on the table glows or shows a pick. No spotlight plays during a peek, because the peek has ended by the time a moment arrives. A tap on a dial with the choice tray open folds the tray and also peeks or opens the panel (the tray's outside-tap layer over the bar stops where the dials end, `bar-edge.ts`). A tap that peeks or ends a peek closes the reading layer. True solo has one dial and no peek.

## Two taps

A button that sends an intent with nothing picked first (Pass, Keep, Mulligan, Leave undefended, Do not travel, Do not engage, No attack, Continue, Done, Commit no one) arms on the first tap: the armed line says what the second tap does ("Tap again: no one quests", "Tap again: planning ends") in the accent, with a 2 pt accent bar running down under it for three seconds (ten while a screen reader is on; the bar shows the timer on a linear curve, so Quick leaves it as long, and reduced motion leaves it still); the second tap sends it; the bar running out, another button, a new pick or a new moment puts it back. Arming changes no button's size or place, so the second tap lands where the first did. The same line shows what a disabled primary waits for. A primary that confirms a pick sends on one tap, because the pick was the first. Each armed button carries the hint "Tap twice to confirm", and a screen reader hears the armed line when it arms. A confirm that costs more than it shows arms the same way, though something is picked: a defender or a hero hit whose fall loses the game ("Tap again: Frodo Baggins falls and the game is lost"; the readout says why), and an attachment whose text the app does not apply ("Tap again: pay 2 for no effect").

## One answer, one tap

When the engine allows one intent and the sheet shows one answer for it (no character can defend, so Leave undefended is the answer, and with one hero it hits, the dock names that hero, says whether the hero is destroyed, and draws the hero beside the enemy; nothing to travel to; a window with only Pass; a Look's Done; a lone option of a choice; an archery total one character must take), the answer is picked already: its card shows the picked mark, the primary is ready and not armed, the line above it says "The only choice now: one tap sends it" in muted (a screen reader hears "Sends at once"), and one tap on the primary or on the card sends it (`onlyAnswer`, `src/play/choice/table-choice.ts`). A sheet opened from the table never counts: the window behind it has other answers. For 350 ms after a new moment appears no tap sends anything, so the second tap of a double tap meant for the moment before does not land on this one.

## Stops and Always use

With Stops on (the carousel's switch), the dock's spoken step starts with "Stops on ·", and an action window that is not one of the player's stops passes by itself unless a card usable there is new since the player was last asked. While stops are on, an action window offers the answer Pass to stop, armed like Pass, its line naming where the run ends at the latest ("Tap again: pass until before the quest resolves", or "until planning", which always asks); a choice on the way ends it sooner. A Response with no cost and nothing to choose offers Always use, armed ("Tap again: use it now and every time"); the table settings list those Responses, and a tap there asks again. The answers that follow a send go in the same tap, so a window the table passes is never drawn: in two-handed play the table does not flash to the other seat and back (`followAnswers`, `stops.ts`). Undo takes back an answer the table gave, and the table then asks at that window.

## Guide

While the first-game guide is on (the default), the dock's folded line is "Guide: …" in muted, the first two times each step's own decision comes up (the step's decision from the turn reference, or what an action window is for). It is not said in a Response or a pick inside a step, nor when the note already says it, and it is the one line the dock shows (the note waits behind the "i" pill), so on a short phone it never pushes the buttons away. It is turned off in the settings.

## Choice tray

Code: `src/play/choice/tray.tsx`; its tiles and the reading panels' frame in `tray-logic.ts`.

It opens for a choice whose options are not all cards drawn whole on the table (an either/or of an effect, a seat, a card out of play, an attachment or a shadow card, which only peek out), and for any window where a Response can be used even when its cards are on the table (`listsResponses`: its cards still glow and a tap on one picks it with the tray open; the count, spoken with the title, leads with the timing, "Response · pick 1 of 2").

- **Place**: the options are rows in the command column, in the dock's own space between its words and its readout, armed line and buttons, which they never cover. They have no panel, no corners and no shadow: words on the column's fade, so they never reach into the canvas or cover an encounter pile (`tray-logic.test.ts` checks the column's box at 844 × 390 and 667 × 375 with insets 0 and 47). The dock grows upward as far as the top bar; the rows scroll inside themselves past the room the title and the buttons leave, and always keep one row with its picture in view (`trayRoom`: 84 pt, 59 pt with the phone's picture).
- **Content**: the options and nothing else. The dock's title asks the question ("Choose a defender"); there is no count over the rows. One row per option, with no plate: the card's picture when the option is a card (51 × 71, the table's card, on a tablet; 34 × 47 on a phone, by its real or its logical height under 400 pt: `smallPictures`, `dock/budget.ts`), with the accent ring it wears on the table while it is picked, the name, the deciding stat from the sheet's `stats`, and the foreseen-damage pill (the damage icon and number on a bone wash; threat red when the card would be destroyed). Under 400 pt of screen height a row is dense: the name, the stats and the pill on one line when they fit, at least 44 pt tall, 48 pt apart. A picked row is washed in the accent (16%, 12 pt corners), its name in accentHigh, with a check. What the rules refuse now is a line of text on no plate, the name in muted and the reason in faint, and not a choice. A row's tap is the same pick as the card's tap on the table (`table-choice.ts`); a long press on a card's row shows it large. `sheet-option-<id>`, `sheet-trigger-<id>` and `sheet-unavailable-<id>` stay on its rows. A choice that may stop early (the Command events: "choose up to X options") keeps its stop off the rows: Done is the sheet's armed pass, and its armed line says what ends ("Tap again: Command goes to the victory display with 2 options used"); the note counts the options left ("Choose up to 2 options, one at a time." then "Choose up to 1 more option.", then "Done ends the choice.").
- **Motion and folding**: it enters in `trayEnter` (16 pt up and a fade) and leaves in `trayLeave`; both snap under reduced motion. There is no scrim: a tap anywhere outside folds it (on the canvas, on the top bar, its dials and rail included but not the menu button, and on the empty part of the command column above the dock's content), and that tap is swallowed: it picks nothing, sends no intent, commits no drop, and opens neither the carousel nor a threat panel. The dock's Choose brings the tray back for the same moment; a new moment opens its tray unfolded. List on the dock opens a choice made on the table as a tray too (the quest commit's characters, the defenders). The tray is the default while a screen reader is on and then does not fold on an outside tap, so every choice is a control a screen reader and Maestro can reach; react-native-web always reports a screen reader, so the web build lists every choice. At the foot of a reading panel opened mid-choice, a muted "Choice waiting" says a choice is still open.

## Reading panels

Code: `src/play/chrome/reading-panels.tsx`, `tray-frame.tsx`.

A discard pile's list, the victory display's, a row's list, the staging threat list (the quest meter), the Log, the threat panel (a dial), the Island Map (its chip) and the table settings open in one place, on one frame: the command column's width (at least 300 pt), 20 pt corners and the `trayEnter` and `trayLeave` motion, anchored to the right edge and the screen's foot and as tall as the room under the top bar, over the command column (the Island Map's panel is wider, The Island Map). The card lists (a discard pile's, the victory display's, a row's, the staging threat's) and the table settings are night panels (`floatingSurface`, opaque); the Log, the threat panel and the Island Map are parchment (what is read and recorded); all have 20 pt corners; each has a round ✕ glass button labelled "Close" at its top right (on paper for the parchment panels) and a scrim from the top bar's foot down that closes it on a tap outside and does nothing more. The top bar stays live, so a dial or the rail replaces the panel. One reading layer at a time, the carousel included (`reading.ts`): a new one replaces the last. When the choice tray is open too, the panel sits over it and closing the panel shows the tray again.

- **Card lists** (`PileList`, `RowList`, `reading-panels.tsx`; user request, 2026-11-03): the count in faint small caps over the title in Marcellus SC, centred, then one row per card with no plate: its picture at the size the choice's rows use (51 × 71 on a tablet, 34 × 47 on a phone, times the UI scale; a card's corners and drop shadow; a raised plate for a facedown card or a missing file), its name in Marcellus SC in bone and, in faint under it, its type and sphere ("Event · Tactics", `words/list.ts`); the victory display's line adds the Victory points the card scores ("Enemy · 4 VP", heard as "Enemy, 4 victory points"), a folded location's line says what is grappled under it. A tap shows the card large; the pressed row takes the pressed fill. A discard pile lists the newest card first; a row's list, left to right.
- **Threat panel**: the title "Threat" over the seat's "20 of 50" (an overline names the seat in two-handed play, "Player 2"), how the engagement checks read it, and each staging enemy's name with its engagement cost and what the checks would do with it now, on rows with no border: Engages in ink; Engages Player 2, Stays or Cannot be engaged in inkMuted.
- **Discard pile**: a card the open window lets the seat play from its own discard pile (Elven-light, 10145:A:1) is not drawn on the table: the pile glows as a tappable card does, the window's note says "Tap your discard pile to play Elven-light from it.", and in the pile's list that card stands first, its picture ringed and glowing in the accent, its row washed in the accent with its name in accentHigh and "Playable now · tap to play" (its hint says "Tap to play it"): a tap closes the list and opens its play as a tap on a hand card does (a long press shows it large).
- **Row list** (`row-list`, `row-list-close`, rows `row-card-<id>`): the cards a row folded into its fold stack, left to right, titled "Staging" or "Engagement area" over "3 more cards". A row shows the card large and sends nothing.
- **Staging threat list** (`ThreatSourceList`, `threat-sources`, `threat-sources-close`, rows `threat-source-<id>`): a tap on the quest meter lists the cards that make up its threat, from the engine's own breakdown (`stagingThreatSources`) for the meter's seat, so it adds up to the meter's number: "7 Threat" over "Staging threat" ("Player 2's staging threat" once stages are separate), then a card row per card, the most Threat first, its line saying the Threat it adds ("Enemy · 3 Threat"; an engaged enemy or the active location that contributes says so, "Enemy · Engaged · 2 Threat"). A card that does not contribute its Threat is not listed. When held effects raise or lower the total, a muted line under the rows says by how much ("Other effects: −2 Threat"). A row shows the card large and sends nothing.
- **Log**: titled "Log" (the round is in its spoken name, "Log, round 3"); this round's engine steps, each with its cause in inkMuted under it (the rule title, or the card and its printed line), with the threat, willpower and attack icons and the token art as marks. Removed progress reads "2 progress removed from Into the Wild", never a minus sign. It is text, not a control. Its lines keep their ruled lines.
- **Table settings** (`SettingsPanel`): the Settings screen's rows on the night panel: the title "Table settings" in Marcellus SC at the left (the one reading panel whose title is not centred), then `SettingsRows` (`setting-rows.tsx`, the same code as the screen) in a scroll: Display, Motion and feel, At the table, as on Settings, without Stops on (the carousel carries the Stops). Theme and UI scale apply at once, so the panel reflows at the new scale or colours. At its foot, All settings (accent text, `settings-all`) closes the panel and opens Settings with `from=table`, which leaves the table as Rules does (the game is saved); with that param Settings shows Back ("Back to the table", `app-settings-back`, `router.back()`). The round ✕ at its top right is `settings-close`. The panel is 40% of the canvas, kept between 360 and 460 pt at the UI scale and within the room the screen leaves (`settingsPanelWidth`, `settings-panel-width.ts`), so three words fit at Large. While it is open "Choice waiting" takes muted. testIDs are `settings-*` (`settings-speed-quick`, `settings-scale-large`, `settings-theme-light`, `settings-quality-low`, `settings-fog`, `settings-spotlight`, `settings-contrast`, `settings-haptics`, `settings-guide`, `settings-always-<effect>`).

## Phase carousel

Code: `src/play/phases/phase-carousel.tsx`, columns in `carousel-columns.tsx`, data `phaseCarousel` in `src/play/phases/carousel.ts`, every word from the turn reference through `data/rules`.

A tap on the rail opens one panel of the round. It is for reading, so it is a lit night panel (`floatingSurface`, opaque, 20 pt corners, a deep shadow), not paper, floating 8 pt inside the canvas and 4 pt under the top bar, over the canvas only, so the dock stays usable and the panel and the choice never cover each other. A tap outside closes it (`carousel-scrim`) and does nothing more; the rail again or Android Back closes it too; it sends no intent. It drops in `carouselEnter` (12 pt down with a fade) and leaves in `carouselLeave`; it snaps under reduced motion, and nothing animates while it scrolls. It is the room under the bar less 8 pt (or the foot's safe inset, if larger), at most 340 pt (`carouselHeight`, `carousel-size.ts`).

- **Phases** (left column, 132 to 168 pt): "Round 1" (or "Before the round") in a muted Marcellus SC label on one line at the top, 20 pt in from the panel's edge so it clears the corner, then the round's seven phases in `ROUND` order (Resource, Planning, Quest, Travel, Encounter, Combat, Refresh) as 38 pt rows with a 3 pt hit slop each side for the 44 pt target (`carousel-phase-<id>`; the label and all seven rows fit the panel at 667 × 375 and 844 × 390 with no scrolling, `carousel-size.test.ts`), each a dot and the name in Marcellus SC caps: the dot is accent now, accentDeep when done, an outline to come; the name accent when current, bone to come, muted past. - **Steps** (right column) of the phase being read: the master Stops switch (`carousel-stops`, the word Stops with the setting's sentence as its hint) at the top right, with the accent S and "A stop is ahead" (`carousel-stop-ahead`) beside it while a stop is ahead.
- Each step (`carousel-step-<id>`) stands on a plain dot timeline: a dot on a line to the next step (accent with a soft ring now, accentDeep when done, an outline to come), its rule titles in bone label (accentHigh now, muted when done), the player's decision in muted, and the step's action windows as Stop switches (`carousel-stop-<id>`), each a whole row at least 44 pt tall. ## Quest advance

Code: `quest-advance.ts`, `use-quest-advance.ts`, `quest-advance-view.tsx`.

When an intent the player sent advances the quest, and when the table opens on a game that is not over (so a new game shows its first quest card), a screen like the card preview opens over the table, after any spotlight: the stage it reached, large on the left on its B side (`picture-key.ts`; side A where the build has none), and a column at its right, one stack: "Stage 2 of 3" as an accent overline ("Player 2 · Stage 3 of 4" for a split table's seat in two-handed play, the stage read as that seat holds it), the stage's name in the title face ("A Fork in the Road"), and a muted line with its quest points, the printed line that wins it, or the rule that holds it back in its own sentence, with no lead; when one intent advanced two seats (stage 2B) a muted line each tells the others ("Player 1 advanced to Searching the Woods"). No Details button; a tap on the dimmed screen does nothing. Under the words, one primary (`quest-advance-progress`) goes back to the table: Begin on the opening of a new game (round 1, nothing played yet), Continue on the opening of a resumed one (a later round, or a save that already had intents: `resumed` in the table store) and on a stage held back, Progress on a stage that can progress (`advanceButton`); Android Back does the same. When the stage's own When Revealed asks the player something (41004:B:1, 41005:A:1), the question stands there in place of Progress: the dock's step and title, its options as tiles (`quest-advance-option-<id>`) and the dock's buttons with their two taps (`DockButtons`); a long press on an option shows that card in the card preview, which stands over this screen, and a muted line under the options says so ("Hold a card to read it."); once answered the screen goes back to the table by itself. While it is up the table answers nothing for the player (Stops, Always use); it answers the moment once the screen closes. The engine has advanced already: the screen holds the moment so the new stage is read before the round goes on.

## Undo

Undo is the first of the dock's quiet pills. It takes back the last choice while it showed nothing hidden (`docs/brief.md`, The engine plays the game); each tap goes back one more, and it leaves the dock once a card has been revealed since. While a pick is being made, its first tap puts back the pick only ("Put back this pick"); while a sheet opened from the table is up, Cancel is there instead. The cards move back the way they came. The banner says "Taken back", the choice in words ("Frodo Baggins committed to the quest") and what it had done.

## Haptics

A tick when a button arms, a confirm when an intent is sent or taken back, a reject when the reducer refuses one, a play the heroes cannot pay for is tried, or a card that cannot be played is let go out of the hand scrub, a light tick when a card played from the hand lands (`tickLand`, at most one per new state), a medium impact at a spotlight's hit stop (`tickImpact`, at most one per strike), and the scrub's selection tick (`tickArmed`; no scrub runs during a spotlight). The confirm plays the moment the reducer accepts the intent, before the table redraws and the save is written (a refused intent gets only the reject); the landing tick plays as the card's motion clock passes the landing, so a touch that snaps the motion plays none. On iOS the selection, light and medium impact and warning feedback; on Android the system's Segment tick, Confirm, Reject and Long press constants (`performAndroidHapticsAsync`), which follow the phone's touch-feedback setting, never the vibrator. They add to the screen and never replace it. The **Haptics** setting (`haptics`, on by default) turns all of them off.

## Banner

Code: `moment-banner.tsx`, `banner-span.ts`.

After each intent that changed the table, a panel centred on the screen at its top edge, 4 pt under the safe area, over the top bar, the table and any open panel, never over the command column and its tray, says what happened. It has 20 pt corners and no outline. On Dark it is night at 90% with no lit edge; the combat banner, for a failure or an attack, is a dark red under a 3 pt threat-red band along its top, the only line it draws. Light keeps its pale `raisedTop` at 90%. The corners are a style on the filled view (a class on a reanimated view does not apply). The title in Marcellus SC and up to five centred lines with their marks, 260 to 440 pt wide, narrower when the screen's middle leaves less room, never wider than the screen less 16 pt a side: "Quest failed · Willpower 3 against threat 5 · Threat +2", "Black Rider attacks · Attack 3 against Sam Gamgee's defense 2 · Shadow, Hasty Departure: … · 1 damage on Sam Gamgee", "Traveled to Bag End".

- A tap closes it; a swipe sideways (56 pt) or up (34 pt), or a flick, slides it off and closes it (a shorter drag springs back; its round summary, when one follows, then shows); a screen reader's escape gesture or its Dismiss action closes it too. It is announced on both platforms (`announceForAccessibility`). A new moment enters afresh even while the last is up.
- The banner and each line fade in (`bannerIn`) and the banner fades out (`bannerOut`). Its lines resolve in beats: the first with the title, then one more every 650 ms (a timer, not a token), each ringing the cards it names on the table (reduced motion shows every line at once and rings none). It closes 4.5 seconds after its last line shows; the Log keeps every step.
- The moment that ends a round (not setup) is followed by the round's summary in the same banner, "Round 3 ends": the threat before and after when it moved, progress placed, damage taken and dealt, encounter cards defeated or explored, cards drawn. When an attack lost its defender before it struck, a line says "With Sam Gamgee gone, the attack is undefended".
- When the moment resolved an attack that plays as a spotlight, the banner enters, and its beats and hold begin, when the spotlight ends or is skipped. Where there is no spotlight it shows at once.

## Android Back

First skips a playing spotlight, then cancels an open scrub, then ends a peek, then closes the table menu or the dock's More or "i" popover, then the preview, then the reading layer (the carousel, a panel, the log, a pile's list), then puts back a sheet opened from the table, then folds the choice tray, and only then leaves the table.
